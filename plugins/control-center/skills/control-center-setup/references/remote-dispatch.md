# Step 5 — The phone test

This is the moment the whole thing becomes real to them. Never skip it, never rush it.

## Script

> *"Last big one. Grab your phone."*
> *"Open [app] and sign in with the same account — same as this computer."*
> *"Now type exactly this: **what's on my calendar today?***"

When the answer lands on their phone:

> *"That was your computer, doing what you asked, from your phone. That's the whole idea. ✅"*

## Which path to set up

**Phone → cloud (easiest, works even with the laptop shut).**
Claude Code on the web (claude.ai/code) or Codex cloud tasks. Great for research, writing, documents, pages, code. Set this up by default — it's the one that just works.

**Phone → this computer (needed when the job touches their own machine, their local files, or a browser they're signed into).**
Requires the computer stays on and awake (that's why Step 2 asked). The simplest reliable pattern is an **inbox**:
- They send a job from anywhere — an email to themselves with a set label, a note in a shared folder, a task in their tracker.
- A routine on the computer checks it every few minutes and does the job, then replies where they'll see it.

Explain it to them in one line only: *"Send yourself an email with the word DO in the subject and your computer will pick it up within 5 minutes."*

## Safety, said plainly, once
> *"One thing: anything that gets sent, published, or paid, it'll always ask you first — even from your phone."*

And keep the kill switch true: **"pause my control center"** stops every routine.
