# Event Launcher: best practices for live launch events (research notes, 2026-10-17)

Scope: small service businesses, coaches and consultants with lists of roughly 300–5,000 and often no ads budget, planning, filling, running, closing and following up a live launch event (3- or 5-day challenge or bootcamp, live workshop, webinar, product launch).

**Method and reliability warning.** Page fetching was blocked in this session. WebFetch failed DNS resolution for every domain, and curl got a 403 from the egress proxy. Every claim below therefore comes from **search-engine result snippets and summaries**, not from reading the pages. Numbers are quoted as the snippets gave them. Treat exact wording as "verify before quoting publicly". "Date n/s" means the snippet did not show a publication date.

Tags: **[platform/vendor data]** = a vendor's own platform or customer data. **[independent data: who, sample]** = third-party research. **[practitioner: who]** = an operator's self-reported numbers or experience. **[folklore]** = a widely repeated claim with no traceable data.

## What matters most (10 lines)

1. **Show-up is the biggest lever and the most measured number.** Webinar platforms report 33–60% of registrants attending, depending on definition. Creator funnels fed by ads run lower: about 25% live and 38% evergreen (Scale For Impact). A warm small list should plan on 30–50% live.
2. **Price and the room set conversion.** Agency data from 114 webinars: 8–15% of attendees buy at $97–497, 5–10% at $500–1,500, 1–4% at $2,000+. Above about $2k, send people to an application or call instead of checkout.
3. **Charging anything raises attendance, but less than the folklore says.** Free in-person events have a median 28% no-show versus 17% for paid (Pheedloop, 1,070+ events). Within the same event, payers show up only 2–4 points more reliably.
4. **Reminders work, with a modest independent effect.** A Cochrane review of SMS reminders found a 1.14x attendance ratio, and two reminders beat one. Vendor claims of SMS "doubling" attendance are not supported independently.
5. **Small lists can launch.** Practitioners report 1–2% of a list buying as normal and about 5% as strong. A 474-person list produced 25 sales; a waitlist converted at 34.9%. Stu McLaren is quoted as saying about 200 people is enough for a founding-member launch.
6. **Sales cluster at the deadline.** Denise Duffield-Thomas: 40% of sales landed in the final 2 days. Affiliate data from 2016: 40–60% on the last day. Plan email volume and live touchpoints for the close, and keep the deadline real.
7. **Live beats recorded for conversion; recorded beats live for effort and volume.** Porterfield: about 9% live versus 5–6% evergreen. Heart, Soul & Hustle: a non-live launch made about $7k against $48–52k for its live challenges.
8. **Paid mini-events ($25–$100 workshops, bootcamps, challenges) are a strong 2024–26 pattern for small lists.** HSH turned 17.7–19.2% of paid-challenge participants into members, and Brunson sells a $100 three-day live workshop. Most hard numbers for this format are still practitioner self-reports.
9. **AI changes the trust maths.** Gartner (September 2026): 65% of consumers say brands use too much AI content and 57% trust brand messaging less. Klaviyo: visible AI marketing lowers trust for 31% and raises it for 7%. So use AI behind the scenes and keep the human visibly live.
10. **Scarcity must be real.** The FTC treats fake countdown timers as dark patterns (2022 staff report). The UK DMCC Act (in force 6 April 2025) lets the CMA fine directly for false urgency. The FTC keeps suing coaching programmes over earnings claims.

## 1. Formats compared

**Small-list context.** The average Kajabi creator is reported to have an email list of about 4,000, 1,000–10,000 followers and about 300 paying customers (Sacra analysis, 2025) [independent data: Sacra, method n/s]. That is this skill's audience. No Kajabi, Teachable or Kit launch-conversion study turned up (Gaps).

**When each fits (derived):**
- List <1,000, no ads: paid workshop or bootcamp ($27–$97), a repeated live webinar, or a founding-member launch.
- 1,000–5,000 warm list: a 5-day challenge (free or paid) or a 3-day bootcamp, plus a 5–7 day cart.
- High ticket ($2k+): webinar or workshop into an application or call.
- Recurring/community offer: a paid challenge into membership (HSH model).
- Low host energy: a pre-recorded "non-live" launch, with roughly a third of the revenue.

## 2. Benchmarks (ranges, who measured)

### Registration page conversion
- Personalised landing pages 25%, content hubs 30% (ON24 2025 report on 2024 data, via MarketingProfs summary, 2025) [platform/vendor: ON24, B2B].
- Target about 40% landing-page conversion (Scale For Impact benchmark, 114 webinars, $100M+ revenue, 2026) [practitioner/agency: Scale For Impact, course creators].
- 15–35% "depending on audience warmth"; 35–50% registration opt-in (vendor playbooks, 2026) [platform/vendor, weak].
- **Planning range:** warm list traffic 35–50%, cold or social traffic 15–30%.

### Show-up (registrant → live attendee)
- ON24: 57% (2024 data) and 60% (2025 data); counts live plus on-demand together; 239 average attendees (2026 report, via secondary) [platform/vendor: ON24, B2B, millions of interactions].
- Livestorm: 51.3% average show-up for 2025 events (48.9% in 2024); Tuesday 51.7%, Monday 51.5%; 33,786 sessions, 4.35M registrations; 15.3% register on the day and 49.6% in the final week (Livestorm 2026 Benchmark Report) [platform/vendor: Livestorm]. Third-party summaries also quote 47.7% live for the same report, so the definitions differ.
- Univid: 49% live, 57% live + replay; 2,000+ webinars, Jan 2023–Aug 2025, 70% European, medians (Univid Webinar Insights 2025) [platform/vendor: Univid].
- Contrast: about 62% watch live or on demand; median live attendance about 41.6% (500k+ registrants, 2025) [platform/vendor: Contrast].
- Goldcast: 33% average attendance in 2024, up from 29% (B2B, 2025 report) [platform/vendor: Goldcast].
- **Creator and course funnels:** about 25% live and 38% evergreen (Scale For Impact, 2026) [practitioner/agency]. Live 25–35% versus just-in-time evergreen 60–70% (EasyWebinar, date n/s) [platform/vendor].
- **Free vs paid:** median no-show 28% free versus 17% paid; for small events (10–49 people) 37% versus 17%; 1,070+ live in-person events (Pheedloop Event Data Lab #05, 29 Apr 2026) [independent-ish: Pheedloop platform data]. Report #06: within one event, payers show up only 2–4 points more reliably than comped attendees. Most of the gap comes from whether an event charges at all.
- Paid versus free challenge completion: free 3–8%, $27–$97 paid 35–55%, high-end 70–80% (CommuniPass, 2026) [platform/vendor, unverified, sells paid-challenge software]. **Do not use as a benchmark.**
- **Planning range for this skill:** free webinar to a warm small list, 30–45% live. Free challenge Day 1 live, 15–35% of registrants. Paid $27–$97 event, 50–70% Day 1. Treat all three as **derived assumptions**, not measured norms.

### Effect of reminders
- SMS reminders, healthcare appointments: risk ratio 1.14 (about 78.6% vs 67.8% attendance); SMS about equal to a phone call (Cochrane review 2013, 7 studies, n=5,841). Two reminders beat one: 4.4% vs 5.3% and 5.8% missed (2018 three-arm trial, n=54,066) [independent data: Cochrane / RCT; healthcare, not webinars; via secondary summary].
- Webinar SMS: 11% → 68% (Mobile Text Alerts) [platform/vendor]; about 25% → 50% after texting links shortly before start (Growth Tools) [practitioner: Growth Tools]; about +80% from day-before and day-of SMS (Act-On) [platform/vendor]; 15% → 30% (Scale For Impact) [practitioner/agency]. Dates n/s.
- Email sequence: 3 reminders lift show-up from about 35–40% to 55–65% (WebinarNinja) [platform/vendor]. Confirmation + 1 day + 1 hour + "live now" gives 50–55% versus 35–40% with one reminder (vendor blog) [platform/vendor]. Reminder opens run 45–65%; the 1-hour and "we're live" emails often exceed 60% (Sequenzy, 2026) [platform/vendor].
- Calendar: add-to-calendar "cuts no-shows by up to 30%" (CalendarLink) [platform/vendor]; no independent study found.
- **Derived:** stack calendar + email + SMS. Expect relative lifts of roughly 10–30% from proper sequencing, not doubling.

### Day-over-day attendance decay (challenges)
- The only measured figure found: 51.8% retained from Day 1 to Day 5 (144/278) in a paid bootcamp [practitioner: HSH, about early 2025].
- Free FB-group challenge: 1,500–1,900 live daily from 16,339 registrants (fairly flat by day, but about 10% of registrants) [practitioner: Denise DT].
- Scale For Impact models a 30% daily show-up as an assumption, not a measurement [practitioner/agency].
- **Gap:** no multi-challenge dataset on daily decay was found.

### VIP upgrade take rate
- 3.84% for a $47 VIP with a 30-day trial, offered at the end of Day 1 and again on Day 3, in Brunson's Think and Grow Rich challenge (community notes from a FHL "Funnel Debriefs" session, Skool, date n/s) [practitioner: Russell Brunson, secondhand].
- Modelled at 10–15% (Scale For Impact assumption) [practitioner/agency].
- One case: the 10% who bought a $27 VIP produced 45% of $10k certification sales (Scale For Impact guide) [practitioner/agency, single case].
- **Planning range:** about 4–15% of registrants. VIP buyers are disproportionately likely to buy the core offer.

### Sales conversion: attendees vs registrants vs list
- By price, of webinar attendees: $97–497 8–15%; $500–1,500 5–10%; $2,000+ 1–4%; overall 3–10% (Scale For Impact, 114 webinars, $100M+, 2026) [practitioner/agency].
- Live 10% vs simulive 8% vs JIT evergreen 6% attendee-to-buyer; live "peaks" at 15–30% (EasyWebinar) [platform/vendor].
- Porterfield: live about 9% vs evergreen 5–6%; webinars 10–12%, up to 20% [practitioner: Amy Porterfield].
- Of total leads: 3.7% (952 sales / 25,682 opt-ins) [practitioner: Denise DT, $1M launch debrief part 1]. 2020 launch: about 5% topline (407/8,038), about 3.5% adjusted [practitioner: Denise DT]. General expectation "1–2% of people will buy" (her $750k lessons) [practitioner: Denise DT].
- Small list: 474 list → 25 sales (about 5%); waitlist converted 34.9%; sales page 7.05%; "average list converts 1–2%" [practitioner: Nicola Moors, about 2025].
- Paid challenge → membership: 17.66% and 19.2% of participants [practitioner: HSH].
- Webinar → call: 10–20% of attendees book a call; call close 10–25% (vendor and agency blogs) [platform/vendor, weak].
- Agencies: attendee-to-customer 1.2% (Focus Digital, Dec 2023) [unverified method].
- **Planning model:** buyers ≈ 1–5% of list, 3–15% of live attendees (price-dependent) and 15–20% of paid-event participants.

### Cart-close spike
- 40% of sales (340) in the final 2 days [practitioner: Denise DT, launch debrief part 2].
- 40–60% of affiliate sales on the last day, sometimes 70%+ [practitioner: Matt McWilliams, 2016].
- "Typically 50% of sales happen on the last day" (secondary PLF summaries) [folklore unless measured; directionally consistent].
- **Planning range:** 35–60% of revenue in the last 24–48 hours.

### Replay views
- On-demand is about half of ON24 attendees (2024 data; 2026 report summary says about 50–52%) [platform/vendor: ON24].
- 42% of views come from replay (Contrast) [platform/vendor].
- Univid: 49% live and 57% total, so replay adds about 8 points of registrants [platform/vendor].
- On-demand watch time drops about 42%, to 33 minutes (AdVids) [platform/vendor, weak].
- Promoting the replay to non-attendees adds about 15% more viewers (Demio, attributed to John Ainsworth) [practitioner].
- Send the replay within 1–3 hours; opens fall about 40% after 3 hours (WebinarNinja) [platform/vendor].

### Refund rates
- **No reliable benchmark found.** CommuniPass claims 2–6% historically and 21% platform-wide in Q1 2026 (28% for $497–1,997), with no method and a sales motive [platform/vendor, unverified]. One course seller claims under 2% (Simple Seven) [practitioner]. A coach case shows 22% refunds on a launch versus 3.2% on a challenge format (vendor article) [platform/vendor].
- Card-network limits matter more for small sellers. Visa VAMP replaced VDMP/VFMP on 1 Jun 2025, and the US "excessive" ratio fell from 2.2% to 1.5% on 1 Apr 2026. Mastercard ECM triggers at 100 chargebacks and 1.5% (Chargeflow and others, 2025–26) [platform/vendor summaries of network rules].
- Launch unsubscribes: about 10% overall in a quiz-driven launch, against about 0.5% per normal newsletter; an earlier launch saw about 2.7% [practitioner: Denise DT].

## 3. Show-up engineering

- **Calendar invites (.ics / add-to-calendar on the thank-you page, plus Google/Outlook links):** cheap commitment device. Vendor claims up to 30% fewer no-shows; no independent figure. Put it on the confirmation page and in every reminder [platform/vendor: CalendarLink; customerthink checklist].
- **SMS:** independent evidence shows a modest lift (RR 1.14) and that two reminders beat one. Practitioners report big gains from texting the link at start time. Send a "we're live" email and SMS at start (Scale For Impact) [practitioner/agency].
- **Timing of registration:** about half of registrations arrive in the final week and 15% on the day (Livestorm 2025 data). Keep promotion running through the day of the event, and leave registration open through Day 1–2 of a challenge [platform/vendor].
- **Homework / indoctrination:** prime Day 1 hard. Scale For Impact calls the biggest leak "people who registered, got busy, forgot, and never showed", not the Day 5 pitch [practitioner/agency]. Porterfield wants attendees to get 2–3 pieces of free value before the pitch [practitioner].
- **Community (2026):**
  - Facebook groups still work at scale (Denise DT ran 16,339 people through a pop-up group), but only about 19% of registrants joined the group in the Crazy Eye case [practitioner].
  - The case for Skool (no feed, leaderboards) comes from Skool-affiliated sellers; there is no independent engagement data [platform/vendor].
  - WhatsApp: a vendor case reports 75% completion and 30% buying [platform/vendor, unverified]. The UN-Habitat 5×5 WhatsApp challenge (Nov 2024) recommended a leaderboard outside the chat [independent: UN-Habitat programme evaluation, small].
  - **Derived:** for 300–5,000 lists, the community is optional. Email + SMS + Zoom chat may beat a group nobody joins. If you use one, choose where the audience already is.
- **Gamification and prizes:** a webinar with a prize raised sales on that webinar by 30%; cash prizes were used in her challenges [practitioner: Denise DT, 2020 launch post]. Claims of "30x engagement" from gamification have no support (Xperiencify listings) [folklore].
- **Start time and length:**
  - Livestorm: Tuesday, Monday and Wednesday are near-identical; 11am and 2pm ET recommended.
  - Univid (mostly European): Wednesday 51%, and 3pm beat 11am (58.3% vs 56.9%).
  - Goldcast (B2B): Monday had the highest attendance.
  - Average engagement is about 49–51 minutes (ON24).
  - Automated webinars should pitch before about 42 minutes (EasyWebinar) [platform/vendor].
  - Challenge sessions of 30–45 minutes a day (practitioner guides).
  - **Derived:** day and time matter less than reminders. Pick a time the audience can actually make and test it.
- **Live vs pre-recorded vs "live-ish":**
  - Live converts better: Porterfield 9% vs 5–6%; EasyWebinar 10% vs 8% simulive vs 6% JIT.
  - The perceived presence of the coach drives completion; pre-recorded challenges see about 20–30% (CommuniPass, vendor).
  - Simulive or JIT gets higher show-up.
  - **Do not label recorded sessions as "live"**: a practitioner compliance warning (2024 podcast) and the FTC deception standard [practitioner].
  - **Derived "live-ish" pattern:** pre-record the teaching, then go live for the Q&A and the pitch.

## 4. The pitch and close

- **When to pitch:**
  - Webinar: after belief-shifting content, about 10 min stack and 5 min close [practitioner: Brunson via secondary]. Moving key content earlier in a webinar lifted conversions by about 2 points [practitioner: Porterfield].
  - Challenge: the VIP is offered at the end of Day 1 and again on Day 3 (Brunson TGR notes). The offer reveal comes about 60% of the way through, not at the end (Scale For Impact). HSH uses "gradual exposure" that grows each day [practitioners].
- **Offer stacks:** stack items, then reveal a price well below the stacked value (Perfect Webinar summaries) [practitioner]. **Derived:** for small, sceptical audiences, keep stack values honest. Gartner and Klaviyo data show trust erosion.
- **Payment plans:** a payment option lifts conversions about 17% on average (SamCart database) [platform/vendor]. Learnybox suggests a 10–20% premium on instalment totals; defaults can reach 15–20% without dunning [platform/vendor, unsourced]. Denise DT credits a new, longer payment plan for part of her final-days surge [practitioner].
- **Fast-action bonuses:** widely used. **No measured lift found** (Gap).
- **Application vs checkout:** direct checkout at $2,000+ converts 1–4% (Scale For Impact). Some vendors report 5–20% in a tight room. Use applications or calls above about $2k [practitioner/agency]. Webinar → booked call 10–20% and close 10–25% [vendor, weak]. **Derived:** for service businesses, the "sale" is often the booked call, so the skill needs a calls track.
- **Sales calls after events:** no independent data; the vendor ranges above are the only figures.
- **"Double close":** the term was **not found** in Fladlien or Brunson sources (searched). Practitioners use it loosely for (a) pitching at the end of the live event and again at cart close, or (b) pitching, Q&A, then re-pitching. Treat it as **[folklore/terminology]** and define it in the skill as "close live, then close again at the deadline with a final live Q&A".
- **Cart open length:** PLF uses 5–7 days [practitioner: Walker via secondary]. Short 24–72h carts produce a sharper spike but less total time to decide. No comparative data was found. **Derived:** 3–5 days for small lists with one mid-cart live Q&A and a final-day live session; 5–7 days if affiliates or partners are involved.
- **Honest scarcity and deadline tools:**
  - Deadline Funnel-style per-person deadlines are well liked in reviews (G2/Capterra) [practitioner reviews]. The "+30% conversion" figure is a competitor citing HubSpot, generic [platform/vendor].
  - **Legal:** the FTC 2022 staff report "Bringing Dark Patterns to Light" lists fake countdown timers and false scarcity [independent: FTC staff report, 15 Sep 2022].
  - UK DMCC Act in force 6 Apr 2025; the CMA can fine up to 10% of global turnover. Its first investigations (18 Nov 2025) cover urgency and drip pricing. Earlier action against Emma Sleep targeted timers that reset on refresh [independent: law-firm summaries of CMA actions].
  - FTC earnings-claims cases against coaching: Publishing.com $1.5M (2025); Ganadores/Shemin ban and $20.3M judgment (largely suspended); Automators AI ban [independent: FTC/press].
  - **Derived:** any deadline must actually close the offer, and a timer must not reset.

## 5. Post-event

- **Replay strategy:** send within 1–3 hours [vendor]. A replay window of 24–72 hours is the practitioner norm (no data found). A "last chance" email drives half or more of replay purchases (SegMetrics playbook) [platform/vendor].
- **No-show sequences:** 55–65% of registrants do not attend live [vendor]. Promoting the replay to non-attendees adds about 15% more viewers (Demio/Ainsworth) [practitioner]. Some practitioners say a large share of sales comes from replay viewers (Porterfield DCA vault) [practitioner, no figure].
- **Live vs replay conversion is disputed.** One agency study says live attendees convert to pipeline 4x more than replay viewers (Visionary Marketing, 412 B2B webinars, 2026) [practitioner/agency]. Another says replay viewers have higher intent (HeyStream) [platform/vendor].
- **Encore:** no data found for a named "encore" method. **Derived:** for small lists, rerun the live session once during the cart (mid-cart Q&A or encore) rather than relying only on the replay.
- **Downsell:** no measured data found. Practitioners use a lower tier or payment plan for non-buyers at cart close (Gap).
- **Evergreen conversion:**
  - The rule of thumb is that evergreen converts about half as well as live [practitioner/vendor].
  - Just-in-time evergreen gets 60–70% show-up but about 6% attendee-to-buyer (EasyWebinar).
  - Automate only after live proves about 10% attendee-to-buyer (EasyWebinar advice).
  - HSH non-live launch (pre-recorded webinar + thank-you-page sales + Deadline Funnel): about $7k against a $5–10k goal and far below its live launches, though over 19% of bootcamp joiners bought (27 Mar 2025) [practitioner: HSH].
  - No evergreen-challenge data found.

## 6. AI-era changes 2025–2026

- **Trust backlash ("AI slop"):**
  - Gartner (survey of 1,006 US consumers, May–June 2026, published 22 Sep 2026): 65% think brands produce too much AI content; 57% are less trusting of brand messaging; 35% rely on influencers less because of AI [independent data: Gartner, n=1,006].
  - Klaviyo/Datalily (8,000 consumers in 8 countries, Dec 2025, reported Mar 2026): visible AI marketing makes 31% trust a brand less and 7% more [independent-ish: Klaviyo-commissioned, n=8,000].
  - NP Digital (Oct 2025, n=2,000): 16% strongly and 42% somewhat trust AI content [platform/vendor survey].
- **AI presenters:** viewers who identified an AI avatar presenter reported lower trust, engagement and satisfaction, though retention was unchanged (study summarised by Philippa Hardman, Substack, about 2025) [independent data, secondhand]. **Derived:** do not front a live launch with an AI clone, and disclose any synthetic video.
- **AI behind the scenes works.** ON24 reports engagement with AI-generated webinar content (clips, summaries, derivative assets) up more than 7x in 2024 [platform/vendor: ON24 2025 report]. Personalisation produced about 4x more demo requests [platform/vendor]. **Derived uses:** AI for replay clips, recap emails, personalised follow-up by question asked, and FAQ drafting, with a human reviewing.
- **Attention:** Jeff Walker says AI sales copy is flooding the internet and attention is harder to get, so story and relationship matter more (SPI 847, Dec 2024) [practitioner]. At B2B events, sessions per attendee fell from 5.3 to 4.2 (spring 2025 → spring 2026) while meetings rose (RainFocus) [platform/vendor]. Small in-person gatherings under 150 grew 34% year on year (Bizzabo, H1 2025) [platform/vendor]. **Derived:** smaller, more interactive, more human formats are favoured.
- **Live demos:** no direct data. **Derived from the trust data:** a live, unscripted demonstration and live Q&A are the hardest things for AI content to fake, so feature them.
- **Distribution shift:** comment-to-DM launch flows (Manychat) and social-first launches, e.g. Jessi Jean [practitioner/secondary].
- **Deliverability for launch email bursts:** the Google/Yahoo bulk-sender rules (announced Oct 2023, enforced through 2024) require SPF/DKIM/DMARC, one-click unsubscribe and a spam rate under 0.3% (aim for 0.1%) above 5,000 messages a day [platform/vendor: Google/Yahoo via ESP docs]. Most lists in scope fall under 5,000 a day, but launch-week complaint rates still matter.

## 7. Agreement, disagreement, folklore

**Practitioners agree on:**
- Show-up and Day 1 activation are where most launches leak.
- Live converts better than recorded.
- Sales spike at a real deadline.
- Price tier changes the conversion range.
- Value must come before the pitch.
- Smaller lists can launch successfully with the right offer (Walker: failures trace to the offer).

**They disagree on:**
- **Free vs paid entry:** volume and list growth versus commitment. The data shows a real but modest attendance effect.
- **Where to pitch in a challenge:** VIP on Day 1, reveal at about 60%, gradual exposure, or the classic Day 5.
- **Facebook groups:** "dying" (Skool sellers) versus still effective (Denise DT).
- **High-ticket direct sales from a webinar:** 1–4% (SFI) versus 5–20% (other vendors).
- **Replay vs live buyer quality.**
- **Cart length:** 5–7 days versus 24–72 hours.
- **Whether webinars are "dead":** platform data shows attendance flat or up from 2024 to 2025.

**Folklore to drop or qualify:**
- "50% of sales on the last day" as a universal. Measured: 40% in the final 2 days; 40–60% on the last day for affiliates in 2016.
- "SMS doubles attendance." Independent effect is about 14% relative.
- "Paid challenges get 70–80% completion, free 3–8%." Vendor figures with no method.
- "47% average webinar attendance" as a norm. It is definition-dependent; ad-fed creator funnels run about 25–35%.
- "Gamification increases engagement 30x." Unsupported.
- "Calendar invites cut no-shows 30%." Vendor claim.
- "Challenges triple conversions." Self-reported by Alina Vincent's students.
- "You need a big list." A list of about 200–500 can launch.
- "Evergreen 'live' labels and resetting timers are harmless." Regulators treat both as deceptive.
- "Webinars are dead." Not supported by platform data.

## Implications for the skill (derived)

1. **Format chooser** inputs: list size, warmth, price point, host energy and recurring vs one-off.
   - <1,000 or no ads: paid workshop or bootcamp ($27–$97) or a repeated live webinar.
   - 1,000–5,000 warm: a 5-day or 3-day challenge.
   - $2k+: application or call close.
   - Low energy: "live-ish" (recorded teaching, live Q&A and close).
2. **Built-in funnel calculator** using low/mid/high ranges, each labelled with its source and confidence:
   - Registration from list: user input (no benchmark found; assume a 10–30% planning band and flag it as an assumption).
   - Live show-up: free 30–45%, paid 50–70%.
   - Day 1 → Day 5 retention: ~50%.
   - VIP take: 4–15%.
   - Attendee-to-buyer: by price tier.
   - Revenue share in the last 48 hours: 35–60%.
3. **Show-up checklist** that the skill generates:
   - .ics and add-to-calendar on the thank-you page.
   - Confirmation, plus reminders at −1 day, −1 hour and "live now" by email.
   - SMS at −1 hour and "live now" if consent exists.
   - Day 1 homework.
   - Replay emailed within 1–3 hours.
4. **Close planner:**
   - Cart length 3–5 days (5–7 with partners).
   - Mid-cart live Q&A, final-day live session, final-day email cadence (3+ emails).
   - Payment plan with a modest premium.
   - Bonuses that are real and expire for real.
   - A "double close" defined as live close + deadline close.
5. **Compliance guardrails, enforced as warnings:**
   - No resetting timers, and no "live" label on recorded sessions.
   - Every deadline must actually close.
   - Earnings claims need substantiation (FTC).
   - Testimonials must be genuine (FTC reviews rule).
   - UK audiences: DMCC Act.
   - Watch the chargeback ratio (Visa 1.5% excessive threshold from Apr 2026).
6. **AI stance:**
   - AI drafts and repurposes (emails, clips, recap, FAQ, personalised follow-ups), with human review.
   - The host appears live.
   - Disclose synthetic media.
   - Avoid an AI-polished voice in copy for audiences already sceptical of AI content.
7. **Post-event module:**
   - No-show replay sequence.
   - Optional encore.
   - Downsell or payment plan at close.
   - Debrief that captures daily attendance, VIP %, offer click %, sales by hour, refunds at 14 and 30 days, and unsubscribes.
   - Recommend evergreen only after a live run proves about 10% attendee-to-buyer (expect about half the live rate).
8. **Small-list realism:** tell users that 1–5% of the list buying is normal-to-strong. Recommend a waitlist ("hand-raiser") step, since waitlists convert far higher (34.9% in one case).
9. **Calls track for service businesses:** the conversion event may be a booked call. Track show and close rates on calls separately.
