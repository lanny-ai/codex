## 1. What matters most (10 lines)

1. **Start recruiting at least 90 days out**, because the best partners book their promo calendars months ahead: "I need 90 days to recruit. Why? Because the big people are booked." (M18 ~863)
2. **Never launch an untested offer.** Beta-test it with a few partners, then go to big partners with proof: "You can't get your launches big without good test data." (M18 ~924–936)
3. **Recruit in waves, biggest first.** Start with your own network, then your top partners one-on-one, then ask each "yes" for three introductions, then blast the wider list, then run a referral contest. (M18 ~871–896)
4. **Agree on what a good result looks like before anyone promotes.** Ask "what is your expectation of a good mailing?", then follow up against that number. (M18 ~444–471; J19 ~1075–1080)
5. **Structure every deal and put it in writing**: the type, the volume, the start and stop dates, what happens if it goes well, and what happens if it goes bad. (M18 ~945–952; J19 ~732–804)
6. **Design leaderboards and contests to reward momentum, not just sales.** Count referred partners' sales toward the referrer's position. (M18 ~913–921)
7. **Make your best partners feel special** with custom landers, custom swipe copy and custom assets, for top partners only. (M18 ~1017–1030; J19 ~1421–1428)
8. **The launch window is short, so have the tech, the split tests and the segmentation ready before day one**: "Every launch I've ever done, I have broken a server." (M18 ~989–1001)
9. **Your reputation is the real asset.** Disclose weaknesses up front, reserve the right to test, and make partners whole if a deal fails. (M18 ~953–988, ~1083–1088)
10. **Run it as a sales team with a scorecard.** Have a closer, a support/project person, daily activity metrics, quotas and weekly reports. "Common sense… just not common practice." (J19 ~158–160, ~429–457)

## 3. Her launch method, step by step

Validity key: **[Valid 2026]**, **[Valid, adapt]**, **[Dated]**.

### 3.1 Planning: decide the goal, then the timeline

- **Decide what the launch is for, and accept its cost.** "Be clear about what you want to accomplish. If you want to have the biggest launch ever, you better be prepared to do reciprocals for… ever." She gives an eight-figure example: "We email that… list three times a day for a year to get through all the reciprocals." Her choices: care more about brand, so pay more up front; want to sell high ticket on the back end, so focus on buyers and leads. (M18 ~848–858) **[Valid 2026]**: the skill should ask "what is this launch for: list, cash, back-end high ticket, or brand?"
- **A launch can be a list-building event that feeds a later launch.** "Let's say that your book is coming out in March. We'll do a docu-series launch in February. So we can build up that list and… launch your book to that list." (M18 ~409–411) **[Valid 2026]**: a free challenge or workshop that builds the list ahead of a paid offer is the same pattern.
- **Timeline: at least 90 days, ideally 6 months.** "I need 90 days to recruit… If you want to have a big launch, you need big affiliates… minimum 90 days. I would love six months, but I never get it." (M18 ~863–870) **[Valid 2026]**
- **Check partners' launch calendars.** If partners all launch in the same month, they cannot promote for you. Ask early, or offer an incentive to move someone else's slot. (M18 ~1073–1082) **[Valid 2026]**
- **Keep the pipeline full between launches.** On evergreen she said "Every time I sell a house, I become unemployed". Book recurring promotions every six weeks at the start of the year, and get into partners' autoresponders, upsell flows and thank-you pages. "Know how long these deals take… it's going to be a long conversation over many months." (M18 ~659–682) **[Valid 2026]**

### 3.2 Test before you recruit: proof first

- "I will not take a client with an untested launch or offer, ever… Am I going to bring something that I don't know works to any of my partners? No." (M18 ~859–861)
- She calls this kicking the doors down with test data: "No one likes to be the test bunny… Test your launch… in beta with a few partners. We do 10 partners doing 10,000 clicks each." (M18 ~924–930)
- Her go/no-go thresholds: below about a $1 EPC, or below about $2 earnings per lead on lead-gen funnels, "we're not going to do it". If the numbers are close, for example an 80-cent EPC, "let's take the next 90 days… optimize… figure out where it's broken and then we'll roll it out and we'll recruit." (M18 ~931–936)
- "Give yourself breathing room… every list will convert differently." (M18 ~943–945)
- **[Valid 2026, scaled down]** A small business has no affiliates to beta-test with. The equivalent is a **pilot cohort** or a small paid beta that produces conversion numbers and testimonials before a big push.

### 3.3 List building and pre-launch: recruitment in waves

Her slide heading, as transcribed, is "Deploy in waves" (M18 ~858). Her order (M18 ~871–896):
1. **Own network first**, "because I don't want to give away a second tier [commission]".
2. **Then partners one-on-one, biggest to smallest**: "Go talk to them one on one. Start with your whales first… the top 20 affiliates drive 80% of your revenue… Get them nailed down."
3. **Ask every yes for referrals**: "Can you refer me to… three people that I don't know yet? And I'll second tier you on that… I will do the same for you… This is not a one-way deal."
4. **Then ask partners to blast their own affiliate lists** "to clean up the rest. Get a bunch of minnows."
5. **Then the referral contest**: "Before launch, we'll say, hey, we have a launch coming up in 90 days. If you can refer me to anybody that I don't know yet… the most [referrals] get $5,000…" She adds: "I'm always surprised who they drag in, always."

Other recruitment points:
- **Know what a good partner is to you.** "For me, anybody that has over 100,000 names on their list is a good affiliate… I don't want to talk to your people that are minnows. That's for my junior JV manager." (M18 ~921–924)
- **Intro swaps.** Introduce the same number of new people to each other, "match… size and ability", and "always ask first, do you want an intro?" (M18 ~826–834)

**[Valid 2026]**: the logic of sequencing by leverage is timeless. **[Adapt]**: for a small coach, "partners" means peers with audiences (podcasters, complementary coaches, community owners), and waves 3–5 shrink to a single "who else should I talk to?" ask.

### 3.4 Pre-launch content and partner assets

- **Custom assets for top partners only.** Custom landing pages ("Glenn [endorses] on their sales page"), "custom email swipe copy… work with their copywriters and your copywriters", an advertorial, and "Facebook compliant graphics". Swipes and landers should match the partner's niche. "This is only for your largest affiliates… all this stuff will mean that they mail more. And what they're mailing keeps converting." (M18 ~1017–1030) **[Valid 2026]**
- **Ask what assets work for each partner before you hand them a swipe**: "What assets work best for them… short ClickBank email, bait emails… longer content pieces? What are they mailing right now?" (J19 ~1089–1092) **[Valid 2026]**
- **Different traffic needs different entry points.** "If someone comes from YouTube, they're going to buy from a VSL… if… a content blogger… written." When an offer fizzles, "is the offer itself fatigued? Or is it all the assets and the customer acquisition in front of it?" (J19 ~341–380) **[Valid 2026]**
- **Segment at opt-in.** She credits Tyler Bramlett: a short welcome video asks new subscribers to tick their interests from a list of 15, "so now your list is segmenting itself as it comes in… another way you don't burn out your… list." (M18 ~1002–1016) **[Valid 2026; even more important now]**

### 3.5 Affiliate/JV management

**Deal structure** (M18 ~808–826; J19 ~732–804)
- Deal types: straight lead swap; lead swap with commission; click swap with or without commission; **hybrid deals**. Her hybrid example: "for every four leads you send me, we'll give you one sale back."
- Her preferred hybrid: "guarantee the front end… then give away a percentage of the back end… so they're not sending me crap." In 2019 she gives a **three-tier payment** example for Agora: a per-lead guarantee, a bonus if the lead buys the book, then a share of the $3,000 back end.
- **Spell out every term**: "What is a start and stop time of this deal?… When we're done… leads that didn't buy… Where do they go?… What happens if the deal goes bad?… What happens if the deal goes well?" Then "everything in writing… store it somewhere that your team can actually refer to it", meaning a CRM, not someone's email. (J19 ~742–804, ~846–848)
- **[Valid 2026]** The clauses about lead ownership and what happens after the mailing map directly to modern co-hosted webinars and summit partnerships.

**Set expectations before they promote** (M18 ~444–471; J19 ~1068–1097, which she calls "Set the table")
- "What is your expectation of a good mailing? And I leave that open." Her story: a big affiliate earned $25K commission on one mailing and then vanished. Amber found out later the affiliate expected $50K: "you came in 50 percent lower… So after that, I swore that I wasn't going to go in blind again."
- With a target agreed, a beat triggers "Would you like to send it on opens? Here's a new subject line. Here's a new tracking link." A miss triggers a "why do you think this happened?" conversation.
- Also ask: "What makes their best and worst partnerships work… Dream big together on what happens if it's a perfect partnership." (J19 ~1081–1093)
- **[Valid 2026]** This is a high-value, low-effort habit for any co-promotion.

**What "all in" means to her** (M18 ~937–942)
- "Two solo emails minimum that includes leads and buyers with two sends on opens. Two referrals [to] good affiliates that I don't know yet… supporting me on social and then at the end cleaning up the rest of their affiliate email list." The host adds that you should ask for four if you want two.
- **[Valid, adapt]** For a small partner, the ask becomes two emails, one social post, and one introduction.

**Reciprocity and fairness** (M18 ~770–778, ~953–988)
- "Everybody wants one way deals." She won't burn her list on a partner offer that doesn't convert. Instead, "I'll increase your commission… make intros… be on your podcast… promote you on social."
- **Reserve the right to test, even inside a swap.** If the partner's offer bombs: "give me something else I can test… Or… I will give you a dollar per click for everything that I owe you."
- "My duty is to my list and my customers first, not you… But I will make you whole in some way."
- **Disclose weaknesses up front.** A client with a 20% refund rate didn't tell partners and "pissed them off". "This community is so small… We all talk."
- **[Valid 2026]**

**Leaderboards, contests and prizes** (M18 ~886–921; J19 ~1003–1006)
- There are two contests:
  - A **pre-launch referral contest**: prizes for most referrals, and for referrals who send 100+ leads.
  - A **sales leaderboard** during the launch.
- She calls the leaderboard design "get crafty": "anyone that they refer me to that actually mail counts for their position on the leader board."
- At The Truth About Cancer (the $9.8M launch, late November 2016 per J19 ~1167) "we gave away $100,000 in cash. The top prize is $25,000." Mike Adams would have won on sales alone, but the prize went to someone whose own sales plus referred partners' sales were bigger, "the person who gave me the most momentum."
- Size prizes to "whatever you can sustain… matches your goals".
- In 2019 an accelerator student's **flash contests**, which give smaller affiliates a chance to win, were credited with a $1M result. (J19 ~1003–1006)
- **[Valid 2026; dollar sizes are big-launch only]**

**Incentive levers other than commission** (M18 ~800–807, ~834–848; J19 ~268–340)
- Higher commission or CPA tiers for volume, with the right to step back down ("a certain threshold… crank them down").
- **Speed of payment**: "pay you in 24 hours… watch how fast they mail"; "cash is king".
- Podcast appearances, social promotion, introductions, a guru webinar, and testing partners' offers on her list.
- **[Valid 2026]**

**Partner communication** (M18 ~701–726; J19 ~381–428)
- Send a monthly broadcast to all partners that is "more than we got this hot new email swipe copy". Share winning ads, "here's our new office… because of you", and **affiliate partner spotlights** with a second-tier link.
- Get off email and text: "get on the phone… or… video chat"; "most affiliate managers fail… because they like to hide behind email."
- **[Valid 2026; the tools are dated]**: she names Skype, which Microsoft retired in May 2025. Voxer and voice memos still exist. The principle of voice or video over email is valid.

**Second-tier commission policy** (M18 ~525–598; J19 ~895–936)
- This was the most-quoted takeaway in 2018. Write a policy that all second-tier commission goes to the company. In her audits, "90% of the time… the parents are the affiliate managers themselves and not the company."
- Her own team pools second-tier money into a shared account that pays for staff event travel, and the person who brought the deal gets 25%.
- **[Valid 2026]** This is relevant only once a business has staff recruiting partners.

**JV policy, external and internal** (M18 ~472–488; J19 ~937–950)
- **External** (public): your demographic, the offer types that work, what you won't promote, and "you reserve the right to test".
- **Internal**: blacklist, whitelist, private commission ceilings.
- The host adds: "say it out loud to them", meaning don't just post the policy and assume partners read it. (M18 ~493–504, host)
- **[Valid 2026]**

### 3.6 Launch-day and in-launch mechanics

- **Prepare the tech, because the launch window can't be redone.** "Every launch I've ever done, I have broken a server… Please increase the size of your servers… backup URLs, set up proper segmentation from the get-go."
- **Have split tests ready to run.** "We just have such a limited amount of time to squeeze as much juice from that lemon… We don't have a luxury of evergreen in the middle of a launch." She wants headlines, trailers, sales pages and carts ready to test. (M18 ~989–1001)
- **Respond to partners within service levels, fastest for the biggest.** Weekdays within 8 hours, weekends within 24, "on a launch, I'm way strict… tiered based on how big they are… If a whale… is emailing me, he's going to be serviced first." (M18 ~744–751)
- **React to partner performance during the launch.** If a partner beats their target, ask for a resend to non-openers with a new subject line and link. If they miss, have the conversation. (M18 ~464–469)
- **Buy media during the launch.** "If you want your launch to get big and you're not buying media during your launch, I would… at the very least, just retarget."
- **Host counterpoint (Glenn, M18 ~1037–1066):**
  - He ramps paid media about a month before internal launches.
  - He warns that if you already run heavy Facebook traffic to a similar offer, you must pull it back during the launch or you lose money: "the crossover with JV and Facebook now is so big."
- **[Valid, adapt]**: media and retargeting are still valid. The 2018 Facebook economics are **[Dated]**: Apple's 2021 iOS 14.5 tracking change hurt attribution, and her own 2019 numbers show health launches shrinking (see 3.10).
- **Cart open/close, scarcity, bonuses and webinar/event structure: not in these transcripts.** Neither talk covers cart windows, deadline mechanics, bonus stacks, webinar run-of-show, show-up rates or close-rate scripts. Her "launches" in these talks are mostly docuseries and lead-gen funnels pushed by affiliate email. The only bonus mention is "Offer a bonus" as a lever to persuade a one-way partner (M18 ~836), not a buyer bonus. **The skill must source cart, scarcity and event mechanics elsewhere.**

### 3.7 Team roles and project management

**2018 structure** (M18 ~689–700)
- "A project manager… handles… all the stuff in between the clients and our team. Make sure nothing gets dropped."
- "A lead JV manager… Dealmaker closer. They're on the phones."
- "A JV assistant. Their only job is to make things easy for the lead… grabbing links or pulling commission reports… getting creatives."
- "You want the deal maker to be making deals, not filling out… paperwork."

**2019 refinement** (J19 ~196–267)
- "Your affiliate manager should be a salesperson… They need quotas… key performance indicators… they're really hunters. The best affiliate teams have a hunter and a growth manager."
- The **owner** should come in "like an Air Force strike", for example a co-registration page or a webinar together, not run the day-to-day.
- **Shared inbox for redundancy**: "partners@ / JV@ / affiliates@… my whole team shares that one inbox." (M18 ~682–688)

**[Valid 2026, scaled down]** For a solo coach this collapses to one person wearing three hats, which is an argument for the skill to automate the assistant and project-manager parts (tracker, asset kit, reminders).

### 3.8 Metrics she watches

- **Offer metrics:** EPC (earnings per click) and EPL/CPL (earnings or cost per lead). Thresholds: about $1 EPC, about $2 EPL. "They're both important." (M18 ~931–936)
- **Internal thresholds:** her client gave a partner $2 per lead "and… $60,000 later, those leads did not back out. We made maybe $20,000." Know your own break-even before you guarantee. (M18 ~518–525)
- **Activity metrics, every morning:** "how many [personal] emails… mass blast emails… phone calls… video chats… and… the clicks that we booked and the sales." These give her an early warning, because zero activity this week means low sales next month. (J19 ~429–457)
- **Monthly scorecard per manager:** new relationships (5 per month), bookings (30 per month excellent, 20 good, 10 or fewer unacceptable), second-tier growth (goal $100K per year), partner NPS above 85%, and regular ROI reports. Rating plus outcome analysis. (M18 ~727–763)
- **Accountability report:** contacts by channel, time with top-10 versus current versus new partners, bookings, and revenue per click. "Pick the top three metrics first." "Do not have your affiliate manager give themselves a score. They are going to give themselves an A every time." (J19 ~961–1067, ~630–633)
- **Networking scorecard for events** (her team attending conferences, not hosting): a pre-event target list (5–10 current partners to grow, with specific numbers, and 5–10 new targets with reasons), nightly and morning review, and a post-event CRM upload. Review event ROI at 1, 3, 6 and 12 months. Her top managers average "$300,000 worth of business from every event". Attending an event costs about $5,700. (J19 ~491–700)
- **Show-up rate and live-event conversion: not covered.**
- **Industry metric confusion (2019):** "some people are measuring by EPC… revenue per name… revenue per delivered email." (J19 ~1312–1320) **[Valid 2026]**: the skill should fix its definitions up front.

### 3.9 What she says breaks launches

- The wrong person in the partner seat: "You set them up to fail." (M18 ~422–431; J19 ~196–267)
- Deals with no structure, and no agreed expectation before mailing. (M18 ~432–471)
- No written JV policy, or only a generic one. (M18 ~472–488)
- Untested offers, and less than 90 days of recruiting. (M18 ~859–877)
- Tech that can't handle volume, and no split tests ready. (M18 ~989–1001)
- Over-promising reciprocity, then not delivering ("keep your reputation clean"). (M18 ~1083–1088)
- Hiding behind email. (J19 ~381–428)
- Contacting partners only "two days before their launch". (M18 ~702–703)
- Launching from a dead start every time. (M18 ~659–682)
- Over-reliance on affiliates: "it makes me nervous when any kind of mine has like 90 to 100% of their revenue coming from affiliates." (J19 ~1567–1573)

**2019 headwinds** (J19 ~1144–1320)
1. CPAs and conversion falling for over 60% of health, wellness and financial partners. Her launch peaks fell from $9.8M (2016) to $4.8M (2017) and $6.7M (early 2018). One client went from $1M on 150K leads in 2018 to $404K on 137K leads in 2019. **[Dated numbers; the trend is a valid warning]**
2. Email deliverability: "ESPs are stopping us… throttling… AWeber shutting down clients… for mailing third-party offers." **[Still valid, and stricter in 2026]**: Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender rules since February 2024 require authentication, one-click unsubscribe and low spam rates. (This is my context, not from her talk.)
3. Partners dilute their support by mailing "eight different offers in the three-day window". **[Valid]**
4. Compliance: friendly-from names, dual unsubscribe, suppression files. **[Valid; she predicted lawsuits]**
5. Partners failing to honour agreements, and click-bait sends. **[Valid]**
6. Big partners stopping third-party promotion. **[Valid]**
7. No common metric language. **[Valid]**

### 3.10 Post-launch

- **Pay what you owe, fast, and do the reciprocals.** The reciprocal debt is the real cost of a big launch ("three times a day for a year"). (M18 ~850–853)
- **Use the "goes well" clause.** If a partner did well, plan co-registrations and go big on each other's launches. "Go bigger with your best affiliates… Don't do this with new people right away." (J19 ~789–796, ~1479–1489)
- **Treat a deal gone bad as a relationship opportunity.** "This is our opportunity to strengthen our relationship… she made a referral to our accelerator the next day." (J19 ~827–845)
- **Monetize the back end.** Successful operators "work on improving your back-end monetization and continuity. So you can increase CPA, pay faster, pay on more parts of your funnel." (J19 ~1331–1336, ~1503–1504)
- **Upload new contacts to the CRM, tagged by size and niche; follow up "ruthlessly".** (J19 ~670–682)
- **[Valid 2026]**

## 4. Fit with a small service business (coach or consultant, list of 500–5,000, no affiliates yet)

### What scales down well

| Amber's principle | Small-business version for the skill |
|---|---|
| 90-day minimum runway | Plan the event 8–12 weeks out. Partner podcast and guest slots book 4–8 weeks ahead, so outreach starts early. |
| Test before you scale | Run a pilot (beta cohort, small paid workshop, or a past-client run) to get a conversion number and testimonials before a public launch. |
| Waves by leverage | Wave 1: past clients and warm network. Wave 2: 5–10 hand-picked peers with audiences, approached one-on-one. Wave 3: ask each "yes" for one intro. Wave 4: your own list and social. |
| "What's a good result for you?" | Ask every co-host or promo partner what success looks like for them (sign-ups, leads, commission, exposure) and write it down. |
| Structured, written deals | A one-page partner agreement: what they promote, dates, commission or swap, who owns the leads, and what happens if it underperforms. |
| Custom assets for top partners | A partner kit (2 emails, 2 social posts, graphics, tracking link), personalised for the top 3 partners. |
| Segment at opt-in | Ask 1–3 questions on the registration form (problem, stage, budget) to tag and personalise follow-up. |
| Tech prepared, split test ready | Load-test registration, have a backup link for the live room, and pre-write two subject lines for each key email. |
| Reputation first, make partners whole | Honour every promise, and disclose limits up front (refund policy, who it is not for). |
| Daily activity metrics | A simple tracker: outreach sent, conversations, partners confirmed, registrations per day versus target. |
| Monthly partner touch that isn't a pitch | A quarterly "partner thank-you" email with results and a spotlight. |
| Speed of payment | Pay partner commissions within 7 days of the refund window closing, and say so up front. |

### What does not scale down, or does not apply

- **Commission-driven recruitment at scale**: the $100K prize pools, 30 bookings a month, 100K-name partner thresholds and $1 EPC guarantees. A coach with 500–5,000 names has neither the margin nor partners of that size. **Reciprocity is also asymmetric**: a small list cannot repay a big partner's mailing, so offer non-list currency instead (podcast slot, guest training, introductions, done-for-you content). She lists these levers (M18 ~776–778, ~843–848).
- **Second-tier policies, affiliate-manager scorecards and the hunter/growth-manager split**: these only matter once there is staff. Keep a placeholder for "when you hire".
- **Contest mechanics**: keep them simple and small. For example, a public "top 3 partners" thank-you, a free seat or a small cash prize, or her flash-contest idea (J19 ~1003). The insight to keep is rewarding referred momentum, not only direct sales.
- **Email-volume tactics** ("three times a day for a year", resend to non-openers for partners): risky under 2024+ bulk-sender rules and with small, relationship-based lists.
- **Docuseries as the launch vehicle**: the skill's events (challenges, workshops, webinars, bootcamps) are live and interactive. She doesn't cover show-up, live engagement, the pitch, cart close or post-event sales calls, so those must come from other sources.
- **Health/info-product economics**: front-end loss leaders funded by back-end continuity. A service business's back end is usually a high-ticket program or retainer sold on calls, so "EPC" becomes registrations to attendees to booked calls to closed clients.
