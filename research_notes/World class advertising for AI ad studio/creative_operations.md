## 1. The creative brief: what the best briefs contain

### Takeaway
The industry consensus artifact is a short, single-minded brief: one problem, one audience, one desired response, one proposition (not a tagline), with proof points that support rather than list. The two most portable templates are the IPA/APG-style brief (problem, audience, objective, single-minded proposition, support, mandatories) and the GET / WHO / TO / BY one-liner; performance shops add a lean "why are we making this ad" brief split into strategic and production halves.

### Cited Findings
- IPA "Better Briefs" guidance: a good brief is "laser-focused on the problem or opportunity the creative work needs to solve"; a single-minded brief contains only one key message; that message is not a tagline and is not meant to be creative itself — it points where the creative solution should go; proof points should support it without becoming a list. — [IPA, "The best way for a client to brief an agency" / Better Briefs booklet](https://ipa.co.uk/media/12104/better-briefs-booklet_10_single-pages.pdf); also hosted by the 4As: [AAAA copy](https://www.aaaa.org/wp-content/uploads/IPA-Best_way_for_client_to_brief_agency.pdf?access_pid=104907)
- The same IPA booklet cites the BetterBriefs study claim that roughly one third of marketing budgets are potentially wasted through poor briefs and misdirected work (industry-survey claim, not an audited figure). — [IPA Better Briefs](https://ipa.co.uk/media/12104/better-briefs-booklet_10_single-pages.pdf)
- A commonly cited client-brief element list (Smart Insights, echoing IPA/ANA/Gavin Llewelyn): scope and ambition, budget, timescale, target audience, objectives, single-minded proposition, mandatory deliverables, background. — [Smart Insights](https://www.smartinsights.com/agency-marketing/growing-a-marketing-agency/what-makes-for-effective-briefing/)
- Richard Holman (2019): every good brief needs one sentence that encapsulates its essence (the single-minded proposition); he credits John Hegarty with a three-part test — does the line make rational sense, emotional sense, and could it work as a rough press ad on its own. — [Richard Holman, "Simplicity, Inspiration & Truth"](https://www.richardholman.com/blog/2019/6/17/simplicity-inspiration-amp-truth-how-to-write-better-creative-briefs)
- Guidance on what good single-minded propositions have in common is collected by Creative Brief Workshops. — [creativebriefworkshops.com](https://creativebriefworkshops.com/2044-what-all-good-single-minded-propositions-have-in-common/)
- GET / (WHO) / TO / BY format: GET = the specific target and their problem/current mindset; TO = the mindset change or action sought; BY = the single message/proposition that gets them there. — [Strategy Finishing School, "How to use the creative brief GET/WHO/TO/BY"](https://strategyfinishingschool.com/how-to-use-the-creative-brief-get-who-to-by/); [Scribd, GET_TO_BY Brief Writing](https://www.scribd.com/document/452348343/GET-TO-BY-Brief-Writing)
- Origin is disputed: one course resource says BBDO developed it; a 2023 Marketing Babylon critique says BBDO used it in its briefing template but the author "couldn't track a single/first origin". The same critique argues weak Get/To/By briefs fail at each step (vague audience, vague end goal). — [Course Sidekick](https://www.coursesidekick.com/marketing/3528885); [Marketing Babylon, "Get/to/bye-bye strategy" (Feb 2023)](https://marketingbabylon.com/2023/02/24/get-to-bye-bye-strategy-how-to-fix-advertisings-favourite-framework/)
- A marketing-brief guide calls Get/To/By "the simplest and one of the most effective strategy frameworks" for a brief (opinion). — [Briefly, marketing brief guide](https://trybriefly.com/resources/marketing-brief)
- Performance-side briefs: Motion's help centre publishes copyable brief templates from power users; contributors stress stating *why* the ad is being made and keeping briefs lean/simple. — [Motion Help Center, "Creative brief templates built by the pros"](https://help.motionapp.com/en/articles/12168768-creative-brief-templates-built-by-the-pros)
- Motion's 2024 "Day in the Life of a Creative Strategist" talk splits briefing into a **strategic brief** and a **production brief**. — [Motion talk (2024)](https://motionapp.com/library/talk/a-day-in-the-life-of-a-creative-strategist-2024/)
- A generic (non-agency) brief template list for comparison — background, objectives, audience, awareness levels, key messaging/positioning, measurement, execution, timelines, budget. Notably includes "Awareness Levels," the bridge to performance briefs. — [Demand Metric template](https://www.demandmetric.com/content/creative-brief-template)

### Inferences
- For an AI studio, the brief should be a two-layer artifact: (a) strategic brief = Get/Who/To/By + single-minded proposition + 1–3 proof points + mandatories (brand assets, legal, offer); (b) production brief = format, length, hook options, script/shot list, creator/persona, aspect ratios, naming tag. This mirrors Motion's strategic/production split and the IPA single-message rule.
- Hegarty's "could it be a rough press ad" test is a cheap automated gate: if the proposition can't be rendered as a single headline+visual, it's not single-minded enough.
- Get/To/By maps cleanly onto performance vocabulary: GET = persona + awareness stage; TO = desired action/belief shift; BY = angle/proposition.

## 2. Concepting: generating and selecting ideas; idea vs execution vs tactic; award-judging criteria

### Takeaway
Award bodies score separately for idea, strategy, execution and results, which is a ready-made rubric: D&AD asks "Is the idea inspiring? Is it brilliantly executed? Is it fit for purpose?", and Cannes weights shift by category (e.g. Creative Effectiveness 25/25/50 idea/strategy/impact). The "idea vs execution" distinction is institutionalised in these rubrics; practitioner copy rules (Harry Dry) give a tighter line-level gate.

### Cited Findings
- D&AD Pencils 2026 entry kit: judges ask "Is the idea inspiring?", "Is it brilliantly executed?", "Is it fit for purpose?"; for Digital Experience Design, execution is prioritised over idea. — [D&AD Awards 2026 Entry Kit (PDF)](https://media.dandad.org/documents/Entry_Kit_2026_ENG.pdf); [D&AD Pencils](https://www.dandad.org/awards/pencils)
- D&AD New Blood: Graphite Pencil bar = "a good idea, well executed, and relevant to the brief"; Yellow Pencil = outstanding work that nails the criteria; White Pencil replaces Yellow in Impact categories. — [D&AD New Blood advice](https://www.dandad.org/insights/awards/what-winning-a-new-blood-awards-pencil-means-new-blood-advice); [D&AD FAQs](https://www.dandad.org/awards/new-blood-awards/faqs)
- D&AD 2025 jurors emphasised commercial viability — impact on business success and consumer behaviour. — [shots.net, 2025 Pencil winners](https://shots.net/news/view/d-and-ad-announces-2025-pencil-winners)
- Cannes Lions Creative Effectiveness: 25% idea, 25% strategy, 50% impact and results. — [Cannes Lions, Creative Effectiveness – what you need to know](https://www.canneslions.com/awards/lions/creative-effectiveness/what-you-need-to-know)
- Cannes Lions Creative Strategy: 30% interpretation of business/brand challenge, 30% insight/breakthrough thinking, 20% creative idea, 20% outcome/results. — [Cannes Lions, Creative Strategy](https://www.canneslions.com/awards/lions/creative-strategy/what-you-need-to-know)
- Cannes Direct (2026 juror Mark Elwood, as told to Creative Salon): 30% idea, 20% strategy, 20% execution, 30% impact/results. — [Creative Salon, juror predictions 2026](https://creative.salon/articles/features/cannes-lions-juror-predictions-2026)
- Older Cannes Creative B2B (2022): 30% idea, 20% strategy, 30% execution, 20% results; Film/Audio kits list idea, execution and impact without percentages (2022 Film kit: judged "predominantly" on idea and execution). — [Creative B2B Lions 2022](https://info.canneslions.com/creative-b2b-lions-2022/entries-and-judging/about); [CL 2022 Entry Kit](https://enterprise.orf.at/fileadmin/newsletter/2022/03-M%C3%A4r/Lions_Live_Briefing/CL_2022_ENTRY_KIT.pdf)
- Harry Dry (Marketing Examples) three copy rules, used as a review checklist: (1) Can I visualise it? (concrete, "keep zooming in"), (2) Can I falsify it? (specific, checkable claims over "best"), (3) Can nobody else say this? (if a competitor could say it, it doesn't differentiate). Wording of rule 3 varies across write-ups. — [Upgrow, Harry Dry 3 rules](https://upgrow.io/blog/harry-dry-copywriting-3-rules); [Impact Thinking](https://impactthinking.beehiiv.com/p/harry-dry-3-rules-copywriting); [Demand Curve #202](https://www.demandcurve.com/newsletters/growth-newsletter-202)
- Distinctive assets as an execution gate: Romaniuk's grid scores each brand asset on Fame (% of category buyers linking it to the brand) and Uniqueness (% of links that go to this brand rather than competitors); quadrants roughly "Use or Lose" (high/high), "Avoid Solo Use" (high fame/low uniqueness), "Investment Potential" (low fame/high uniqueness), "Ignore or Test" (low/low). Small brands are advised to start with high-uniqueness assets and repeat, not change, them. Romaniuk: better one asset at 100/100 than several with only potential. — [ResearchGate figure, Romaniuk 2018 grid](https://www.researchgate.net/figure/Distinctive-Asset-grid-from-Romaniuk-2018-The-Distinctive-Asset-grid-combines_fig1_392496534); [nineblaess, DBAs for small businesses](https://www.nineblaess.de/blog/distinctive-brand-assets/); [TianPan summary (2025)](https://tianpan.co/blog/2025/09/01/building-distinctive-brand-assets-by-jenni-romaniuk)
- 2026 peer-reviewed benchmark: shape-based assets are strongest across industries; an average distinctive asset scores ~26% Fame and ~54% Uniqueness (as reported by secondary source). — [International Journal of Advertising (2026)](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/02650487.2026.2637295)

### Inferences
- A usable "idea vs tactic" test, derived from the rubrics: an *idea* survives a change of format/channel and can be stated in one sentence tied to the proposition (scores on "idea" and "strategy"); a *tactic/execution* is a format, trick or hook that only works in one placement (scores on "execution"). In performance terms, idea ≈ angle/concept; execution ≈ hook + format.
- An AI studio can implement a weighted scorecard directly: e.g. for performance work use Cannes Direct-style weights (Idea 30 / Strategy 20 / Execution 20 / Predicted impact 30), plus Harry Dry's three binary checks on every line, plus a distinctive-asset check (is a high-uniqueness brand asset present in the first seconds?).

### Gaps
- Could not retrieve primary text from "Hey Whipple, Squeeze This" (Luke Sullivan), "Predatory Thinking" (Dave Trott) or "Alchemy" (Rory Sutherland). [background, unverified]: Sullivan advises generating a large volume of ideas (a wall of rough thumbnails) and killing most; the art director + copywriter "creative team" pairing dates to Bill Bernbach at DDB (1949+); Trott emphasises "the problem is usually the answer" and the order Impact → Communication → Persuasion; Sutherland argues for testing "illogical" ideas because rational ones are already competed away. Verify before citing.
- No primary account of how CDs at specific agencies run concept reviews (e.g. W+K "work-in-progress" reviews, BBH "Black Sheep" ethos) was retrieved.

## 3. Performance creative agencies: research, matrices, concept vs iteration vs variant, naming, cadence, analytics

### Takeaway
Performance shops operate a 7-step loop (research → ideation → briefing → production → evaluation/QA → launch → creative analysis), anchored on a layered taxonomy (Angle → Mechanic → Hook → Format, with persona and awareness stage mapped on top), tagged through strict naming conventions, and measured with hook rate (3s plays/impressions) and hold rate (15s plays or ThruPlays/3s plays). Since Meta's Andromeda retrieval system, the emphasis has shifted from small iterations to genuinely different concepts.

### Cited Findings
**Taxonomy (Motion, 2025–2026; single vendor's taxonomy, not an industry standard)**
- Motion's stack: Messaging Angle → Creative Mechanic → Hook → Visual Format. Angle = the strategic reason the ad should matter, stateable in one phrase, recurring across multiple ads, and explicitly *not* a format or a hook. Hook = the execution of the angle in the first few seconds. Mechanic = the cognitive/emotional move that makes the concept land (distinct from hook tactics and formats). Format = what it looks like (static, video, carousel, UGC). "Concept" is used for the finished idea built from these layers rather than a separate tier. — [Motion Frameworks library](https://library.motionapp.com/frameworks/); [Motion Creative Mechanics](https://library.motionapp.com/creative/mechanics/); [Motion 2026 Bootcamp homework week 3](https://motionapp.com/events/2026-creative-strategy-bootcamp/homebase/homework-week-3); [Motion visual-formats skill mirror](https://skillselion.com/skills/motion-team/creative-strategy-skills/visual-formats)
- Motion's open-source "Creative Strategy Engine" uses pain or desire as the primary anchor, maps persona and awareness stage on top, and includes a review-mining step extracting pain points, objections and swipeable phrases. — [Motion Creative Strategy Engine](https://go.motionapp.com/creative-strategy-engine); [mirror](https://skillselion.com/skills/motion-team/creative-strategy-skills/creative-strategy-engine)
- Motion uses Schwartz's five customer-awareness stages for advertising and flags the common gap of over-investing in bottom-funnel creative. — [Motion, five customer awareness stages](https://motionapp.com/blog/five-customer-awareness-stages-advertising)

**Workflow, roles, cadence**
- Motion 2024: creative strategist's cycle = research, ideation, briefing (strategic + production brief), content creation, evaluation, launch, creative analysis. — [Motion talk 2024](https://motionapp.com/library/talk/a-day-in-the-life-of-a-creative-strategist-2024/)
- Motion 2026 production-system guide: three core hires, creative strategist central (analyses performance, writes scripts/shot lists, reviews footage before editors); weekly performance review feeds the next creative sprint. — [Motion, high-volume ad production system 2026](https://motionapp.com/blog/how-to-build-a-high-volume-ad-production-system-for-meta-and-tiktok-in-2026)
- Motion meeting templates: monthly creative review (plan next month's strategy, assign launch roles, set goals) and a sprint agenda (research → execution → review → client submission → launch with deadlines for new creative and tests). — [Motion, 3 growth marketing meeting templates](https://motionapp.com/blog/3-meetings-growth-marketing-agenda-templates)
- Motion workflow article for Facebook/TikTok performance creative. — [Motion, performance creative workflow](https://motionapp.com/blog/how-to-build-a-performance-creative-workflow-for-facebook-tiktok-ads)
- Independent 2026 review: Motion stops at analysis — briefs and learnings must be handed to production manually (i.e., the learning→brief handoff is the usual gap). — [adlibrary.com Motion review 2026](https://adlibrary.com/posts/motion-app-review-2026)

**Naming conventions**
- Motion recommends structured ad naming so creative attributes can be grouped in analytics. — [Motion, Facebook naming conventions](https://www.motionapp.com/blog/facebook-naming-conventions)
- A 4-layer naming model (asset file, ad, ad set, campaign) with a rollout process; ad-set names capture testing dimensions (audience type, bid, budget group). — [AdManage, ad creative naming conventions](https://admanage.ai/blog/ad-creative-naming-conventions); [Segwise naming guide 2026](https://segwise.ai/blog/ideal-campaign-naming-convention-ad-analysis)

**Creative analytics**
- Hook (thumbstop) rate = 3-second video plays ÷ impressions; hold rate = 15-second plays ÷ 3-second plays (some teams use ThruPlays ÷ 3s plays). — [Motion, key creative performance metrics](https://motionapp.com/blog/key-creative-performance-metrics); [Segwise](https://segwise.ai/blog/creative-metrics-predict-roas)
- Benchmarks are third-party and conflict: "good" hook rate quoted as 18–28% (median ~23%) for cold in-feed prospecting by one source vs 30–40% good / 40%+ elite by another; hold rate averages ~40–50%, under 30% weak. Placement (Reels vs feed, cold vs warm) changes baselines; compare within account and within test. — [Jetfuel 2026 benchmarks](https://jetfuel.agency/creative-performance-metrics-for-dtc-video-ads-hold-rate-thumbstop-and-thruplay-benchmarks-2026/); [AdManage, good hook rate](https://admanage.ai/blog/what-is-a-good-hook-rate-for-facebook-ads); [Hawky](https://hawky.ai/blog/hook-rate)
- Motion publishes 2026 creative benchmarks via its bootcamp. — [Motion 2026 Creative Benchmarks](https://motionapp.com/events/2026-creative-strategy-bootcamp/homebase/2026-creative-benchmarks)
- Dara Denney (2026 talk) on the biggest creative-analysis mistake. — [Motion talk](https://motionapp.com/library/talk/dara-denney-reveals-the-biggest-creative-analysis-mistake-in-2026-most/) (content not retrieved)

**Andromeda / creative diversity (2025–2026)**
- CTC (Common Thread Collective, ~170 ecommerce brands managed) argues Andromeda didn't "break" Meta; it revealed brands never had a creative strategy, and the system now requires creative diversity at a scale most brands haven't invested in (opinion/positioning piece). — [CTC Coach's Corner, Andromeda 2026](https://commonthreadco.com/blogs/coachs-corner/meta-andromeda-roas-creative-strategy-2026)
- Andromeda is described as Meta's retrieval stage narrowing millions of ads to a few thousand candidates; near-duplicate creatives (same shot, different headline/background) are grouped as one ad, so superficial variants don't buy extra delivery. — [Convert.com](https://www.convert.com/blog/growth-marketing/meta-andromeda-cro-guide/); [1800DTC agency insights](https://1800dtc.com/reads/agency-insights-what-metas-andromeda-update-actually-changed-and-how-were-adapting-our-ads); [Pathlabs](https://pathlabs.com/blog/meta-andromeda-ai-advertising-performance)
- Motion bootcamp panel (recorded Jun 2026): pre-Andromeda = iterate a winner with small changes then expand; post-Andromeda = iterations need larger changes, expanding into new personas and pain points; presents a seven-step "Creative Scaling Engine" from winner to refresh. — [Motion talk, "Scaling UGC ads post-Andromeda: one winner, many formats"](https://motionapp.com/library/talk/scaling-ugc-ads-post-andromeda-one-winner-many-formats/)
- P.D.A. (Persona, Desire, A[wareness]) is cited as a framework for building conceptually different ads. — [nikhil.pro, What is Meta Andromeda](https://nikhil.pro/meta-andromeda/)
- Caveat: one source says Andromeda's effects are hard to isolate and verified cases are limited. — [nikhil.pro](https://nikhil.pro/meta-andromeda/)

### Inferences
- Working definitions for an AI studio (synthesised from Motion's stack + Andromeda guidance):
  - **Concept** = one angle × one mechanic, expressed as a one-line idea; new concept = new angle or new persona/pain or new mechanic.
  - **Angle** = the reason-to-care (pain, desire, objection, proof) in one phrase.
  - **Hook** = first 1–3 seconds/headline executing the angle (visual + text + VO).
  - **Format** = container (static, UGC talking head, demo, founder story, carousel, meme, testimonial montage).
  - **Iteration** = same concept, meaningfully changed element (new hook set, new creator, new format).
  - **Variant** = cosmetic change (headline, colour, CTA) — post-Andromeda likely treated as the same ad; low value for testing, fine for fatigue.
- Naming template proposal (inferred from 4-layer naming advice): `{date}_{conceptID}_{angle}_{persona}_{awareness}_{format}_{hookID}_{creator}_{v#}` so every analytics view can pivot by layer.
- Matrix artifact: rows = personas × awareness stages; columns = angles; each cell can hold multiple hook × format executions. Diversity check = count of distinct cells covered per month, not number of ads.

## 4. Iteration loops, loser diagnosis and learning libraries

### Takeaway
The dominant (opinion) practice is to keep most production on net-new concepts and a minority on iterating winners, iterate by changing one meaningful variable (new hooks on a winning body, new persona on a winning angle, new format/creator), and test new against new. Metric diagnosis follows the funnel: weak hook rate = opening problem; good hook/weak hold = body problem; good hold/weak CTR or CVR = offer/CTA/landing problem.

### Cited Findings
- Dara Denney's reported split: ~90% net-new creative, ~10% iteration on winners; Barry Hott cites it to warn against "Iteration Paralysis" (teams recycling winning formulas until they stop winning). Secondary/social source, Jan 2025. — [Barry Hott on X](https://x.com/binghott/status/1877015320327487900)
- Test new creative against new creative, not against seasoned winners (winners carry delivery history that skews results) — attributed to Denney via Motion by a secondary source. — [Thrive Agency, creative flywheel](https://thrive-agency.co/blog/creative-flywheel-ad-diversity/)
- Iterate by changing one thing: new hooks on proven bodies; new personas on proven angles; ≤2 variations per concept (practitioner opinion). — [Thrive Agency](https://thrive-agency.co/blog/creative-flywheel-ad-diversity/)
- Hook-first testing: one body + 3–4 structurally different hooks, body and CTA fixed; build a reusable hook library. — [Scrolling Media, "Stop testing ads. Start testing hooks"](https://www.scrolling.media/post/stop-testing-ads-start-testing-hooks)
- Test one element at a time (hook, visual, CTA) to attribute cause; modular hooks/bodies/CTAs recombined to raise volume; loop = hypothesis → production → launch → monitoring → analysis → application. — [Segwise testing framework](https://segwise.ai/blog/facebook-ad-creative-testing-framework); [Benly](https://benly.ai/learn/ad-creative/creative-testing-framework)
- Barry Hott's "ugly ads" thesis: overly polished creative tends to underperform on paid social (opinion, self-reported results). — [Dara Denney LinkedIn, "Make ugly ads with Barry Hott"](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/daradenney_meta-ads-creative-hack-make-ugly-ads-with-activity-7142923930847100928-Nrbc)
- Post-Andromeda, iterations must be bigger (new personas/pain points), per Motion panel (Jun 2026). — [Motion talk](https://motionapp.com/library/talk/scaling-ugc-ads-post-andromeda-one-winner-many-formats/)
- Volume heuristics in circulation ("8–20 concepts", "2 concept tests and 6–12 ads/week at $30–100K/month") are practitioner inference, not platform guidance. — [The Performers, ultimate guide to creative testing](https://www.blog.theperformers.io/p/ultimate-ultimate-guide-creative-testing); [Thrive Agency](https://thrive-agency.co/blog/creative-flywheel-ad-diversity/)
- Refresh creatives roughly every 2–4 weeks or when frequency exceeds ~3–4 and performance drops. — [Saurav Does Marketing](https://www.sauravdoesmarketing.com/post/how-many-ad-creatives-do-you-actually-need-for-your-meta-ads-budget)

### Inferences
- Loser-diagnosis decision tree for the studio (inferred from metric definitions): hook rate below account median → rewrite hook/first frame only; hook OK, hold rate < ~30% → body/pacing/mechanic problem; hold OK, CTR low → CTA/offer clarity; CTR OK, CVR low → landing page/offer mismatch, not creative. Only kill the *angle* when 2–3 differently-hooked executions of it all fail.
- Learning library artifact: one row per tested concept with hypothesis (angle/persona/awareness/mechanic), the naming-convention ID, spend, hook/hold/CTR/CPA, verdict (win/lose/inconclusive), diagnosed failure layer, and "next action". The 2026 Motion review notes this learning→brief handoff is usually manual — an obvious place for an AI studio to automate.

### Gaps
- No primary agency example of a creative learning library (template or sample entries) was found.
- Primary sources for Denney's 90/10 and the "test new vs new" rule were not retrieved (social/secondary only).

## 5. Review and quality gates: critique checklists; great vs competent

### Takeaway
No agency CD checklist was retrieved directly, but a defensible gate stack can be assembled from published criteria: brief-fit (single-minded proposition, Hegarty test), idea quality (D&AD "inspiring / brilliantly executed / fit for purpose"), line-level copy (Harry Dry's visualise/falsify/only-we), brand linkage (Romaniuk distinctive assets), and pre-launch performance hygiene (hook in first seconds, angle stateable in one phrase).

### Cited Findings
- D&AD's three questions (inspiring idea; brilliant execution; fit for purpose) separate "great" (Yellow/Black) from "competent" (Graphite: "a good idea, well executed, relevant to the brief"). — [D&AD 2026 Entry Kit](https://media.dandad.org/documents/Entry_Kit_2026_ENG.pdf); [D&AD New Blood](https://www.dandad.org/insights/awards/what-winning-a-new-blood-awards-pencil-means-new-blood-advice)
- Cannes Creative Strategy weights insight/breakthrough thinking at 30% — i.e., "great" requires a non-obvious insight, not just craft. — [Cannes Lions Creative Strategy](https://www.canneslions.com/awards/lions/creative-strategy/what-you-need-to-know)
- Harry Dry's three rules presented by some sources as a copy review checklist. — [Upgrow](https://upgrow.io/blog/harry-dry-copywriting-3-rules)
- Motion's 2026 model has the creative strategist review footage before it reaches editors (a production QA gate). — [Motion production system 2026](https://motionapp.com/blog/how-to-build-a-high-volume-ad-production-system-for-meta-and-tiktok-in-2026)
- Angle must be stateable in one phrase and reusable across ads (a strategy gate). — [Motion bootcamp week 3](https://motionapp.com/events/2026-creative-strategy-bootcamp/homebase/homework-week-3)

### Inferences
- Proposed gate sequence for an AI studio: G0 Brief gate (one audience, one proposition passing Hegarty test, success metric defined) → G1 Concept gate (angle in one phrase; idea survives format change; insight is non-obvious; differs from last 10 tested concepts on ≥1 of persona/angle/mechanic) → G2 Script/copy gate (Harry Dry ×3; hook lands in ≤3s; one message) → G3 Craft gate (distinctive asset visible early; native to placement; legible on mute) → G4 Post-launch gate (hook/hold/CTR vs account median; log learning).
- "Competent" = clear, on-brief, well-made; "great" = adds a non-obvious insight plus an unmistakably ownable execution (fails the "could a competitor run this?" test).

## 6. Adapting for small service businesses (one founder, ~$300–$3,000/month)

### Takeaway
At this scale statistical creative testing is mostly infeasible; the sources converge on 2–3 ads live at a time, 7–14 day test windows, ~20% of budget for testing via Meta's tool, refreshing every 2–4 weeks, and judging on upstream metrics (hook/hold/CTR, cost per lead) because ~50 conversion events per test is rarely reachable.

### Cited Findings
- Under $100/day: 2–3 creatives, 7–14 days, "test one concept slowly". — [AdManage, how many ad creatives to test](https://admanage.ai/blog/how-many-ad-creatives-to-test)
- Budget decides count; ~3 ads is a floor; overloading an ad set (e.g., 15 ads funded for 5) "destroys your test signal". — [Flighted, ads per ad set 2026](https://www.flighted.co/blog/how-many-ads-should-you-put-in-a-meta-ad-set); [Blip](https://withblip.com/blog/how-many-ads-to-run-meta-campaign/)
- Small-account rule of thumb: roughly one new ad per $1,000 monthly spend (practitioner heuristic). — [Saurav Does Marketing](https://www.sauravdoesmarketing.com/post/how-many-ad-creatives-do-you-actually-need-for-your-meta-ads-budget)
- Meta's built-in creative testing tool supports 2–5 test ads; Meta suggests ≤20% of budget on the test. — [Jon Loomer, Meta creative testing tool](https://www.jonloomer.com/meta-creative-testing/)
- Budget guidance for testing Meta creatives. — [Starpop](https://starpop.ai/blog/articles/how-much-budget-to-test-meta-ad-creatives); [Adrio](https://adrio.ai/blog/how-many-ad-creatives-test-meta)
- ~50 optimisation events cited as a decision threshold (hard to reach at small budgets). — [AdManage](https://admanage.ai/blog/how-many-ad-creatives-to-test)
- Small brands should pick high-uniqueness brand assets and repeat them rather than change them. — [nineblaess](https://www.nineblaess.de/blog/distinctive-brand-assets/)

### Inferences
- Minimum viable creative OS for a solo service business: monthly cycle, not weekly. Each month: 1 strategic brief → 2–3 genuinely different concepts (different angle or persona, not variants) → 1 format each, 2–3 hooks on the strongest → run 10–14 days → judge on hook rate, hold rate, CTR, cost per lead vs own baseline → keep 1 winner, log learnings → next month: new hooks/format on winner + 1–2 new concepts. Roughly 60–70% new / 30–40% iteration is likely more realistic than 90/10 at tiny volume (inference, no source).
- Research substitutes for big-agency inputs: Google reviews of the business and competitors, Reddit/local Facebook groups, Meta Ad Library of local competitors, and the founder's own sales-call objections; feed into the persona × awareness × angle matrix.
- Founder-on-camera "ugly" formats (Hott) are cheap and native; the AI studio's leverage is in research, concept variety and hook writing, not polish.

### Gaps
- No source specific to local service businesses (plumbers, clinics, consultants) with verified test outcomes at <$3K/month was found; all small-budget guidance is generic ecommerce/practitioner heuristics.
- No reliable data on minimum impressions needed for stable hook-rate readings at small budgets.
