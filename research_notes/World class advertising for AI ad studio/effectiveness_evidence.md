## 1. Binet & Field: brand/activation split, emotion, fame, small brands, B2B, services

### Takeaway
The 60/40 brand-to-activation rule is an average optimum from the UK-heavy IPA Databank, not a law. It moves with context: about 46/54 for B2B, about 80/20 for financial services, about 51/49 for non-financial services, and lowest for "perishable" services such as travel. The IPA data also say that bigger brands get more out of advertising, and new brands grow fastest from a low base. No source found gives a split specific to small businesses. Small local service firms sit far outside the databank's sample.

### Cited Findings
- **Origin of 60/40.** *The Long and the Short of It* (IPA, 2013) analysed 996 IPA effectiveness case studies. The best results came at roughly 60% brand building and 40% sales activation. The benchmark comes from a non-random, UK-heavy archive. — [growthmethod.com summary](https://growthmethod.com/long-and-short/); [deepmarketing.it](https://www.deepmarketing.it/en/blog/60-40-rule-budget-brand-performance-2026); [boringlyeffective.com review](https://boringlyeffective.com/long-and-short-of-it-review/)
- **Refined optimum.** *Effectiveness in Context* (IPA, 2018) reportedly refined the overall optimum to 62/38. It said the ratio varies with industry sector, purchase model, product innovation, category life-stage and brand size. Secondary sources only. — [Campaign: "Binet and Field reveal key formulas for brand-building"](https://www.campaignlive.co.uk/article/binet-field-reveal-key-formulas-brand-building/1495641); [Pushers framework page](https://wepushers.com/frameworks/long-and-short-of-it/)
- **Category optima.** Optimum splits run from 80:20 (brand:activation) in financial services to 51:49 in non-financial services. The brand share is lowest in "perishable services" such as travel. The reported actual average for financial services in 2014–2016 was 54:46, about 26 points short of its optimum. Source is an unofficial Scribd copy of the report slides plus secondary summaries. — [Scribd copy of Effectiveness in Context](https://www.scribd.com/document/678740533/Effectiveness-in-Context); [System1 blog "Saved by the Baby"](https://system1group.com/blog/saved-the-baby-binet-and-field-on-effectiveness-in-context)
- **Online research.** The more online research consumers do in a category, the more important it is to invest in a strong brand. — [Pushers summary](https://wepushers.com/frameworks/long-and-short-of-it/)
- **Brand size and returns.** "The bigger a brand's market share, the more efficiently advertising works." Market leaders earn the highest ROI and return on sales: about 6x No. 2 brands in the UK and 4x in the US. No. 3 and No. 4 brands are often unprofitable. Market leaders also find it easier to use advertising to reduce price sensitivity. Secondary summary. — [Campaign](https://www.campaignlive.co.uk/article/binet-field-reveal-key-formulas-brand-building/1495641); [Jesper Åström LinkedIn analysis](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/making-sense-les-binet-peter-fields-strange-jesper-%C3%A5str%C3%B6m)
- **New brands.** A table in the Scribd copy gives base rates of market-share growth at zero excess share of voice: 3.3% for new brands, 0.7% for medium brands and 2.6% for large brands. In other words, new brands grow from novelty even before outspending anyone. Unverified against the official IPA text. — [Scribd copy](https://www.scribd.com/document/678740533/Effectiveness-in-Context)
- **Excess share of voice (ESOV).** About 0.5 percentage points of annual market-share growth per 10 points of ESOV (share of voice minus share of market). Nielsen describes the underlying sample as 171 campaigns, 1980–2010. Later sources give 0.5–0.7 depending on category, and about 0.7 for B2B. — [Nielsen, "What is share of voice" (2025)](https://www.nielsen.com/insights/2025/what-is-share-voice/); [inversion.agency ESOV explainer](https://inversion.agency/articles/esov)
- **Small brands and share of voice.** Earlier John Philip Jones work, as cited by Ritson, found that small brands must run a share of voice above their share of market. Large brands can under-spend relative to share and still hold position. — [Marketing Week, Mark Ritson on share of search](https://www.marketingweek.com/mark-ritson-share-of-search-share-of-voice/)
- **B2B: 5 Principles of Growth in B2B Marketing (2019).** LinkedIn B2B Institute with Binet & Field, using the IPA Databank (B2B cases). **Industry-funded: LinkedIn sells B2B media.** Five principles:
  1. Share of voice above share of market drives growth.
  2. Balance brand and activation. B2B optimum about 46% brand / 54% activation. LinkedIn's own summary page rounds this to about 50/50.
  3. Acquisition (broad reach) beats loyalty targeting.
  4. Aim for mental availability and fame.
  5. Emotional messaging works better long term; rational messaging works better short term.
  — [LinkedIn B2B Institute page](https://business.linkedin.com/advertise/resources/b2b-institute/marketing-as-growth); [Alex Murrell summary](https://www.alexmurrell.co.uk/summaries/the-b2b-institute-the-5-principles-of-growth-in-b2b-marketing); [ISB blog on the B2B research](https://blogs.isb.edu/cbm/2019/11/25/breaking-new-ground-with-binet-and-fields-latest-b2b-research/); [The Drum exclusive](https://www.thedrum.com/opinion/exclusive-look-binet-and-field-s-new-b2b-marketing-research)
- **B2B sample.** A related B2B Institute/IPA release refers to almost 1,500 campaigns collected since 2006. Sources are inconsistent about whether this is the B2B subset or the whole databank. — [IPA EffWorks page](https://ipa.co.uk/effworks/marketing-marketing-v2/five-principles-of-growth-in-b2b-marketing)
- **Conflicting B2B split.** Sources disagree on the exact B2B figure: 46/54, a 46–50/50–54 range, or "near 50:50". At least one blog reverses the order. — [The Drum](https://www.thedrum.com/opinion/exclusive-look-binet-and-field-s-new-b2b-marketing-research); [razorsharppr.com](https://www.razorsharppr.com/blog/3-things-binet-and-field-can-teach-b2b-marketers)
- **Emotion in B2B.** Binet & Field reportedly found emotion "almost as important in B2B buying as in B2C". Critics note the framework rests on B2C-heavy data, and that rational, education-led content may outperform in highly technical markets. — [customerthink.com](https://customerthink.com/unconventional-views-on-b2b-growth/); [B2B Marketing Directions](https://b2bmarketingdirections.blogspot.com/2020/02/unconventional-views-on-b2b-growth.html)
- **95:5 rule.** John Dawes (Ehrenberg-Bass) for the B2B Institute, 2021: only about 5% of B2B buyers are in market at any time. This is the argument for reaching "out-market" buyers with brand advertising. **LinkedIn-funded.** — [LinkedIn B2B Institute](https://business.linkedin.com/advertise/resources/b2b-institute/marketing-as-growth)
- **Binet on the split itself.** Binet has argued that brand and activation is partly a false dichotomy: good activation should also carry brand-building cues. — [Les Binet LinkedIn post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/les-binet-9bb7453_brand-vs-activation-a-false-dichotomy-activity-7022177396585832449-kbtU)

### Inferences
- A small service business typically has tiny share of market, short-term cash pressure and local reach. Two pressures pull in opposite directions:
  - The IPA data say small brands get weaker returns per dollar.
  - The IPA data also say small brands need share of voice above share of market to grow.
  - The practical rule is to make every activation ad also carry brand cues: a consistent name, distinctive assets, emotional tone. Do not try to fund a separate 60% brand budget.
- For services, the evidence spans 80:20 (financial services) to about 51:49 (non-financial services). Home and local services are probably nearer the activation-heavy end than the 60/40 headline. Trust-heavy services such as legal, financial and medical lean more toward brand.
- In local markets a small firm can plausibly reach a high share of voice cheaply. ESOV logic may apply at a geographic level, but this is not tested in the data.

### Gaps
- Could not access *The Long and the Short of It*, *Effectiveness in Context* or *Media in Focus* primary text to verify these figures:
  - emotional vs rational efficiency multipliers (commonly cited as emotional campaigns being about 2x as likely to report very large profit gains)
  - fame being the most efficient campaign type
  - short-term activation effects peaking within about 6 months
  These are widely repeated but **not verified in this session**.
- No Binet & Field analysis found on businesses with under $1M revenue or very small budgets. The IPA Databank is dominated by large, mostly UK advertisers with budgets far above small-business scale.
- No primary source found for a "small brand" or "new brand" specific brand/activation ratio. The reverse claim (that launches need more activation) circulates in blogs without a citation.
- The 2023 *Long and the Short of It* follow-ups and Binet's 2024–25 work on digital-native brands were not retrieved.

## 2. IPA Databank: creativity and effectiveness ("The Crisis in Creative Effectiveness")

### Takeaway
Creatively awarded campaigns were historically about 12x as efficient as non-awarded ones (1996–2008). By 2006–2018 that advantage had fallen below 4x, and on the latest data awarded work was no more effective than non-awarded work. Field attributes the decline to short-termism: short, activation-focused campaigns that waste creativity.

### Cited Findings
- **Report scope.** *The Crisis in Creative Effectiveness*, Peter Field for the IPA, launched at Cannes Lions on 19 June 2019. It covers almost 600 IPA case studies (about 1996/98–2018), cross-referenced with Gunn Report creative awards data. — [IPA publication page](https://ipa.co.uk/knowledge/publications-reports/the-crisis-in-creative-effectiveness); [IPA PDF](https://ipa.co.uk/media/7699/ipa_crisis_in_creative_effectiveness_2019.pdf); [InPublishing](https://www.inpublishing.co.uk/articles/new-ipa-report-reveals-crisis-in-creative-effectiveness-14597)
- **Efficiency multiplier.** Creatively awarded campaigns were about 12x as efficient as non-awarded ones in 1996–2008. This fell below 4x in 2006–2018. Awarded campaigns are now "less effective than they have ever been in the 24-year run of data" and "no more effective than non-awarded campaigns". Consistent across sources. — [Contagious](https://www.contagious.com/news-and-views/creative-effectiveness-peter-field-IPA-crisis-report); [Marketing Week](https://www.marketingweek.com/crisis-creative-effectiveness/); [WARC](https://www.warc.com/newsandopinion/opinion/the-crisis-in-creative-effectiveness/en-gb/3122)
- **Cause: short-termism.** The predecessor report, *Selling Creativity Short* (IPA, 2016), warned of the same risk. — [IPA, Selling Creativity Short](https://ipa.co.uk/knowledge/publications-reports/selling-creativity-short-creativity-and-effectiveness-under-threat)
- **Short-term awarded campaigns.** Said to be about 50% less effective than long-term awarded campaigns, and about 25% less effective than non-awarded work. This is a third-party summary only; treat with caution. — [inversion.agency](https://inversion.agency/articles/creative-effectiveness-crisis)
- **Kantar/WARC 2023.** About 450 ads from Kantar's Link pre-test database matched with WARC ROI data. The most creative and effective ads produced more than 4x the profit. Kantar ranks creative quality as the second most important profit driver after brand size. **Industry-funded (Kantar sells creative testing).** — [Kantar, "The art of proof"](https://www.kantar.com/inspiration/advertising-media/the-art-of-proof-how-creative-quality-drives-profit); [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/industry/news/creativity-engaging-stories-drive-brand-sales-says-kantar-report-123062100477_1.html)
- **Kantar India 2025.** Strong creative delivered 36% higher profitability than weak creative. **Industry-funded.** — [exchange4media](https://www.exchange4media.com/marketing-news/creative-quality-drives-36-higher-profitability-kantar-report-145217.html)

### Inferences
- Creativity pays only when it runs long and consistently enough to build memory. For an AI system, this argues for producing one consistent creative platform with variations, not a stream of unrelated "fresh" ads.

### Gaps
- Could not retrieve the PDF's internal tables: campaign-duration effects, fame vs other effect types, and channel breakdowns.
- No small-business or local-advertiser cases identified in the databank.

## 3. Creative quality as the largest sales driver (Nielsen / NCSolutions / Kantar / Google)

### Takeaway
The most-cited figure is that creative accounts for about 47–49% of the incremental sales driven by advertising. It comes from Nielsen and Nielsen Catalina Solutions (now NCSolutions) analyses of about 450–500 US CPG campaigns, in 2017 and again in 2023. Marketers think creative is only about 19% of the effect. The finding is CPG and US-specific, and the measurement firms that produced it sell creative and media measurement.

### Cited Findings
- **2017 Nielsen/NCS analysis.** About 500 CPG campaigns run in 2016–Q1 2017. Creative = 47% of sales contribution from advertising, plus 2% for context, giving roughly 49%. **Industry-funded/measurement vendor.** — [Shno ad creative statistics compilation](https://www.shno.co/marketing-statistics/ad-creative-performance-statistics); [WARC, Creative Quality Score](https://www.warc.com/content/article/warc-exclusive/why-your-next-campaign-should-have-a-creative-quality-score-according-to-research-on-1m-ads/140416)
- **2023 update (NCSolutions, with Nielsen).** Nearly 450 sales-effect studies; one write-up says about 500 CPG campaigns. Contribution to sales lift:

  | Driver | Share of sales lift |
  |---|---|
  | Creative | 49% |
  | Brand | 21% |
  | Reach | 14% |
  | Targeting | 11% |
  | Recency | 5% |

  The MarketingCharts headline notes that media's influence is growing over time. **Industry-funded.** Sample size differs by source. — [MarketingCharts](https://www.marketingcharts.com/television-80662); [Inside Radio/Westwood One](https://www.insideradio.com/free/what-drives-advertising-effectiveness-its-not-what-most-marketers-think/article_8eaae06a-012f-11ef-90e4-5b800fba3731.html)
- **Marketer perception.** Advertiser Perceptions survey, February 2024: brands and agencies believe creative accounts for only 19% of sales effect. — [Inside Radio](https://www.insideradio.com/free/what-drives-advertising-effectiveness-its-not-what-most-marketers-think/article_8eaae06a-012f-11ef-90e4-5b800fba3731.html)
- **Separate NCSolutions creator-content study.** May 2024: creative is nearly 50% of sales lift. This is a different study from the above. **Industry-funded.** — [NetInfluencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/ncsolutions-creative-content-advertising-effectiveness-research/)
- **Google/Kantar/Marketing Week 2025.** 8 in 10 marketers see creative quality as a key driver, but fewer than half measure it. **Industry-funded (Google).** — [Think with Google](https://business.google.com/us/think/measurement/creative-measurement-gap-ai-google-kantar/)
- **Kantar validation of Google's ABCD framework.** ABCD = Attract, Brand, Connect, Direct for YouTube. Kantar used AI to validate it. Specific sales-lift numbers were not retrieved. **Industry-funded.** — [Kantar](https://www.kantar.com/industries/technology-and-telecoms/validating-googles-abcd-framework-with-the-power-of-artificial-intelligence)
- **"56%" digital-creative figure.** Could not be confirmed. One article attributes 56% to a MAGNA purchase-intent study and 70% to Google, but those are different metrics from sales contribution. — [Advertising Week](https://advertisingweek.com/filling-the-creative-data-gap-to-counter-rising-cpms/)

### Inferences
- Creative is the largest single lever a small advertiser controls. Its targeting and reach are constrained by budget anyway, so creative quality gating is the right place for an AI system to invest.
- The "Brand" share (21%) reflects existing brand equity, which small firms lack. Creative may matter even more for them, but this is not tested.

### Gaps
- No primary Nielsen or NCSolutions PDF retrieved. Methodology appears to be matched-panel purchase data (test vs control households) from NCS sales-effect studies, but this is not confirmed.
- No equivalent decomposition found for services, local, or B2B advertisers.

## 4. Ehrenberg-Bass: mental availability, distinctive brand assets, category entry points

### Takeaway
Ehrenberg-Bass argues that brands grow mainly by reaching more category buyers, especially light buyers, and by being easy to think of and easy to recognise. In practice this means:
- linking the brand to many category entry points (CEPs): the buying situations that bring a brand to mind
- using distinctive brand assets (DBAs) that are both famous and unique to the brand
For a small service business this argues for consistent assets and situation-based messaging. The evidence for these exact prescriptions at very small scale is thin.

### Cited Findings
- **Category entry points.** CEPs are the cues that bring a brand to mind in buying situations. They are identified by asking why, when, where, with whom and with what the purchase happens (*How Brands Grow Part 2*, Romaniuk & Sharp, 2015/2021). — [Sharley summary](https://sharley.substack.com/p/summary-how-brands-grow-part-2-by)
- **Distinctive assets.** Strong assets must be widely recognised (fame) and tightly linked to one brand (uniqueness). They need regular evaluation and maintenance (*Building Distinctive Brand Assets*, Romaniuk, OUP 2018). — [Sharley summary](https://sharley.substack.com/p/summary-how-brands-grow-part-2-by); [Marketing Architects](https://www.marketingarchitects.com/blog/v41-n3-your-brand-assets-may-be-working-against-you)
- **Fame comes first.** Without fame an asset may not register at all, so measurement is framed as fame plus uniqueness. — [Marketing Architects](https://www.marketingarchitects.com/blog/v41-n3-your-brand-assets-may-be-working-against-you)
- **Small brands and loyalty (Romaniuk).** Small brands should not expect the loyalty levels of big brands (the "double jeopardy" pattern). Light buyers are the major growth opportunity. — [Bigeye podcast with Jenni Romaniuk](https://www.bigeyeagency.com/podcast/better-brand-health-with-jenni-romaniuk)
- **Better Brand Health.** Romaniuk's book (OUP) on measuring mental availability. — [OUP](https://www.oup.com.au/general-interest/redirects/how-brands-grow2)
- **95:5 rule.** Dawes (Ehrenberg-Bass) for the LinkedIn B2B Institute: about 5% of B2B buyers are in market at any time. **LinkedIn-funded.** — [LinkedIn B2B Institute](https://business.linkedin.com/advertise/resources/b2b-institute/marketing-as-growth)

### Inferences
For small service businesses, the evidence implies these rules:
1. Keep the brand name, colour, logo, tagline and any character, voice or jingle consistent across every ad, so the assets can become famous over time.
2. Show the brand early and often.
3. Write ads around specific buying situations, for example:
   - "burst pipe at 10pm"
   - "selling your house this spring"
   - "new hire paperwork"
   This is in place of generic claims of quality.
4. Target the broad category, not just retargeted warm leads.

This is extrapolation. Ehrenberg-Bass evidence is drawn mainly from FMCG and large-brand panel data.

### Gaps
- The search returned secondary summaries, not peer-reviewed Ehrenberg-Bass papers. Quantitative DBA results were not retrieved, such as the Fame/Uniqueness grid thresholds or the share of ads correctly branded.
- No Ehrenberg-Bass study found specifically on small or local service firms.

## 5. System1 / Orlando Wood: right-brain vs left-brain features

### Takeaway
Wood's *Lemon* (IPA, 2019) and *Look Out* (IPA, 2021) argue that advertising has drifted toward "left-brain" features since the mid-2000s, and that "right-brain" features predict both attention and long-term market-share effects.
- **Left-brain features:** flat visuals, isolated products, on-screen text, voiceover, rhythmic or beat-driven sound.
- **Right-brain features:** living characters, dialogue, a sense of place, implicit cues such as glances and expressions, melody, humour, story.
The evidence is real but largely produced by System1, which sells this testing.

### Cited Findings
- **Feature lists (Lemon, 2019).** Left-brain: flat visuals, isolated features, on-screen text, voiceovers, rhythmic or repetitive soundtracks. Right-brain: whole scenes, characters, unspoken cues such as facial expressions, dialogue, a sense of place, humour, melodic music. Wood ties these to "betweenness", meaning interaction between characters. — [Alex Murrell summary](https://www.alexmurrell.co.uk/summaries/orlando-wood-lemon); [RTÉ Media Sales review](https://mediasales.rte.ie/news/lemon-by-orlando-wood-review/); [System1 Lemon preview](https://system1group.com/blog/lemon-preview)
- **Lemon sample.** A random sample of 100 UK and 100 US TV ads from System1's Ad Ratings database, scored on System1's 1–5 Star Rating, which System1 says predicts market-share growth. 5% of the ads with the most right-brain features achieved 5 stars, against 0% of those with the fewest. US ads averaged 3.4 left-brain features vs 2.6 right-brain features. **Industry-funded (System1).** — [System1 IPA "A Slice of Lemon" deck](https://maynardpaton.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/12/SYS1-A-Slice-Of-Lemon-Presentation-IPA.pdf); [Contagious](https://www.contagious.com/en/article/news-and-views/why-left-brain-thinking-is-killing-advertising-effectiveness); [LBB Online](https://lbbonline.com/news/are-ads-becoming-too-far-left-brain)
- **Look Out (IPA, 2021).** Right-brain features are strongest at grabbing attention and provoking emotion. Supported by a correlation analysis of 195 US TV ads combining System1 star ratings with TVision attention data. Right-brain features map to broad, long-lasting business effects; left-brain features map to direct, short-term effects. **System1-produced.** — [System1 Look Out press release](https://system1group.com/blog/look-out-press-release); [Screenforce "A Glimpse Inside" PDF](https://screenforce.nl/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/20220519-pdf-System1_Look-out_A-Glimpse-Inside-Orlando-Wood.pdf); [thinkTV "A Glimpse of Look Out" PDF](https://thinktv.ca/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/A-Glimpse-of-Look-Out-egta_Orlando_Wood.pdf)
- **Left-brain ads still have a role.** They can drive direct effects in targeted campaigns. Right-brain features serve long-term market-share gains. — [System1 "Three Keys That Unlock Attention"](https://system1group.com/blog/achtung-3-keys)
- **IPA online video study.** Wood with Peter Field, IPA Databank online-video campaigns 2016–2020, **in partnership with Facebook (industry-funded)**. Online video with human and unexpected (right-brain) features was more likely to generate market share, profit and sales gains than instructive, mechanistic (left-brain) video. Right-brain features also drew more attention; left-brain features disengaged viewers. — [IPA news release](https://ipa.co.uk/news/new-research-reveals-key-to-growing-market-share-with-online-video)
- **Radio/audio.** The Radiocentre "Listen Up!" report (2023) extends the emotion-effectiveness link to audio ads. **Industry-funded (radio trade body).** — [Radiocentre PDF](https://www.radiocentre.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Listen-Up-FINAL.pdf)
- **Criticism.** The hemispheric framing (from Iain McGilchrist) is criticised as pop neuroscience. Supporters reply that it describes attention styles, not literal brain halves. Reviewers note "it's not so simple as left-brain bad, right-brain good". No independent peer-reviewed replication was found. — [directnewideas.com](https://www.directnewideas.com/magazine/watch_out_advertising_is_becoming_increasingly_left_brained_/?id=3409); [LBB "Are we over-analysing advertising?"](https://lbbonline.com/news/are-we-over-analysing-advertising)

### Inferences
A creative checklist for AI-generated video and image ads could favour:
- a recognisable human or character, ideally the owner or a real customer
- spoken dialogue between people, not a voiceover over product shots
- a specific, real place, such as the local street or the job site
- a small story with a beat of humour or warmth
- melody over a pounding beat
- minimal on-screen text

The checklist could penalise slogan-heavy, text-over-product, fast-cut "salesy" executions, except in pure retargeting or direct-response slots.

### Gaps
- Could not retrieve the specific *Lemon* cross-tab linking IPA Databank "very large effects" to feature counts, or the longitudinal trend chart (the shift is said to start in the mid-2000s).
- No evidence on whether these features hold for static social images or for very low-budget creative.

## 6. Attention, early branding, sound-on, emotion in short-form (Kantar / Meta / TikTok / System1)

### Takeaway
Platform-funded studies agree on four points:
- Most short-form ad impact happens in the first few seconds.
- Brand cues should appear in the first 2 seconds.
- Sound, especially sonic brand assets, lifts recall.
- Creator-style, human content holds attention better.
Nearly all of this evidence is funded by the platforms.

### Cited Findings
- **TikTok with Kantar.** About 3,500 ads in the US, Canada and UK, running in 2021 and published in 2022. About 90% of ad-recall impact falls in the first 6 seconds. The first 2 seconds carry the most value for ad recall, and the first 2.5 seconds for awareness. **TikTok-funded.** — [TikTok Creative Center, "Power of Creative Elements"](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/creativecenter/quicktok/online/Power_Creative_Elements/pc/en)
- **System1 with TikTok.** Effect on brand awareness:

  | Early element | Brand-awareness lift |
  |---|---|
  | Sonic assets in the first 2 seconds | +191% (largest) |
  | Logos in context | +182% |
  | Fluent characters | +57% |

  Early branding was also linked to a 48% reduction in negative sentiment, a 57% rise in happiness and a 19% drop in attention decay. Creator-led ads captured 39% more attention than traditional ads. **Platform-funded.** — [Chief Marketer](https://www.chiefmarketer.com/system1-tiktok-study-branding-elements-and-creator-led-content-improve-short-form-video-ads/)
- **Sound.** 73% of TikTok users say they "stop and look" at ads with sound (Kantar cross-platform study, 2021, cited by TikTok). This is a self-reported survey and **TikTok-funded**. — [TikTok, Evolution of Sound Vol. 2](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en/blog/evolution-of-sound-volume-2)
- **Kantar attention guidance.** Kantar measures attention with facial coding plus eye-tracking and separates active from passive attention. It advises that the opening seconds should set tone, build interest or signal the payoff. — [Kantar, Decoding Attention](https://www.kantar.com/inspiration/advertising-media/decoding-attention-five-lessons-to-transform-your-creative)
- **Look Out / TVision.** Right-brain features correlate with higher attention (195 US TV ads). See section 5. — [System1 press release](https://system1group.com/blog/look-out-press-release)

### Inferences
Rules for short-form video:
- Show the brand name, logo, owner's face or sonic cue within 2 seconds.
- Design for sound-on, but caption for sound-off.
- Use a human, creator-style format: the owner talking to camera or a customer telling their story.
- Hook with the situation (the CEP) in the first second.

Early branding should be woven into the content, not a static logo slate. System1 found "logo in context" effective.

### Gaps
- No Meta-specific Kantar study was retrieved, for example Meta's "thumb-stopping" or 1–2 second branding research, or the Meta/Nielsen sound-on studies.
- No independent (non-platform) replication of the first-2-seconds findings was found.

## 7. Testimonials, social proof, specificity and risk reversal (services/B2B)

### Takeaway
The best quantified social-proof evidence is from e-commerce reviews (Spiegel Research Center, 2017):
- Products with 5 reviews had 270% higher purchase likelihood than products with none.
- Returns diminish after about 5 reviews.
- The effect is larger for higher-priced items.
- Purchase likelihood peaks at 4.0–4.7 stars, not a perfect 5.0.
Evidence on money-back guarantees is mixed and mostly lab-based. Rigorous, published evidence specific to service-business ads was not found.

### Cited Findings
- **Spiegel Research Center, Northwestern Medill, 2017** (with PowerReviews data):
  - Purchase likelihood for a product with 5 reviews was 270% greater than for one with none.
  - Displaying reviews raised conversion by 190% for lower-priced products and 380% for higher-priced products.
  - Marginal benefit drops rapidly after the first 5 reviews.
  - Purchase likelihood peaks at average ratings of 4.0–4.7 and declines as ratings approach 5.0.

  Scope caveat: part of the data came from a single high-end gift retailer, with about 57,000 consumer reviews and 65,000 verified-buyer reviews covering about 13,500 products. The 270% figure is often overstated as "reviews raise sales 270%". **Vendor-supplied data (PowerReviews).** — [Spiegel Research Center](https://spiegel.medill.northwestern.edu/how-online-reviews-influence-sales/); [Spiegel eBook PDF](https://spiegel.medill.northwestern.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2021/04/Spiegel_Online-Review_eBook_Jun2017_FINAL.pdf); [MarketingSherpa chart](https://www.marketingsherpa.com/article/chart/university-study-online-reviews-affect-sales)
- **Money-back guarantees, German consumer-panel experiment.** Guarantees reduced performance risk, financial risk and anticipated regret. Strict vs lenient terms changed the effect. — [ResearchGate: Understanding Money-Back Guarantees](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/251479646_Understanding_Money-Back_Guarantees_Cognitive_Affective_and_Behavioral_Outcomes)
- **Guarantee credibility.** It depends on how generous the guarantee is, how familiar the store name is, and perceived price. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/286120810_Do_Consumers_Perceive_Money-Back_Guarantees_as_Believable_The_Effects_of_Money-Back_Guarantee_Generosity_Store_Name_Familiarity_and_Perceived_Price)
- **Null result on purchase intent.** Another study found that a guarantee marginally improved expected quality and reduced perceived risk, but had no effect on purchase intent. — [ScienceDirect, Journal of Business Research](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0148296313003603)
- **Profitability.** Retailers can profit from guarantees under specific conditions, and guarantees raise satisfaction (ESMT research summary). — [ESMT Berlin](https://esmt.berlin/knowledge/research-insights/economics-money-back-guarantee)

### Inferences
- For services, where quality cannot be inspected before purchase, reviews and testimonials plausibly matter even more than for products. The Spiegel finding that the effect is larger for higher-priced items supports this indirectly.
- Ads should show:
  - a review count and average rating, framed as credible rather than claimed as "perfect 5.0"
  - named, specific testimonials rather than generic praise
- Guarantees reduce perceived risk, but their effect on conversion depends on credibility. Specific, generous terms from a named local business are more believable than vague promises.

### Gaps
- No rigorous field experiment found on testimonials, numeric specificity (e.g., "312 roofs replaced" vs "many roofs"), or guarantees in **service-business advertising**.
- The classic specificity research on precise numbers and claims credibility (e.g., the Janiszewski & Uy precise-anchor work) was not retrieved.
- No B2B case-study or testimonial effectiveness data was found.

## 8. Frequency, wear-out and creative fatigue

### Takeaway
In traditional media, true creative wear-out is rarer than marketers assume: advertisers tire of ads before audiences do.
- Radio data showed 2 of 25,000 ads declining.
- An Analytic Partners analysis reportedly found almost all ads pulled before showing wear-out.
In digital direct response, Meta data show conversion likelihood falling about 45% by the 4th repeated exposure. Wear-out is heterogeneous across campaigns. Creative quality and creativity slow it.

### Cited Findings
- **ABX/RAB radio study (2025).** Only 2 of 25,000 AM/FM ads showed decline in some creative-effectiveness metrics, and both had high cumulative spend and long time in market. **Industry-funded (Radio Advertising Bureau).** — [Westwood One blog](https://www.westwoodone.com/blog/2025/03/31/abx-rab-radio-creative-wear-out-study-validates-mark-ritsons-conclusion-that-consumers-dont-get-tired-of-ads-only-marketers-do/)
- **ARF review.** True creative wear-out is less common than assumed. It occurs mainly with heavy short-term frequency or poor creative quality. — [ARF, "Do Ads Really Wear Out?"](https://thearf.org/category/ua_resource/do-ads-really-wear-out-evidence-and-implications-for-media/)
- **Analytic Partners (secondary, unverified).** Of more than 50,000 ads, only 14 ran their full course and showed wear-out when replaced. The other 51,232 were pulled without signs of fatigue. — [New Digital Age](https://newdigitalage.co/strategy/ad-fatigue-is-it-really-the-problem-you-think-it-is/)
- **Meta Analytics.** By the 4th repeated exposure, likelihood of conversion falls about 45%. There was no evidence of an early "wear-in" period for direct-response objectives. Brand objectives such as ad recall may behave differently. **Meta company blog.** — [Analytics at Meta on Medium](https://medium.com/@AnalyticsAtMeta/creative-fatigue-how-advertisers-can-improve-performance-by-managing-repeated-exposures-e76a0ea1084d)
- **Display ads, academic.** Wear-out on clicks is heterogeneous. 4 of 30 campaigns showed significant wear-out after 1–2 exposures, while 10 showed little even after 50 exposures. — [AEA 2015 conference paper](https://www.aeaweb.org/conference/2015/retrieve.php?pdfid=2969&tk=nRA8GNdB)
- **Creativity and wear-out, peer-reviewed.** Creative ads wear in faster and wear out more slowly than ordinary ads when repeated. — [ResearchGate: Advertising creativity and repetition](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/270126665_Advertising_creativity_and_repetition_Recall_wearout_and_wearin_effects)

### Inferences
- Do not rotate a whole creative platform just because it feels old. Keep the brand assets and core concept constant for long periods.
- Within paid social direct response, rotate *executions* (hooks, visuals, testimonials) under one consistent platform. Watch frequency per person: about 4 or more exposures to the same direct-response ad is where Meta data show steep conversion decline.
- Higher creative quality extends the useful life of an ad.

### Gaps
- No primary Kantar, System1 or Ehrenberg-Bass frequency or wear-out studies were retrieved. Ehrenberg-Bass generally argues reach matters more than frequency, but this is not verified here.
- No data found on wear-out in small local audiences, where a small addressable pool means frequency builds quickly. This is a key risk for small service businesses running geo-targeted social ads.
