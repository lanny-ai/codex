# Google Business Profile, local ranking and listings: research notes

Compiled 2026-10-28. Scope: what is true now, with emphasis on Nov 2025 to Oct 2026. Audience for the eventual skill: owners of small local service businesses (trades, clinics, advisors, coaches with a local base), aged 55 to 75, non-technical.

Method and limits: every claim below rests on web-search extracts. Direct fetches of whitespark.ca, support.google.com, ftc.gov, brightlocal.com, steadydemand.com and harmo.me failed (hosts not allowed), so primary documents were not read in full; their figures come via snippets or third-party summaries. Strength tags: **[official]** platform or regulator, **[vendor]** vendor-published data, **[independent]** independent data or study, **[practitioner]** experienced-agency testing or opinion, **[folklore]** widely repeated, unsourced.

## 1. Local ranking factors in 2025-2026

**The survey.** Whitespark's "2026 Local Search Ranking Factors" was published 6 Nov 2025: 47 invited experts scored 187 factors, and for the first time each factor was scored separately for Local Pack/Maps, Local Organic and a new "AI search visibility" column. **[practitioner survey, via Whitespark page snippet and summaries by Harmo, SEOMadman, gmbapi]**

**Top Local Pack factors (per secondary summaries; exact scores need checking against the report):**
1. Primary GBP category (score ~227)
2. Proximity of address to the searcher (~225)
3. Keywords in the GBP business title (~223)
4. Physical address in the city of search (~213)
5. Business is open at time of search / hours (~189, a new entry)
6. High numerical star rating
9. Quantity of native Google reviews

**Thematic weights (Local Pack, secondary summaries):** GBP signals ~32%; reviews rose from ~16% (2023) to ~20%; on-page, links and citations all fell; citations ~6-7% of pack weight. Citation consistency fell to ~28th of relevant factors, from top-10 in 2013-2015. **[practitioner survey via summaries; figures differ slightly between summaries]**

**Local organic:** summaries say on-page and link signals still dominate local organic (your website's ranking in the normal results); the AI-visibility column rewards a dedicated page per service (ranked #2 in one summary), "best of" list inclusions, unstructured mentions and consistent entity data. **[practitioner survey via summaries]**

**Keywords in the business name.** Whitespark's Q3 2026 roundup: businesses with the keyword in the title held 2x to 5x more top-3 pack positions (correlation only; adding words not on your signage is a policy violation, section 2). **[vendor correlation]** Sterling Sky (2022): keywords inside reviews did not move rankings; small sample. **[practitioner test]**

**Reviews as ranking input.** Survey consensus: rating, count and recency matter and gained weight; "velocity beats total count" is a summary's phrasing, not a measured finding. Owner replies are recommended by Google; no reliable evidence they move rank. **[practitioner; folklore for replies]**

**Photos and posts.** Sterling Sky's controlled tests found no ranking effect from adding photos (four listings); a Nov 2025 analysis of 8,186 businesses suggested recent photos help mainly in visual categories. Weekly posting for nine weeks on three profiles (441 keywords) produced no pack movement; the average post gets under 20 views in its seven-day life. Posts and photos are for conversion and trust, not rank. **[practitioner tests, via secondary coverage]** Google's "42% more direction requests with photos" figure circulates only via secondary sites. **[folklore]**

**What Google changed, Nov 2025 to Oct 2026 (and the run-up):**
- Chat/messaging and call history: ended 31 July 2024 (new chats stopped 15 July 2024). **[official via SEL, SERoundtable coverage]** Several 2026 blogs say WhatsApp can now be added as a contact option; single-source. **[vendor, unverified]**
- Social links: businesses can add Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn, TikTok, X, YouTube etc. since Aug 2023; from July 2025 Google began auto-adding links. **[official feature; Whitespark report of auto-adding]**
- AI review summaries: Gemini-generated 1-3 sentence summaries of review themes on Maps listings, announced Nov 2024, "Summarized with Gemini" label, not guaranteed for every place. **[official via SERoundtable, Places API docs]**
- Q&A removed: Q&A API shut 3 Nov 2025; public Q&A section began deprecation 3 Dec 2025, disappearing from most profiles in Q1 2026; old questions were not migrated. **[official change, widely corroborated by BrightLocal, PPC Land, others]**
- Ask Maps: Gemini conversational search in Maps, announced 12 March 2026 (US and India first), drawing on 300M+ places and ~500M reviews. Blogs say it reads GBP fields, website content and reviews; the owner cannot write its answers. **[official via press; owner-input claims practitioner]**
- AI-assisted business description (Google suggests wording from your profile, website and posts) and more granular service-area settings: single-source vendor claims. **[vendor, unverified]**
- Review enforcement: Google's Maps blog (16 April 2026) said it will remove fake content, pause new reviews on a profile during a spam spike, alert the owner and show a banner, and target "pay to remove one-star reviews" scams. **[official]** Blogs add that on 17 April 2026 the rating-manipulation policy gained bans on staff review quotas and asking customers to name an employee, and that repeat violations stack. **[vendor, single-source]** On 3 July 2026 Google said it was investigating disappearing reviews and had paused new reviews on affected profiles. **[official via TechWyse]**
- AI Overviews and AI Mode in local: Whitespark (Q2 2025) found AI Overviews on ~68% of local queries vs a map pack on ~39%, but for "service + city" queries the pack appears 90%+ and AIO only ~15%; Local Falcon measured 40.2% with a wider mix. Sterling Sky: AI local packs often show one or two businesses; 88% of 322 markets had fewer unique businesses. A June 2026 study (1,120 searches, 7 verticals, 13 cities) found 28.5% of pack businesses missing from AI Mode. **[vendor/practitioner studies]**
- Verification: video verification is now the default route; some get live video calls. **[vendor, consistent across sources]**
- Opening date, booking links, "justifications" (the snippet under a pack result such as "Their website mentions..." or review quotes): no 2025-2026 test found. Treat as completeness items and conversion aids, not rank levers. **[gap]**

## 2. GBP policies: eligibility, prohibited practices, suspensions

**Eligibility.** Google's help pages (via snippets): a profile is for businesses that make in-person contact with customers. If you do not have a storefront where you receive customers, you must hide the address; the profile then shows only the service area. **[official]** Home-based trades, mobile clinicians, advisors and coaches who meet clients face-to-face (at home with signage and hours, or at the client's location) are eligible as service-area businesses (SABs), with the home address used for verification and hidden. Up to 20 service areas can be listed. **[official via Whitespark, Local Falcon, Sterling Sky explainers]**

**Not eligible:** online-only businesses; virtual offices, PO boxes and mailbox stores; coworking desks without signage, staff and customer reception during hours. One coaching guide: a coach who only works over Zoom "does not technically qualify". Birdeye's looser reading ("virtual consultants can create profiles if they engage directly with customers") contradicts the others and is the minority view. **[official via practitioner explainers; contradiction flagged]**

**Prohibited practices (Google's business representation and review policies, via snippets and summaries):** adding keywords, cities or taglines to the name that are not on real-world signage; fake or ineligible addresses; incentives of any kind for reviews (payment, discounts, gifts, giveaway entries) whether the review is positive or negative, and incentives to edit or remove a negative review; "discouraging or prohibiting negative reviews, or selectively soliciting positive reviews" (review gating); reviews from owners, staff or swapped between businesses; review kiosks/on-premises pressure and employee-name requests (2026 additions per blogs). Penalties under the fake-engagement policy: blocking new reviews for a period, unpublishing reviews, a consumer warning on the profile. **[official policy text quoted by SearchLab, SEOlogist and others; 2026 additions are vendor-reported]**

**Suspensions.** Most-cited 2026 triggers: SAB showing a home address; profile address not matching the website; keyword-stuffed names; PO box or virtual office; category mismatch; duplicates; bulk rapid edits; fake engagement. Soft suspension (hidden, dashboard accessible) vs hard (account locked). Reviews are hidden, not deleted. **[practitioner, consistent]** A reported wave from ~27 April 2026 hit home-service SABs with "Deceptive Content" flags, with a May follow-up at account level; scale and cause unconfirmed, and all sources sell reinstatement. **[practitioner, unverified]**

**Reinstatement.** Fix the violation first (restore legal name, hide address, correct service area), then appeal once through Google's form with a business licence, utility bill or lease, signage and vehicle photos, and a matching website; never create a duplicate. Timelines reported anywhere from a few days to 3-6 weeks. A claim that Google now allows only two attempts before permanent removal is single-source. **[practitioner; "two attempts" unverified]**

## 3. Reviews

**Consumer expectations (BrightLocal Local Consumer Review Survey 2026, 1,002 US adults, fielded Feb 2026, via PinMeTo, Tap Review and others):** 31% will only use a business rated 4.5+ (17% in 2025); 68% require 4.0+ (55% in 2025); 47% will not consider a business with fewer than 20 reviews; 74% look for reviews from the last three months and 32% from the last two weeks; 85% more likely to use a business after positive reviews; 80% more likely to use a business that replies to all reviews, but templated replies put off 50%. **[independent survey, via secondary]**

**Benchmarks for the top 3 (contradictory):**
- Local Falcon whitepaper, 14 Jan 2026, 50.4M search results: median 3-pack review count per category ranges ~20 (home health care), 28 (general contractors), 40-54 (lawyers, accountants), ~56 (electricians), ~150 (chiropractors), ~215 (plumbers), 200-265 (HVAC, pest control), ~227 (breakfast restaurants), ~346 (dentists); typical medians 50-300; most 3-pack listings rate 4.8-4.9. **[vendor data]**
- RankLocalToday (711 listings, 6 industries, 8 cities): median top-3 count 261; average rating 4.78 (top 3) vs 4.69 (positions 8-15); count predicts rank far more than rating. **[vendor data, small]**
- Smarfle (7,481 businesses, 15 industries, June 2026): top-3 thresholds 74 (pool service) to 2,084 (HVAC); average rating 4.82. **[vendor data]**
- BrightLocal 2025 (6,300 businesses, 26 industries): top 3 average 47 reviews, 4.47 stars; far below the 2026 category medians (mixed categories, small markets). **Contradiction flagged.** Practical rule: benchmark against the lowest-review business consistently in the top 3 for your own town and category.
- Review velocity: no rigorous benchmark exists. Vendor rules of thumb: 3-5 per month for small markets, 3-8 mid-size, 10-30 in large metros; "4-10 per month for dentists, lawyers, plumbers". **[folklore/vendor]**

**Asking for reviews (what works):** ask within hours of the job or visit (vendors put peak at 0-4 hours); text beats email (reported completion 12-20% vs 3-8%; figures vary and are vendor-sourced); one reminder 3-5 days later roughly doubles yield; in-person ask plus a card with a QR code to the direct review link works for trades and clinics; ask everyone the same way (no gating); SMS needs consent (TCPA in the US). **[vendor/practitioner]**

**Responding:** UENI's study of 229,882 reviews (18 months): 5-star reviews got replies 29.3% of the time vs 9.8% for 1-star; average 19.5 days to reply to a 1-star. Vendors recommend replying within 24-72 hours, personally, naming the service, and never arguing. **[independent-ish vendor dataset; practice is practitioner]**

**Fake or unfair reviews:** flag via the Reviews Management Tool (there is now a "fake or incentivized" category); if still live after ~3 days, file the single allowed appeal (up to 10 reviews per batch) naming the policy breached and attaching evidence; if denied, post in the Business Profile Community forum where Product Experts can escalate; legal removal form is separate. One vendor's ~55% removal rate for clear violations is unverified. Google's April 2026 systems now pause reviews during spam spikes and target removal-extortion scams. **[official process via practitioner guides; rates unverified]**

**US law: FTC Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule (16 CFR Part 465), effective 21 Oct 2024.** Bans fake or AI-generated reviews, buying positive or negative reviews, undisclosed insider reviews, company-controlled review sites posing as independent, "review suppression" (legal threats or intimidation to remove honest reviews, or misleadingly displaying only some reviews) and fake social-media indicators. Civil penalties up to ~$53,088 per violation. FTC sent warning letters to about a dozen companies in late 2025; no litigated penalty case found yet. **Nuance:** "review gating" is not a standalone ban in the rule as summarised; Google's policy forbids it, and the FTC rule reaches it only as suppression or misrepresentation. Incentives are allowed under the FTC rule if not conditioned on sentiment; Google forbids them entirely. **[official via FTC warning template and law-firm summaries]**

**UK: DMCC Act fake-reviews ban, in force 6 April 2025.** Bans submitting, commissioning or publishing fake or concealed-incentive reviews and requires review hosts to take reasonable steps against them; incentivised reviews are allowed only if disclosed to readers; suppressing negatives is likely "deceptive"; prize-draw entries are "unlikely" to count as commissioning. CMA can fine up to 10% of global turnover or £300,000 (higher of), individuals up to £150,000; wrote to 54 firms in July 2025; blogs report five investigations opened March 2026 (unverified). **[official via law-firm summaries]**

## 4. Listings and citations in 2026

**Do they still matter?** For Google pack ranking, barely: ~6-7% of weight and citation consistency at 28th (Whitespark 2026). For AI visibility they matter more: three of the top five AI-visibility factors are citation-type ("best of" lists, unstructured mentions, mention volume; ~13% weight). Darren Shaw's advice: be on the top 30-50 directories, fix inaccuracies, stop. Agencies describe citations as "maintenance, not strategy". **[practitioner survey and opinion]**

**What AI engines actually cite (Steady Demand "AI Citation Ledger", Aug 2026, revised Sept 2026):** 1,487 local queries, 50 US metros, 14,472 citations. Business websites were ~60% of citations at first release, ~50% after revision; Reddit dropped out of both engines; Yelp appeared in the business-card grounding of 95.8% of ChatGPT runs (2,880 prompts); the classic pack is ~10x more stable than AI answers ("grounding drift"; a next-day-decay claim was withdrawn). **[vendor data, self-revised]** A home-services study (9,434 ChatGPT answers) cited Expertise.com 32%, Angi/HomeAdvisor 29%, Yelp 0.6%. **Contradiction flagged.** ChatGPT and Perplexity agreed on only ~24% of local picks in one small California test. **[vendor data]**

**Yelp licensing:** Yelp signed a non-exclusive deal with OpenAI (reported 23 July 2026) licensing reviews, ratings, photos and business details into ChatGPT local answers, with branded links and a coming Request-a-Quote lead flow; Yelp data already feeds Apple Maps, Alexa+ and Bing; Perplexity integrated Yelp earlier. **[official via Yelp 8-K and Axios]** Implication: a complete, reviewed Yelp page is now an AI-visibility asset for US local services.

**Free listings worth doing:** Google Business Profile; Apple Business (launched 14 April 2026, replacing Apple Business Connect, free, auto-migrated; feeds Apple Maps, Siri, Spotlight); Bing Places (free, imports from GBP, feeds Bing Maps and Copilot; whether it feeds ChatGPT is disputed); Yelp; Facebook; BBB; Nextdoor; the chamber of commerce; one or two industry directories (Angi/HomeAdvisor and Expertise for trades; Healthgrades/Zocdoc-type for clinics; state bar or professional-body listings for advisors). **[official for Apple/Bing; practitioner for the list]**

**NAP consistency:** still emphasised by every source as a trust signal for both search and AI entity matching, but ranking impact is low; fix glaring conflicts, do not chase hundreds of listings. **[practitioner]**

**Data aggregators (Data Axle, Foursquare, Neustar/Localeze):** sources say aggregator-level fixes are efficient because errors propagate, but none shows paid submission is needed in 2026; free claim-and-correct routes exist; Localeze's partner list is over a decade old; Foursquare's acquisition by Infillion (Sept 2026) is single-source. Verdict: not worth paying for a single-location service business. **[vendor opinion; acquisition unverified]**

## 5. Non-local service businesses (online coaches, consultants)

- A fully remote coach or consultant is not eligible for a GBP; 23% of suspended profiles in one vendor sample were businesses lacking in-person contact (virtual consultants, course creators). A coach with a local base who meets clients in person can list as an SAB with hidden address. **[official via practitioner explainers; 23% is vendor]**
- For AI answers to "best business coach" or "best consultant" queries, LinkedIn is reported as the most-cited domain for professional/B2B questions across six AI platforms; named individuals are the strongest predictor of citation in advisory categories; ChatGPT pulls ~75% of consulting citations from the firm's own site; only 4% of 1,700 B2B services firms were cited for any vertical they claimed, and single-niche positioning was cited far more often. **[vendor studies, methods unclear]**
- Directories: Perplexity frequently retrieves Clutch and DesignRush pages for agency queries; Clutch launched an AI Visibility Dashboard (May 2026) and a ChatGPT marketplace app. For coaches, the equivalent is professional-body directories (e.g. ICF, industry associations) plus LinkedIn and a strong personal site; no study found specific to coaches. **[vendor; gap]**
- "Near me" queries are answered by GBP packs a remote coach cannot enter; the realistic play is city-specific service pages and local press/podcast mentions. **[practitioner inference]**

## 6. Benchmarks, cadences and a realistic weekly budget

- Google's own playbook (as reported) recommends at least one post per week for engagement, not rank. Photo and post cadence does not move rank in controlled tests; reviews do. **[official recommendation via secondary; practitioner tests]**
- Top performers' routine per guides: reply to every review within 24-72 hours; 1 post and 1-3 photos per week; verify hours, holiday hours, phone and links weekly; accept or reject Google-suggested edits; quarterly audit of categories, services and listings. Q&A seeding is obsolete; the replacement is a website FAQ and complete GBP services, which Ask Maps draws on. **[practitioner]**
- Published case studies with hard numbers are thin and mostly vendor (Sterling Sky null results, Local Falcon medians, UENI reply rates). No independent before/after case study of a weekly routine was found. **[gap]**
- Owner time budget from agency guides: 30 minutes (hours, links, reviews) to 60-90 minutes (plus a post, photos and insights) per week; a realistic target for a 55-75 year-old owner is 30 minutes weekly plus a 5-second ask-for-review habit after every job, and a one-hour quarterly check. **[practitioner]**

## Contradictions and claims to verify

1. Exact Whitespark 2026 scores and weights (summaries differ: GBP 32%? reviews 20%? citations 6-7%).
2. Top-3 rating benchmark: BrightLocal 2025 (4.47) vs 2026 category studies (4.8+).
3. Yelp in ChatGPT local answers: 95.8% grounding (Steady Demand) vs 0.6% citations (home-services study).
4. April 2026 Google review-policy additions and stacked restrictions: vendor-only.
5. April-May 2026 SAB suspension wave scale; "two reinstatement attempts"; timelines.
6. WhatsApp contact, AI-assisted descriptions, granular service areas: single-source.
7. Ask Maps inputs (GBP fields? website FAQ?); FAQ rich results deprecated May 2026?
8. FTC rule and review gating (not a standalone ban; Google's policy is).
9. CMA March 2026 investigations; Foursquare/Infillion deal; Bing Places feeding ChatGPT.
10. SMS vs email review conversion percentages (all vendor).
