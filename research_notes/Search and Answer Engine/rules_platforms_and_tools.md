## 1. Rules on claims in content and reviews

**FTC Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule (16 CFR 465).** In force since 21 Oct 2024; counsel report active enforcement with warning letters from Dec 2025, posted on ftc.gov (`practitioner`: Benesch, DLA Piper Jul 2026). Civil penalty cited at up to $53,088 per violation (`practitioner`; verify). The rule bans: (a) fake or AI-generated reviews; (b) incentives *conditioned on sentiment* (paying for 5-star reviews is illegal even with a disclosure); (c) undisclosed insider reviews (owner, manager, employee or relative must disclose the relationship); (d) company-run review sites posing as independent; (e) review suppression, including legal threats and showing only positive reviews while claiming to show all; (f) fake social-media indicators. Asking every customer is lawful; asking only expected-happy customers ("gating") is treated as suppression (`practitioner`: ftcattorney.com, digifeel). **Google is stricter**: it bans *any* incentive for a review (see section 6).

**FTC Endorsement Guides (16 CFR 255, revised July 2023).** Material connections (payment, free service, employment, family) must be disclosed when a "significant minority" of the audience would not expect them; disclosures must be clear and conspicuous; advertisers are liable even if the endorser is not (`practitioner`: DWT, Venable). Practical: a testimonial must reflect a real client's honest experience, and an atypical result needs a statement of typical results (a bare "results not typical" line is not enough; verify wording in 16 CFR 255.2).

**FTC AI-claims enforcement ("Operation AI Comply").** Started Sep 2024, continuing under the new administration using Section 5, no new AI rule (`practitioner`: Benesch, Wright Law). 2026 matters: Air AI proposed settlement 24 Mar 2026 ($18M judgment) over earnings and "replaces staff" claims; Cox Media Group and others, $930k consent orders 27 Aug 2026 over false "AI listening" claims (`practitioner`; details conflict, verify on ftc.gov). A Section 5-and-AI policy statement was proposed July 2026 and is **not final**. Rule for a member: any claim about what AI does for clients needs substantiation.

**State AI laws relevant to publishing AI-assisted content.**
- *Colorado*: SB 24-205 was repealed and replaced by SB 26-189 (signed 14 May 2026); the narrower law takes effect 1 Jan 2027 and targets consequential automated decisions, not marketing copy (`practitioner`: Hunton, Eckert Seamans). A federal magistrate stayed enforcement of the old act 27 Apr 2026 (`gov`/`practitioner`).
- *Utah* (SB 149 as amended 2025): disclose generative-AI use when a consumer clearly asks; "regulated occupations" (health, mental health, legal, financial, etc.) must disclose proactively in "high-risk" interactions; $2,500 per violation; sunset extended to 1 Jul 2027 (`practitioner`/tracker; verify enrolled text). This touches chatbots and AI client interaction, not a static web page.
- *California* SB 942 (AI Transparency Act): operative date moved by AB 853 to 2 Aug 2026; duties fall on *covered providers* of generative AI (watermarks, detection tools), not on a small business that publishes AI-assisted text (`practitioner`: A&O Shearman, ailawsbystate).
- Net: **no US state or federal law found that requires a small service business to label AI-assisted web copy.** Honesty rules (Section 5, state UDAP) still apply to the content's claims.

**Platform policies on AI content (2026).**
- *Google Search*: AI-produced content is not against guidelines; "scaled content abuse" (many pages built to manipulate rankings, however produced) is spam (`official` wording quoted via ppc.land/SEJ). No disclosure requirement found. Secondary blogs claim Mar/Jun/Sep 2026 updates targeting scaled content; unverified (`folklore`).
- *YouTube*: creators must still self-disclose realistic altered/synthetic content; from May 2026 YouTube also auto-labels photorealistic AI content, with labels shown under the player and on Shorts (`official`: YouTube blog; TechCrunch, Variety 27 May 2026).
- *LinkedIn*: shows a "CR" Content Credentials badge when C2PA metadata is present; one marketing blog only (`folklore`; verify on LinkedIn help).

## 2. Regulated professions: what a member may and may not publish

**Financial advisers (SEC-registered RIAs).** The Marketing Rule (17 CFR 275.206(4)-1) allows testimonials (clients) and endorsements (non-clients) only with clear and prominent disclosure that it is a testimonial/endorsement, whether the person is a client, any compensation and material conflicts; a written agreement is needed for compensated promoters above $1,000 in 12 months; the adviser must have a reasonable basis to believe each one complies and keep records. SEC's Dec 2025 Risk Alert flagged testimonials, endorsements, third-party ratings and online reviews as recurring failures and listed hyperlinked or buried disclosures as not "clear and prominent"; staff also cited advisers giving gift cards for third-party reviews (`gov` via `practitioner`: Nelson Mullins, Mintz Feb 2026, Wealthtender). Practical: an adviser may show Google reviews on a site only with the disclosures above and compliance sign-off; never pay for or solicit selectively. **Broker-dealer reps (FINRA Rule 2210)**: testimonials allowed with disclosures that it may not be representative of other clients' experience, is no guarantee of future performance, and that it is paid if more than $100 in value (`practitioner`; verify in 2210(d)(6)). State-registered advisers follow state rules that often mirror the SEC. Check: compliance officer, SEC/FINRA, state securities regulator.

**Lawyers.** ABA Model Rule 7.1 bars false or misleading communications, including true statements that omit context; 7.2(c) limits "specialist"/"certified" to ABA-accredited or state-approved certification with the certifying body named (`gov` text via ACC materials). Testimonials and past results are generally allowed but typically need a "past results do not guarantee" style disclaimer, and some states require pre-filing (Florida: 20-day pre-filing with fees; Texas: filing within 10 days of first use) (`practitioner`; verify). Florida Rule 4-7.14 (amended Aug 2019) lets non-certified lawyers claim expertise only if objectively verifiable *and* with a prominent "not board certified" disclaimer; "board certified" is reserved for the Florida plan (`gov`: Fla. Sup. Ct. SC18-2019). Texas Rule 7.02 bars "specialist" without certification and superlatives like "best" (`practitioner`). Practical: "we handle X cases" not "X specialist"; no "best/top"; disclaim results; file with the bar where required.

**Medical, dental and other HIPAA-covered providers.** HIPAA Privacy Rule: never confirm a reviewer is a patient and never mention diagnosis, treatment, dates, insurance or cost in a public reply, even if the patient posted it first. OCR enforcement: Elite Dental (2019, $10,000 settlement, 2-year monitoring) and a North Carolina dental practice (2022, $50,000 civil penalty, worsened by ignoring OCR) (`gov` via HIPAA Journal, Hall Render). No 2025–2026 OCR review-reply action surfaced (`uncertain`). Safe reply pattern: generic, no acknowledgement of a care relationship, invite offline contact with the practice's privacy officer, and have a written social-media/review policy. Client testimonials on the website require a HIPAA-compliant written authorisation. Blogs saying "thanks for coming in" is itself a violation go beyond any enforcement found (`folklore`).

**Therapists and counsellors.** APA Ethics Standard 5.05 prohibits soliciting testimonials from current therapy clients or anyone vulnerable to undue influence; ACA Code C.3.b extends that to *former* clients (`gov`-equivalent code text via practitioner sites). ACA's revised code is expected to be adopted Sep 2026; verify numbering. Many state boards add their own bans. Practical: no client testimonials or marketing surveys of clients; use colleague endorsements and credential pages; unsolicited Google reviews may stay but replies must not confirm a care relationship.

**Real estate.** Fair Housing Act advertising liability covers websites and social posts, words and images. Describe the property, never the ideal occupant: avoid "perfect for young families," "adults only," "safe neighbourhood," "exclusive," school-quality claims and religious selling points; "master bedroom" is facially neutral per a 1995 HUD memo though many MLSs prefer "primary" (`practitioner`: Fair Housing Institute, flarebuilt). Check: state licensing law, MLS rules, NAR Code of Ethics (not surfaced; verify).

**Insurance agents.** State unfair-trade-practices laws and NAIC-model advertising rules (as adopted per state) require testimonials to be genuine, current, applicable to the policy and accurately reproduced; the advertiser adopts them as its own; a Kansas order penalised an agent for "never had a claim denied" (`gov`: KS order; NAIC Model 660). New York and Florida require disclosure of paid spokespersons (`gov`). Practical: no payout or guarantee claims, no "lowest rates"; check the state insurance department for pre-approval or disclaimer rules.

## 3. YMYL content: what a small practice can publish safely

Google's Search Quality Rater Guidelines (current edition Sep 2025, with YMYL clarifications and AI Overview examples; a Jan 2026 revision is claimed by one blog, unverified) treat health, financial and legal topics as YMYL and put Trust first (`official` via Search Engine Roundtable). Nothing official says a "not advice" line helps ranking; practitioners recommend visible author name, credentials, licence state, review date and reviewer line (`practitioner`). Google says structured data is not required for AI features (`official` via Digidop). Safe pattern for the skill: scope note ("general information for [state] residents; not legal/medical/financial advice; no client relationship is formed"), author box with licence number, plain-language FAQ answers, link to the regulator's public page, and a reviewed-on date. **How AI engines treat YMYL**: studies Oct 2025–Sep 2026 show Google AI Overviews citing YouTube, GoodRx, Amazon and Reddit heavily for health, and citing official sources less often than ChatGPT (2.3:1 vs 4.2:1) (`vendor`: BrightEdge, SE Ranking, LLM Authority Index). Google replied that an expert-written article on a multi-topic blog can be high quality (`official` via SE Ranking). Implication: a small practice page with clear credentials can be cited; ChatGPT leans harder on institutional pages.

## 5. Free tools in 2026 and their limits

- **Google Search Console**: free; Generative AI performance report (beta, 3 Jun 2026, worldwide by late June) shows AI Overviews/AI Mode impressions but no clicks; Settings > "Search generative AI" can block content from AI features (`official`: Search Central blog).
- **Bing Webmaster Tools**: free; AI Performance report (Copilot and Bing AI) expanded June 2026 with Intents, Topics, Citation Share and Compare; IndexNow Insights report since 2024; URL submission and IndexNow key hosting free (`official`: Bing blog).
- **Google Business Profile Performance**: free in the profile manager; no 2026 change surfaced (`uncertain`).
- **Google Trends**: free; last major Trending Now redesign Aug 2024 (10-minute refresh, 125 countries); nothing new found for 2026 (`official`: Google blog).
- **PageSpeed Insights / Lighthouse**: free; INP is the interactivity metric; field (CrUX) data matters more than the lab score (`practitioner`).
- **Rich Results Test** (Google) and **Schema Markup Validator** (schema.org): both still live; RRT checks Google's supported types (count disputed: 25 vs 31), validator checks vocabulary (`practitioner`).
- **WAVE (WebAIM)**: free, one page at a time, no site-wide scan (`practitioner`).
- **AnswerThePublic**: free plan 3 searches/day (`official` help centre). **AlsoAsked**: 3 free searches/day, no volume (`vendor`). **Ubersuggest**: 3 free searches/day with truncated data (`vendor`).
- **Ahrefs Webmaster Tools** (now "Ahrefs Free"): verified sites only; 5,000 Site Audit crawl credits per project per month, 500 Site Explorer credits (`official`). **Semrush free**: 10 requests/day across reports, 1 project, 10 tracked keywords (`official` KB).
- **ChatGPT free**: web search included; US free tier carries ads since Feb 2026; limits eased Aug 2026 (press, conflicting). **Perplexity free**: unlimited basic search, about 5 Pro searches/day (`vendor`). **Gemini free**: compute-based limits since May 2026; model access disputed (`folklore`). All three are enough for a weekly "does the engine mention us" check.
- **Google Alerts**: free, unchanged. **Talkwalker Alerts**: no confirmed shutdown; Talkwalker itself is now "Lumen by Talkwalker" inside Hootsuite, and the free Alerts tool's status should be checked on talkwalker.com before the skill recommends it (`uncertain`).

## 6. Review-request mechanics

- **Link and QR**: Google's own help page says owners may ask customers to visit a Google review link or scan a QR code; the "Get more reviews" / "Ask for reviews" short link is in the Business Profile manager; a QR must lead to the standard review form, never a page that pre-selects stars or pre-fills text (`official` via vendor summaries).
- **Email (CAN-SPAM)**: a review request may be judged commercial by "primary purpose"; CAN-SPAM is opt-out, so include a physical address and working unsubscribe and honour opt-outs promptly (`gov` via practitioner).
- **SMS (TCPA)**: the one-to-one consent rule was vacated (11th Cir., Jan 2025), but prior express written consent is still required for marketing texts; whether a review request is "transactional" is unsettled, so collect written text consent at booking, send in business hours, at most one text a day and three per job, and honour STOP; state mini-TCPAs can be stricter (`gov`/`vendor`).
- **Timing**: no 2025–2026 study surfaced; vendors send within hours of job completion (e.g. 4 hours) and follow up within a week (`vendor`).
- **Never**: gate (send only to happy customers or route unhappy ones to a private form), offer any incentive (Google) or sentiment-conditioned incentive (FTC), write or draft the review for the client, ask employees or family without disclosure, use kiosks or on-site pressure (Google policy summaries; 2026 "explicitly forbidden" framing is vendor marketing), buy reviews, or threaten reviewers.
- **Replies**: reply to all; health and therapy members use a generic template that never confirms a care relationship; never argue or post client details; for fake reviews use the profile's flag option, then the review-management form and appeal; removals happen only for policy violations, not disagreement (`official` via vendors).
