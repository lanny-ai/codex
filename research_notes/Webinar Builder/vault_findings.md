# Webinar Builder: what the founder's vault says (synthesis, 2026-11-02)

Four read-only research passes over the founder's Obsidian vault: the distilled webinar frameworks, the mastermind expert talks (2014–2021, plus 2024–2026 notes), the founder's own webinars and bootcamps, and the partner libraries. The full working notes (about 17,500 words, a vault path beside every claim) stay outside the repo because the paths name attendees, clients and partners. This file keeps only methods, credited to the published teachers who own them, and the founder's own lessons. No attendee, client or partner names, and none of the founder's revenue, list, enrolment or buyer figures.

## Five findings that shape the skill

1. **Live beats automated; never pretend.** The founder teaches that live interaction is what sells and that automated webinars are dead. Frank Kern and Michael Stevenson, who disagree on much else, agree that a recording must never be presented as live. Kern's middle path: find the winning talk live, then automate the winner.
2. **Most of the result is the system around the talk.** Story emails before the invite (three or more), reminders 24 hours, the morning of and 1 hour before, follow-up branching on attended, stayed to the offer, left early and did not attend (Kern's three pillars: indoctrination, the webinar, the post-webinar sequence).
3. **The teaching closes its own loops** (Stevenson): each block confirms a suspicion, admits a fault of the industry, is self-evident, and answers its own question. Open loops teach people they can work out the rest alone.
4. **Say you will sell, and name the objections early.** A stage-close script from the founder's archive announces both halves ("I will share something you can use tomorrow; I will also make an offer") and names the top objections as "what we are not going to talk about today". Stevenson scripts Q&A on time, money, confidence and the spouse.
5. **Review every run.** The founder's best final day was rewritten with AI after a day that did not land (the diagnosis: no gap created between where people were and where they could be). He tells members to record everything and ask AI where it went wrong and how to recover.

## Structures read (credited, not reproduced)

- **Jason Fladlien**: the agreement frame in the first two minutes; education measured by behaviour change; teach the first step, not the expert's whole system; the honest close for presenters who dislike selling.
- **Mike Koenigs**: three lessons ("people can keep track of three"), resolve the personal story quickly, close at about 50 minutes, bonuses mapped to objections, Q&A as the objection close.
- **Michael Stevenson**: three content chunks that close their loops, scripted Q&A on four objections, "these are the questions people most often ask" for recorded runs.
- **Frank Kern**: the six-part content webinar (confirmation, the difference, story, payoff, transition with "so that's how… and if you're serious, here's how I can help", offer); never fake live.
- **Mitch Carson**: precision, ask three times, celebrate the host.
- **Dharma Rose**: the paid web class; full-value delivery; risk reversal.
- **Mike Filsaime**: a time-coded replay page; consequence-based (real) deadlines.
- **Alex Cattoni** (via a framework note): the emotional order understood, safe, empowered, excited.

## Where the experts disagree, and how the skill settles it

| Question | Positions | Skill's choice for this audience |
|---|---|---|
| Length | 2h15 (Stevenson); 2–3h for a desperate audience (Fladlien); about 50 minutes (Koenigs); 40–45 (2025 mastermind advice) | 60 minutes, ending on time; the strongest material before minute 30 |
| Live or automated | Automated (Stevenson); live (founder, Filsaime) | Live first; recorded only after a live run converts, always labelled |
| Replays | None (Stevenson); a profit centre (Filsaime, Koenigs) | A replay with a stated, real expiry; never the identical replay as the whole follow-up (Kern) |
| Where the price goes | Early (stage close); last third (most) | After the teaching, with the offer disclosed on the agenda |
| Payment plans | Recover fence-sitters (Filsaime); drive refunds (Koenigs) | Fixed-length plans with the total shown, from `close.md` |
| "It's not your fault" | Common | Avoided (Fladlien: overused and removes responsibility) |

## Dated tactics the skill refuses

Fake poll results; a recording implied to be live; inflated attendance; seeded questions or hostile chat (one teacher called his own idea "borderline unethical"); a timer extended while saying it is closing; countdowns that reset; replay threats; hidden low-contrast embedded commands; "page coming down" urgency on free content; reading buyers' full names aloud; harsh "go get a job" disqualification. Each has an honest replacement in `honesty-rules.md`.

## The founder's own lessons (no figures)

- Worked: an unflattering origin story so credentials do not sound like bragging; "this may or may not be for you"; segmenting the room with chat questions; a tier ladder of "doors"; an honest deadline (a live bonus session for people who join during the event); Q&A routed to the right door; a moderator dropping the link; AI review of the recording and a rewritten next day.
- Did not work: a day with no gap created; untried breakout rooms; a free tool failing under live load; offers mentioned piecemeal (an attendee asked for a PDF of everything on offer, hence the one-pager); an inconsistent top-tier price; days far too long ("you wouldn't sit through my bootcamp, and neither would I"); a reminder landing in spam; most buyers buying after the event, not during it.
- Gap: nothing in the vault addresses an audience aged 55 to 75 directly; the skill's older-audience choices are its own.
