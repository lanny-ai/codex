Research stream: profile-as-conversion-page and content that makes a solo service expert (45–65, 10–25 yrs in, $100K–$300K) the obvious choice.
Compiled: 2026-10-16.

## What matters most (10 lines)

1. **The profile is now read by the ranking model, not just by people.** LinkedIn's 2026 LLM-based feed ranker tries to judge "what a post is about" and how it fits a member's interests. Practitioners and vendors believe the posts are scored against your headline and About section ("360Brew"). Keep the headline, About section and 2–3 content pillars on exactly the same topic. [verified: intent / unverified: mechanics]
2. **Headline = who you help + the outcome + proof, not your job title.** Banner = the promise plus one call to action. The first two lines of About = the hook. [practitioner method: Welsh; synthesis]
3. **Creator Mode is gone (retired Feb–Mar 2024).** Its tools went to everyone: Follow button, Featured section, newsletter, Live. "Make follow primary" is now a toggle in settings. [verified]
4. **Link buttons have narrowed.** Since May 2025, a free profile link cannot be added or replaced. The custom button ("Book an appointment", "Visit my website", "View my services") needs **Premium Business or Sales Navigator**, and it **replaces the Services section** on the profile. This is a real trade-off for solo experts. [verified]
5. **Verification is cheap trust.** LinkedIn says ID-verified members get up to 60% more profile views and up to 50% more post engagement. A photo gives "up to 21x" more profile views. Both are LinkedIn's own claims, stated as "up to". [verified, company-reported]
6. **Sustainable cadence is 2–5 posts a week.** Buffer's data (2M+ posts) shows per-post gains at that level. Van der Blom now says 2–4. Daily posting is optional, not required. [data study]
7. **Organic reach fell roughly 50% from 2024 to 2025**, so posts must earn attention through relevance and depth. Volume tricks and pods no longer work: LinkedIn is actively killing pods, comment automation and "comment KEYWORD" bait. [data study + verified]
8. **Content mix for a solo expert: authority + proof + personal.** Teach freely and sell the implementation (Hormozi). Use stories to widen reach (Acosta). Proof and case posts convert. Most posts should not pitch, but sell clearly at least weekly in a soft way. [practitioner consensus]
9. **The AI backlash is now built into the product.** LinkedIn added a "Seems like AI slop" report button (Jul 2026, over 1M clicks by Aug 2026). It also removed its own AI post writer and replaced it with a proofreader. Generic AI text is the risk. AI-assisted editing of a real person's real story is allowed. [verified]
10. **Experience is the moat for a 45–65 expert.** Lived stories, named client outcomes, contrarian judgment and decades of pattern recognition are exactly what an LLM cannot fake. Build the skill around capturing the expert's own words (voice notes, interviews), not around generating posts. [synthesis]

### 1.2 Headline

- **Practitioner method (Justin Welsh):** answer three questions. *Who specifically do you help? What specifically do you help them do? What outcome should they expect?* Do not just repeat a job title like "Content Writer at X". People search by keyword in the headline, so be specific. (justinwelsh.me "How to Make a Stand-Out LinkedIn Profile in 8 Steps"; "Optimize Your LinkedIn for 2024").
- **Why it matters more now [unverified mechanics]:** practitioner and vendor write-ups on the 2025–26 ranker (AuthoredUp "360Brew"; upgrowth; Expandi) all claim the model checks post topic against the headline, About and history. LinkedIn's own March 2026 engineering post only confirms that the ranker models "what a post is about and how it relates to a member's evolving interests and career goals" (via Social Media Today 814638; ZenML summary). Treat "headline–content match" as a sensible hedge, not a proven factor.
- **Headline formulas [synthesis; built on Welsh's Who/What/Outcome]:**
  1. `I help [specific buyer] [achieve outcome] without [pain/risk] | [proof: years, clients, $]`
  2. `[Role noun the buyer searches for] for [niche] | [signature method/name] | [credibility marker]`, e.g. "Fractional CFO for $5–20M family businesses | 22 yrs | 140+ owners advised"
  3. `[Outcome] for [buyer] → [mechanism] | Author of [newsletter/book]`
  4. `Ex-[recognisable credential] → now helping [buyer] [outcome]`, which suits 45–65 career-changers
  - Put the searchable keyword (for example "executive coach" or "M&A advisor") in the first ~60 characters. That part survives truncation on mobile and in comments. [synthesis]

### 1.3 Banner (1584×396 px, 4:1)

- **Practitioner method (Welsh):** call the banner the most valuable real estate on the profile. State what you do and who it's for "at a glance". Pick one goal (newsletter sign-ups, website visits, book a consult, follows). Keep the text short, add a clear CTA and use your brand colours. (justinwelsh.me "Most Social Profiles Aren't Very Good…"; stand-out profile article).
- **Practitioner method (Jasmin Alić, as analysed):** use social proof (community testimonials), a contextual photo of him teaching or coaching, large text on the right where the eye lands, and a short CTA to explore the profile. (Platzi course analysis; Taplio banner gallery).
- **Technical [secondary]:** design at 1584×396 (or 2x). The circular photo covers the bottom-left on desktop and moves on mobile, so keep text centred or right and check it on a phone. Max 8MB. (Snappa; Zeliq 2026; MagicStudio).
- **Recommended banner content for a solo expert [synthesis]:** the promise line (the outcome for the buyer) + one proof element (a client logo strip, "120+ owners advised", a book cover or a rating) + one CTA that matches the custom button or Featured item. Nothing else.

### 1.4 About section structure (2,600 chars; first ~2 lines visible)

No LinkedIn primary source sets a structure. The following is a **[synthesis]** of Welsh, Hormozi-style offer logic and the hidden-buyer research (Edelman 2025: 85% value "demonstrated understanding of their specific challenges"):
1. **Lines 1–2 (the hook, visible before "see more"):** name the buyer's situation or pain in their words, plus the outcome you produce.
2. **Who I work with / who I don't:** specificity signals expertise.
3. **The problem, reframed:** your point of view, the "why most X fail" angle.
4. **How I help:** your named method in 3–5 steps or bullets, plus the formats (1:1, advisory, workshop).
5. **Proof:** 3–5 outcome bullets with numbers, client types, years and notable credentials. A 45–65 expert should lean hard on this.
6. **Human paragraph:** 2–3 lines on why you do this, plus personal texture.
7. **CTA:** one action, e.g. "DM me the word 'audit'" is fine in About, or the booking link or newsletter. Repeat the keyword phrase naturally 2–3 times.

### 1.5 Featured section

- **Practitioner method (Welsh, 2024):** link to **only 1–2 destinations**. Featuring your top viral posts "keeps people scrolling without ever joining a list". Put the newsletter in Featured and in the first comment of posts. (justinwelsh.me "Optimize Your LinkedIn for 2024").
- **Recommended order for a solo expert [synthesis]:** (1) the conversion asset: a booking page, a free diagnostic or a lead magnet landing page; (2) a proof asset: a case study PDF/document or a client-results post; (3) optionally the newsletter. Use a custom thumbnail image with a plain-English title.

### 1.6 Services page vs custom button: a decision for the skill [synthesis on verified facts]

- **Free tier:** a Services page (keywords, up to ~10 services, appears in services search and is a free discovery surface) + a Featured link to the booking page + a contact-info website.
- **Premium Business:** a custom "Book an appointment" or "View my services" button, which also shows on posts and in search. It **replaces** the Services section. Choose based on whether inbound comes from search (keep Services) or from content (the button is better).

### 1.7 Photo guidance

- LinkedIn's only data point is "up to 21x views / 9x connection requests" for *having* a photo (company claim). No LinkedIn data on photo style was found. Common guidance (recent, face ~60% of frame, plain background, a real smile, matching how you look on a Zoom call) is practitioner convention, not data. For the 45–65 segment, a **current** photo matters: an outdated photo is a trust leak on the first video call. [synthesis]

## 2. Content strategy for B2B service founders

### 2.1 Platform context (what the data says in 2025–26)

- **Reach decline:** Richard van der Blom's *Algorithm Insights 2025* (1.8M posts, 58k profiles, 31k company pages, 12 months to Feb 2025) found reach down about 50% year on year, engagement at about 75% of prior levels and follower growth at 59% of the prior benchmark. [data study, n=1.8M posts] (Scribd excerpt of chapter 1; ContentIn summary). Later figures conflict: −34%, −47% (Feb→Sep 2025, Oct 2025 edition) and −60% over 2 years for active creators. [data study; numbers contradict]
- **Interest graph over relationship graph:** van der Blom says LinkedIn moved from a relationship graph to an interest graph, so topic authority and per-post merit matter more than follower count. (LeadMagic summary.) [practitioner method: van der Blom]
- **Formats (2025 edition, personal profiles):** polls 1.64x reach multiplier (up from 1.32x), documents/carousels 1.45x (but their median reach fell 25%), images 1.18x (median down 27.4%). (AuthoredUp blog citing van der Blom.) [data study]. *Fad flag:* "carousels get 11.2x impressions" circulates without a source. Don't use it. Polls lead on reach, but LinkedIn has said it shows fewer polls from strangers (2022 feed blog), so the reach is network-bound and weak for winning clients. Polls are a reach format, not a conversion format.
- **External links:** van der Blom figures circulating say one link in the post body cuts median reach by about 18.8%. Some 2026 commentary says the first-comment workaround no longer avoids it. LinkedIn has never published a "link penalty" policy. [unverified / contested]
- **Frequency:** Buffer (2025-08-28; 2M+ posts, 94k accounts; per-account z-scores): 2–5 posts a week vs 1 a week gives **+1,182 impressions per post** and +0.23pp engagement rate. 6–10 and 11+ a week give further but diminishing gains, "if quality holds". [data study, n=2M+]. Van der Blom (2025 podcast) now says **2–4 posts a week**, down from 5–6, and consistency over volume. [practitioner method]
- **Length and hooks:** AuthoredUp-attributed figures: 1,301–2,500 characters gets about 27% more engagement than under 400. Another source says 800–1,000 is the sweet spot. In one dataset, story hooks had the highest median engagement (2.60%), then contrarian (2.31%), then questions (2.16%). Sample sizes are cited as 309k–622k posts depending on the write-up. [data study; contradictory secondary reporting]
- **Saves and substantive comments outweigh likes** in nearly all 2025–26 analyses. Multipliers vary (5x, 5–10x) and none is from LinkedIn. [unverified]

### 2.2 Practitioner methods (precise summaries)

**Justin Welsh** (solopreneur; LinkedIn Operating System and Content Operating System courses)
- **Content Matrix:** a grid of **sub-topics** (themes under your core subject; he suggests about 5) × **content styles** (how a piece is delivered and what it aims to do, e.g. step-by-step guide, contrarian take, observation, listicle). Work through each cell, write a quick headline for each and store them in Notion. Each idea yields 3–4 pieces, so about **75–100 ideas** and a "730-day content library". ChatGPT can help fill the grid. (justinwelsh.me "How to Build a 730-Day Content Library"; "How to Build Your Personal Brand on LinkedIn"). [practitioner method: Welsh]. *Note:* the exact list of 8 styles in the paid course could not be confirmed from free pages.
- **Hub and spoke:** one long-form weekly newsletter (the hub) is cut into 4–6 social posts (the spokes). (justinwelsh.me "Hub and Spoke Content Model").
- **1-3-5 method:** one pillar piece becomes about 15–16 pieces. (justinwelsh.me essays).
- **Profile:** Who/What/Outcome headline; the banner as prime real estate with one CTA; Featured limited to 1–2 links; newsletter in Featured and in the first comment.
- **Style (secondary analysis):** one strong opening sentence + a simple list; "two types of people" contrasts. (Unofficial PDF breakdown; treat as indicative.)

**Lara Acosta** (UK personal-brand creator; "SLAY")
- **SLAY = Story → Lesson → Actionable → You.** The story builds connection, the lesson builds authority, the actionable step gives the reader something to do and the "You" closing question invites comments. (Callum McDonnell Substack; Cool Story newsletter; Buldrr guides.) [practitioner method: Acosta]
- **Her claim:** stories beat purely educational posts. An educational post only reaches people who already know they have the problem, while a story reaches a wider audience. (Callum McDonnell Substack.)
- **The lesson must tie to the specific story**, not a generic life observation, and the actionable step must be concrete enough to do without further instructions. (Buldrr SLAY guide.)
- **CTA by post type (secondary analysis):** educational posts end with a tool or checklist offer, story posts end with a question. (ViralBrain.)
- **Her content-market fit:** uses her own revenue history as the case study and is openly transparent about it. (ViralBrain.)

**Jasmin Alić** ("Hey Jay"; LinkedIn coach and copywriter; repeatedly ranked a top LinkedIn creator)
- **The first three lines carry the post** (the "see more" cut-off). End by prompting engagement rather than with a neat closing line. **"You don't need to sell in your content to sell from it."** (Rosetta.to, quoting The Futur podcast.) [practitioner method: Alić]
- **Comments as distribution:** commenting builds the commenter's own visibility with everyone who engages. Write substantial "mini-post" comments, including a pinned comment on your own post that extends it. (Learn Speak Teach #100; Social Media Tea newsletter summarising his webinar.)
- Says he has a "1-to-9 framework" for generating post ideas without AI (workshop listing; details not public). Consistent showing up, but skipping days when needed is fine (Groucast podcast).
- Writing style (third-party analysis): about 2 in 5 posts are short, punchy standalone advice, with a big share of "selling through value" posts. (MagicPost.)

**Matt Barker** (UK copywriter/content strategist for agency owners and founders)
- **Problem → Agitate → Solution** as the core post framework. It rests on research into the target audience's pain. Motivational or relatable content can win clients better than pitching posts. (The Copywriter Club podcast #450.) [practitioner method: Barker]
- Runs a free 3-day "Clients Come to You" challenge. Content credited with £2M+/$2M+ for himself or his clients. (Favikon; That's Literally It newsletter.) A routine attributed by a third party: post a problem-solving piece for the ideal client, watch engagement for about an hour, then DM a thank-you to people who engaged. [third-party description]
- *Not found:* any "LinkedIn Lab" framework or his exact sell ratio.

**Richard van der Blom** (LinkedIn algorithm researcher; Just Connecting)
- 2–4 posts a week; consistency over volume; **25–30 minutes a day of commenting**; commenting on the *wrong* (off-topic) posts can hurt, because comments shape your interest graph. Topic authority over follower count. (Creator Science podcast; Mics to Millions podcast, Apr 2025.) [practitioner method + data]

**Chris Donnelly** (UK founder, Lottie/Verb; about 1.1–1.2M followers)
- Turns nearly every post into a high-value **"free resource"** (cheat sheets, frameworks, white-background carousels with bold headers) plus a low-friction CTA (often a one-word comment). Posts about daily (31 a month per Favikon; other analysts say 7.8–9 a week). His text-only posts drew more average reactions (~2,060) than his carousels (~880) in one analysis. (Favikon; ViralBrain; unofficial PDF report.) [practitioner method; analytics vary]. *Flag:* the one-word-comment CTA is the pattern LinkedIn is now suppressing (see §3).

**Sahil Bloom** (newsletter-first creator; threads and LinkedIn)
- Hooks built on **curiosity, surprise and simplicity**, sometimes described as the "ICO" pattern (Intriguing statement → Context → Offer of what the reader gains). Close the curiosity loop the hook opened. **Named mental models** ("The 1-3-1 Rule", "Cost of Inaction") with 2x2 or decision-tree visuals. Personal anecdote → general lesson. Newsletter CTA at the end. (beehiiv blog; Prose; Ghost "creator flywheel"; style guides are third-party.) [practitioner method: Bloom]

**Dan Koe** (one-person business; "Two-Hour Writer")
- **Newsletter as the core asset**, reused across platforms. Outline first (hook → insight → practical process), then draft. Condensed newsletter ideas go to X and LinkedIn. Uses AI to break down high-performing posts into patterns, while the novel ideas come from the human. (beehiiv case study; trends.vc; Tagabukid Substack.) [practitioner method: Koe]. Reported time budgets conflict (30 min newsletter vs 2 h a day vs 2–4 h a day).

**Alex Hormozi** (*$100M Leads*, 2023)
- **Hook → Retain → Reward** is the book's named framework for turning content into leads. [verified from the book's table of contents via retail listings]
- **Give away the best information free and charge for implementation.** Release the exact frameworks you use, so those who hit the bottleneck come to you. (Buldrr channel analysis; Lilach Bullock.) [practitioner method: Hormozi, secondary]. The exact "give:ask" ratio in the book was **not checked (search budget exhausted)**. Don't quote a number.

### 2.4 Recommended system for a busy 45–65 solo expert [synthesis]

- **Pillars (3):** (1) **Expertise/POV**: how you think about the buyer's core problem; (2) **Proof**: client stories, before/after, numbers, process breakdowns; (3) **Personal/Values**: decades of career lessons, mistakes, why you work the way you do. Optional 4th: **Offer/Invitation**.
- **Mix per week (3 posts):** 1 teaching/POV + 1 proof/story + 1 personal or soft-sell. Every 2nd–4th week, make one of them an explicit offer post.
- **Batching:** one 60–90-minute weekly session. Record a 20-minute voice note or interview answering 3 prompts drawn from the matrix → transcribe → draft 3 posts in the expert's own words → the expert edits. Repurpose the best post monthly into a newsletter issue (Welsh's hub and spoke, reversed for non-writers).
- **Daily 15–25 minutes:** substantive comments on 5–10 posts by ideal buyers and peer experts (van der Blom, Alić). These are not automated.
- **Writing style:** short paragraphs, plain language, one idea per line, concrete numbers and names, no hashtags needed (hashtags no longer help reach, per van der Blom 2025 and AuthoredUp), at most one link (in the comment or Featured).

## 3. What converts followers into clients

- **Thought leadership influences buying [data study].** Edelman–LinkedIn 2024 B2B Thought Leadership Impact Report: n≈3,500 management-level professionals, 7 countries, fieldwork 2023-11-30 to 12-14. Its premise: buyers are slowing purchases, so thought leadership has to make them rethink their problem. The 2025 edition (n≈2,000; target decision-makers, hidden decision-makers and hidden influencers) reports that 91% of hidden buyers say strong thought leadership makes them more receptive to outreach. It also reports that 79% are more likely to advocate internally for a consistent producer, that 53% say brand recognition matters less when thought leadership is strong, that 85% value demonstrated understanding of their specific challenges, and that 40%+ of deals stall over internal misalignment. (Edelman insights pages; Demand Gen Report; AMI.) *Caveat:* the 60% "will pay a premium" figure is cited secondhand and not confirmed.
- **95:5 rule [data study / model].** Only about 5% of B2B buyers are in-market at any time (B2B Institute with Prof. John Dawes, Ehrenberg-Bass; Marketing Week). So most content should build **memory** ("the obvious choice when the need arrives"). Hard CTAs suit only a small slice of readers. *Contested* by intent-data vendors.
- **Proof posts** (case studies, client-result breakdowns, "what we did / what changed") are the bridge from authority to buying, in practitioner consensus (Postory agency pillars insight/proof/story/how-to; Barker; Acosta using her own revenue as the case study). No platform dataset isolates proof-post conversion. [practitioner consensus]
- **Offers in posts / how often to sell:** no data study found. Practitioner range: Alić (sell *from* content, rarely *in* it) ↔ Donnelly (a resource + CTA on nearly every post). For a 3-post-a-week solo expert, a defensible default is **one soft CTA in most posts** (a P.S., "DM me X if…", the Featured link) + **one explicit offer post every 2–4 weeks** + **a profile that sells 24/7**. [synthesis]
- **Lead magnets and "comment KEYWORD":** LinkedIn's 2022 feed policy says it will not promote posts that ask for likes or reactions purely to boost reach. LinkedIn's VP of Product Gyanda Sachdeva says pods will be made ineffective: automated comments are removed from "Most Relevant" and may be limited to the commenter's connections, and repeat offenders face restrictions (Social Media Today 804970; Siècle Digital 2025-11-10). A secondary glossary cites a **March 2026** LinkedIn update cutting reach for "comment to agree" prompts. *I did not check this against LinkedIn's own announcement (search budget exhausted).* A vendor study (MagicPost, 684k posts) claims keyword-gated posts lost about half their impressions after late July 2026 [unverified vendor data]. **Recommendation:** offer the resource openly (link in a comment or in Featured, or "DM me if you want it"), not comment-gated. [synthesis]
- **Newsletters:** the LinkedIn newsletter's first-issue invite goes to the whole network (verified), which makes it a one-time distribution event. Owned email (Welsh, Koe, Bloom) is the durable asset. A sensible combination is a LinkedIn newsletter for reach plus a lead magnet to email for ownership. [synthesis]
- **Events and Live:** Live eligibility was historically >150 followers/connections + a good policy record (LinkedIn 2020). The 2026 guide says access comes through the Events tool if you qualify. The registration form captures attendee data. I found no 2025–26 organic performance data for consultants. [verified (old) + secondary]
- **Verification as a conversion lever:** "up to 60% more profile views / 50% more engagement" for ID-verified members (LinkedIn via TechCrunch, Dec 2025). This is a cheap trust signal for a profile that is meant to convert. [verified, company-reported]

## 4. Older audience / professional-services specifics

- **Audience fit:** Pew (2023 survey, pub. Jan 2024): **31% of US adults aged 50–64** and **12% of those 65+** use LinkedIn, vs 40% of those aged 30–49. Pew's Nov 2025 update did not add a new LinkedIn-by-age figure. [data study, US adults]. Buyers of a 45–65 expert are often peers, so the audience is present.
- **LinkedIn's stance on AI content [verified]:**
  - Wired (Nov 2024): LinkedIn (Adam Walkiewicz, head of feed relevance) said it doesn't track AI use. It said it has "robust defenses… to proactively identify low-quality, and exact or near-exact duplicate content… we take action to ensure it is not broadly promoted." He framed AI as useful for beating the blank page, with members' original thoughts as what matters.
  - 2026-07-31 (Fortune; CPO Hari Srinivasan): a **"Seems like AI slop"** report option on posts and comments; "hundreds of thousands" of automated slop comment attempts blocked daily, plus billions of other automation attempts. The flags train the models, and "slop is hard to define". Campaign reports early testing at 94% accuracy in identifying generic content. **Over 1M clicks by 2026-08-25** (Fortune).
  - The "Enhance your post" generative tool was retired and replaced with **Post Proofreader** (proofreads without changing voice). PR Daily: LinkedIn isn't banning AI but separating AI-assisted editing from mass-produced posts.
- **AI content and reach data [data study, conflicting]:**
  - Originality.ai 2024: 8,795 long-form posts (2018–Oct 2024), about 54% likely AI; AI-written posts jumped 189% after ChatGPT launched. Likely-AI posts got **45% less engagement** (2022–24 analysis).
  - Originality.ai 2025: 3,368 posts from 99 influential profiles (Jan–Nov 2025), 53.7% likely AI. **Results split by industry**: likely-AI posts *outperformed* by 75% in leadership/inspiration, but human posts won by 44% in healthcare and 40% in government/public affairs; tech and finance showed about a 7% gap. *Caveat:* a vendor's own detector; correlation only.
  - LigoSocial 2026: 134,522 posts from 1,499 profiles, **within-author** comparison. Posts that read as AI got **22% less engagement** than the same author's human-sounding posts. [vendor study, stronger design]
  - Van der Blom (as reported in a secondary PDF): human-crafted content gets about 34% more reach, and an earlier teaser said AI content gets about 30% less reach. Detection method undisclosed. [unverified]
  - **Interpretation:** the penalty seems to hit *generic* AI text, not AI-assisted posts that carry real specifics. For professional services (closer to healthcare and government than to "inspiration"), human texture is likely worth more. [synthesis]
- **What works for non-"creator" experts aged 45–65 [synthesis, grounded in the above]:**
  - Write like a senior advisor briefing a client, not like a growth creator: fewer hooks and gimmicks, more judgment and specifics.
  - Use the experience moat: "In 22 years I've seen this mistake 100 times…", named eras, industry shifts lived through, failures and recoveries.
  - Prefer proof and story over hot takes. Contrarian is fine when it rests on experience.
  - Use a voice-first capture workflow (interview, voice note, dictation) so the text sounds like the person. Use AI for structure and proofreading only. This mirrors LinkedIn's own Post Proofreader stance.
  - Avoid the "AI tells": em-dash cascades, "Here's the thing:", "Let that sink in", triplet lists of abstractions, broetry spacing on every line, emoji bullets, generic CTA questions.
  - Comment-led presence counts as "posting" for those who hate publishing: 15–25 minutes of thoughtful comments a day builds visibility (Alić, van der Blom).
  - Some practitioners suggest ghostwriting, but with the slop button and authenticity scrutiny, ghostwriting must be interview-based and in the expert's voice. [synthesis]
