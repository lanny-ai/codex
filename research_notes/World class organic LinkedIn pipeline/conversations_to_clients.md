# Conversations to clients: the LinkedIn pipeline (attention -> conversation -> booked call)

Research stream for the Build Room OS organic LinkedIn pipeline skill. Audience: established solo service experts (coaches, consultants, advisors, agencies of one), no ads.
Compiled 2026-10-16.

**How to read the evidence tags**
- **verified**: the claim comes from a LinkedIn-owned page (help center, legal, news, Sales Solutions blog). See the access note below.
- **data study (n = ...)**: vendor or independent dataset with a stated sample. Almost all of these are vendor-run (automation tools, agencies), mostly drawn from *cold, often automated* outreach. Read them as a floor for warm, manual outreach, not as a target.
- **practitioner claim (who)**: one named person's method or experience. Nothing measured.
- **Build Room inference**: my synthesis or recommendation. It is not a sourced fact.

**Access note (important).** The session's egress proxy blocked direct page fetches: linkedin.com, business.linkedin.com, news.linkedin.com and most vendor sites returned 403 or DNS failure. Every claim here comes from search-engine extracts of the cited pages. I did not read any of them in full. "Verified" means a LinkedIn-owned URL was the source of the extract. Re-check the exact numbers on the live pages before you publish anything to customers. The shared web-search budget also ran out before I finished. The gaps it left are listed in section 9.

## What matters most (10 lines)

1. **Warm signals beat cold volume.** LinkedIn's own data: InMail acceptance rises when the prospect recently posted (+45%), changed jobs (+62%), shares a connection (+45%) or follows your company (+270%), and when you viewed their profile first (+78%) (**verified**, 2022). Build the pipeline around people who have already engaged.
2. **Commenting is the cheapest attention engine.** LinkedIn has said that commenting once a week can roughly triple profile views (**verified via secondhand report**). Practitioners converge on 5-10 substantive comments a day on niche posts.
3. **Write comments yourself.** LinkedIn's 2026 feed update targets comment automation, engagement pods and "comment to agree" bait. The help center says LinkedIn may limit the visibility of automated or excessive comments (**verified**).
4. **"Comment KEYWORD for the guide" has stopped working.** One 684k-post study found these posts went from slightly above average to about 50-62% below the author's average reach from late July 2026 (**data study**). LinkedIn's spam policy targets content "designed to artificially increase engagement" (**verified**). Put the offer in the post, Featured section or a DM instead.
5. **A connection note barely changes acceptance but raises replies.** Belkins 2026: 25.3% acceptance with a note vs 27.6% without, but 8.2% vs 5.3% replies (**data study**). Free accounts get very few notes (LinkedIn help says 5 a month), so save them for high-value prospects.
6. **Invitation volume is capped.** About 100 a week is the commonly reported ceiling (not stated by LinkedIn). Withdrawing invites does not restore quota (**verified**). Automation tools can get the account restricted (**verified**).
7. **DMs work when short, about them, and easy to say no to.** LinkedIn InMail data: under 400 characters is about 22% better than average (**verified**, 2021-22 data). Josh Braun teaches permission to say no; Justin Welsh sends 10-12 trigger-based, low-pressure DMs a week (**practitioner claims**).
8. **Follow-up carries half the replies.** In Belkins' connector campaigns, replies split about evenly between first touch and follow-ups. Expandi found 3-message sequences beat 5+ (9.8% vs 5.0%) (**data studies**). Plan two follow-ups, then stop.
9. **Cold benchmarks are a floor.** Acceptance about 28.5%, message reply about 10.4% (Expandi, 13.2M attempts). Only 1.3% of connected prospects booked a meeting (Belkins). Warm, manual, signal-based outreach should do far better. That is an inference, and each expert should measure their own.
10. **SSI no longer works as a free KPI.** Multiple reports say LinkedIn switched the Social Selling Index off for non-Sales Navigator users around 7-8 September 2026 (not confirmed on a LinkedIn page). Track your own weekly funnel in the CRM instead. A first client typically takes months of consistency, not weeks (**practitioner consensus**, no large-sample data).

### 1.2 Who to comment on (practitioner consensus)
- **Niche posts your buyers read.** Justin Welsh recommends "thoughtful comments on five to ten niche posts a day" (**practitioner claim**, Welsh newsletter via MarketNews recap). Jasmin Alić-related material suggests about 10 thoughtful comments on posts *outside* your immediate network (**practitioner claim**, secondhand; daily vs weekly not stated).
- **Build Room inference.** Use a 3-tier list:
  - (a) 10-20 ideal-client accounts (warm-up before any DM)
  - (b) 10-20 peer or complementary experts whose audience is your buyer
  - (c) 3-5 larger niche creators for reach
  Use LinkedIn's bell or notification on (a) and (b). Sales Navigator lead alerts do this for (a) (see section 5).
- **Prefer posts that are already getting comments.** ContentIn's comment-count data and van der Blom's interest-graph framing both point this way (**vendor data / secondhand**).

### 1.3 What a good comment is
- **Welsh's formula** (**practitioner claim**): compliment one specific point, expand with your own insight, ask a thought-provoking question. Comments "valuable enough that they could stand alone as posts".
- **Alić** (**practitioner claim**, secondhand): add your own insight or quick lesson. Save deeper questions for DMs. Use author "mini-post" comments on your own posts to extend the post.
- **Never pitch in comments.** Several guides warn this damages your brand (**practitioner consensus**).
- **Length.** Claims conflict:
  - 5+ words are "rich" (Degraux, LinkedIn Pulse)
  - 15+ words favored (creator post)
  - 10+ words "double reach", 15+ "2.5x" (ConnectSafely citing "Lea"/"LinkHub", no methodology)
  **Contradiction / unverified.** **Build Room rule:** 2-4 sentences (about 25-60 words) with one specific idea. Never generic ("Great post!").
- **Timing.** Comment within the first few hours of a post, not after 48-72 h (Degraux, **practitioner claim**). Van der Blom-attributed "first 5 minutes = 4.2x" is **unverified** (it traces to a mockup).

### 1.4 Replying to comments on your own posts
- **Justin Welsh** (**practitioner claim**, Josh Spector's analysis, not Welsh himself; Breakcold says the same): replies to a batch of comments shortly after publishing, then returns about an hour later. Most replies are short. He cannot answer all of them.
- Don't just thank commenters. Ask a follow-up question on selected comments to open a second exchange. Avoid identical canned replies (**practitioner claims**, LinkedIn Pulse authors).
- **Build Room inference (pipeline link).** A commenter on your post is a warm signal. Reply publicly first. If they match your ideal client and the comment shows the problem, send a connection request (if not connected) or a value-first DM within 24-48 h (see templates D1-D3). Log them in the CRM as `signal:commented-my-post`.

## 2. Connection strategy

### 2.1 Limits and rules
| Item | What we know | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Weekly invitation cap | Not published by LinkedIn. When you hit the limit you can send again "within one week". Withdrawing invites does not lift it. You cannot buy more. | **verified** (Help a550555). The number comes from third parties. |
| Commonly reported number | About 100 a week (some say 80-100 free, up to 200 for older or high-SSI accounts). Rolling 7-day window. | **practitioner/vendor claims**, conflicting (LGM, Findymail, Cleverly 2026-05-04) |
| Personalized notes on free accounts | Help page (per search extract): free accounts can personalize **5 invitations a month**, 200-character limit. Premium is unlimited. | **verified**, but **contradiction**: LGM says 10 a month; others say 5-15 (testing); some report 300 characters. |
| Network cap | 30,000 1st-degree connections | **verified** (Help a567364) |
| Automation | Bots and extensions that automate activity, add contacts or send messages violate User Agreement s.8.2 and can lead to restriction or permanent shutdown. LinkedIn may restrict accounts sending excessive invites if it suspects automation. | **verified** (Help a1341387, a1340522, a551012) |

### 2.2 Note vs no note (acceptance-rate data)
| Study | Acceptance with note | Acceptance without | Reply with note | Reply without | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Belkins 2026 (2025 campaigns) | 25.3% | 27.6% | 8.2% | 5.3% | **data study** (vendor/agency, via Expandi) |
| Belkins 2025 | 26.42% | 26.37% | 9.36% | 5.44% | **data study (20M+ attempts, secondhand)** |
| Expandi A/B (Feb-May 2025) | about 1.28 pt difference | | | | **data study** |
| Waalaxy 2025 e-book | 32.3% | 34.3% | | | **data study** (chart hard to read) |
| La Growth Machine (3 x ~9,000 leads, so ~27k) | 29% (honest sales note) / 32% (vague note) | 31% | | | **data study** |
| "45% vs 15% for personalized vs generic" | | | | | **unsourced secondary claim. Do not use.** |

- **Conclusion.** Five datasets agree that a note does not meaningfully raise acceptance and may lower it slightly. Two show a note lifts replies after acceptance. The "personalization doubles acceptance" claim is unsupported. **Contradiction flagged:** the one 45%/15% claim versus five datasets.
- **Morgan J. Ingram** reportedly argues blank requests outperform personalized ones (**practitioner claim**, secondhand summary).
- Expandi 2026: requests sent 7-11 am accept at about 32% vs 23-24% in the evening. Day of week has minimal impact (**data study**).
- Belkins (2026-06-29, 150k+ contacts): about 62% of acceptances happen within 24 h; by 2 weeks, 91% of eventual acceptors have accepted (**data study**). **Build Room inference:** withdraw invites still pending after about 3 weeks (this does not restore quota). Treat non-acceptance as a "not now".

### 2.3 Who to connect with (Build Room inference built on the warm-signal data)
Priority order:
1. People who engaged with your content (commented, reposted, reacted repeatedly)
2. People who viewed your profile and fit your ideal client
3. Ideal-client people you have commented on for 1-2 weeks
4. Peers and referral partners
5. Event or community co-members
6. Cold ideal-client people (lowest)

The basis is LinkedIn's InMail acceptance lifts for recent activity, profile research and shared connections (**verified**, 2022), and Belkins' finding that Messenger campaigns to existing 1st-degree connections had the highest reply rate, 12.2% (**data study**).

- **Use notes** (free: about 5 a month) only for tier 1-2 people where you can reference the specific signal ("saw your comment on X's post about Y").
- **Accept inbound** from anyone plausibly in or near your market. **Build Room inference:** every inbound acceptance from an ideal-client profile is a warm lead. Send a short no-pitch welcome only if there's a real reason (template C3), otherwise engage with their content first.
- **Pruning.** No LinkedIn data found. **Build Room inference:** pruning matters only near the 30k cap, or to remove obvious spam or pitch-bots that pollute your feed and interest graph. Removing a connection is low-risk but has no proven reach benefit. **Unverified.**

### 2.4 Follow vs connect
- Creator Mode was removed in Feb/Mar 2024, and its tools were opened to all members (**reported**, Social Media Today). Follow can be the primary profile button, but **sources conflict** on the current default and the settings path. Check your own profile.
- **Build Room inference.** A solo expert should keep **Connect** as primary while their network is under about 5-10k. First-degree connections can receive DMs, voice notes and messages without InMail credits (voice notes are 1st-degree only, **verified**). Followers can't be DM'd for free. Switch to Follow-primary only if inbound requests are unmanageable.

## 3. DM conversion: engagement -> conversation -> call

### 3.1 Social Selling Index (SSI): pillars and status
- **Pillars** (**verified**, LinkedIn Sales blog / Sales Solutions; each 0-25, total 0-100, updated daily):
  - establish your professional brand
  - find the right people
  - engage with insights
  - build relationships
- **Status, October 2026.** Multiple third-party reports say LinkedIn moved SSI behind Sales Navigator from mid-2025 and switched it off for free and Premium users on about 7-8 September 2026. Reported lockout text: "You do not have access to SSI". Sales Navigator users reportedly still see it. One blog quotes LinkedIn saying SSI "no longer accurately reflects the modern sales environment".
  - Evidence: **practitioner/vendor reports** (Duperrin 2026-08-17; LinkedScore; Rafał Szymański; John Espirian on Threads). **Not confirmed on a LinkedIn page.**
  - **Contradiction:** several 2025-26 vendor explainers still describe it as free.
- **Build Room implication.** Keep the four pillars as a *behavioral checklist*: profile, targeting, insight-led engagement, relationships. Do not use SSI as a KPI. Track the funnel in section 6 instead.

### 3.2 Warm-signal outreach (what LinkedIn's data says)
From LinkedIn's analysis of InMails sent in 2022 (**verified**, LinkedIn Sales blog; LinkedIn's correlational data, no methodology published). Acceptance is higher when:
- the sender viewed the prospect's profile and sent within 30 days (+78%; another LinkedIn post says +86%, **internal contradiction**)
- the prospect follows the sender's company (+270%)
- the prospect started a new job within 90 days (+62%)
- the prospect posted in the past 3 months (+45%)
- the two share at least one connection (+45%) or a school (+37%)
- the sender has a complete profile (+87%)

Acceptance is consistent across days of the week.

LinkedIn Talent data (May 2021-Apr 2022, tens of millions of InMails; **verified**):
- InMails under 400 characters get about 22% higher response than average
- naming a common former employer gives +27% response
- prospects already connected to someone at your company are 46% more likely to accept

LinkedIn Sales Solutions also recommends keeping copy "under 500 characters, or 100 words". **Minor contradiction** with the 400-character figure.

LinkedIn's sales blog also publishes "10 trigger events" (job change, profile view, etc.) and advises treating a profile view as a sales trigger (**verified**, advice not data).

**Trigger events for a solo expert** (Build Room inference built on the above):
- commented on or reposted your post
- commented on a peer's post about the problem you solve
- viewed your profile and fits your ideal client (free accounts see only the most recent 3-5 viewers over 90 days; **contradiction** between help pages)
- new role within 90 days
- posted about a problem you solve
- accepted your connection request
- joined or attended the same event

### 3.4 Follow-up timing and sequence length
- Belkins 2026 (**data study**):
  - connector campaigns: reply rate splits about evenly between first-touch and follow-ups
  - overall funnel: 18.7% accept -> 17.6% of connected reply -> 1.3% of connected book a meeting
  - email: follow-ups produce 58.6% of replies
- Expandi H2 2026 (2,966 campaigns, **data study**):
  - 3-message campaigns reply at 9.8%; 5+ messages fall to 5.0%
  - H1 2026: the 2nd follow-up adds about 4%, with diminishing returns after the 3rd
  - 151-200 characters is the reply peak (8.5%), but relevance matters more
  - Expandi's suggested cadence (advice, not measured): first message 24-48 h after acceptance, then 4-7 days between messages
- **Contradiction.** A 2025 recruiting Messenger dataset shows replies rising up to 4 follow-ups (19.3% -> 28.6%). That is a different context and audience.
- Belkins (via Overloop): Tuesday is the best reply day (6.90%) and Saturday the worst (6.40%). The difference is negligible (**data study, secondhand**).
- **Build Room rule:**
  1. Message 1 within 24-48 h of the signal.
  2. Follow-up 1 after 3-5 days, adding a new piece of value.
  3. Follow-up 2 after 7-10 days, as a permission-to-close message.
  4. Then stop and move to nurture (keep engaging with their content).

### 3.5 Voice notes and video in DMs
- Voice messages are **verified**: mobile app only, 1st-degree connections and group messages, max 1 minute (Help a548233).
- Evidence on effect:
  - Barrows/"Morgan": 40% response, n = 125 (**practitioner claim**)
  - SalesRobot: video 82% / voice 59% / text 27% (**vendor claim, no methodology, promotes its own AI voice-clone tool. Do not use.**)
- **Build Room inference.** Use voice notes as the 2nd touch *after* someone has replied, or for warm signals (commented on your post). Avoid them as a cold first touch. Stay under 45 s. Say their name, cite the specific signal, ask one easy question. Never use AI voice clones; it's inauthentic and conflicts with the automation rules.

## 4. "Comment X to get Y" keyword lead magnets

- **LinkedIn policy** (**verified**):
  - Professional Community Policies: "Don't do things to artificially increase engagement with your content."
  - Spam help page (a1338787): LinkedIn "may remove or limit the distribution of content designed to artificially increase engagement through misuse or misrepresentation of its features". Examples listed include emoji or reaction polls, "chain letter"-type requests for likes or shares, and excessive or repetitive comments.
  - 2022 "Mythbusting the Feed": LinkedIn "may choose to not broadly promote low-quality content, such as engagement bait".
  - 2026 feed update: reducing "comment to agree" prompts.
  - **Note:** no LinkedIn page I reached names "comment KEYWORD for the PDF" specifically.
- **Performance data:** MagicPost, 684,000 posts, June 2025 to September 2026 (**data study**, vendor; each post compared with the same author's posts ±30 days):
  - keyword posts did slightly better than the author's average until July 2026
  - from the week of 27 July 2026 they reach about half as many people; extracts say "52% fewer impressions" and "62% fewer people", an **internal inconsistency to check**
  - the same creators' other posts fell only about 7%
  - comments fell back to average
  - share of tracked posts using the tactic fell from about 1.5-1.9% a month (Oct 2025-Jul 2026) to 0.5% (Sep 2026)
  - cause unknown, correlational
- **Practitioner/vendor view.** The tactic historically worked because comments drive distribution. Vendors now warn that gating with no real asset is engagement bait.
- **Verdict for Build Room (inference):** stop using keyword-comment gating as the primary lead-magnet mechanic in late 2026. Alternatives, from least to most friction:
  1. Ask a genuine question in the post (MagicPost: neutral, no penalty)
  2. "If you want the checklist, DM me 'checklist'" or "reply and I'll send it". This moves the conversation to DMs without inflating comments. Not studied; **unverified** whether it's treated differently.
  3. Put the lead magnet in your Featured section and profile, and mention it in the post
  4. Link to the opt-in page, in the post or first comment (link-reach effects **not researched here**)
  5. Newsletter subscribe
  6. Warm-signal DM to people who engaged with a related post (template D2)

### 5.2 Features that matter for a solo expert
- **Lead and account lists, saved searches, alerts** (job changes, posts, news) and Advanced Search filters: all in Core (**verified**, Help a104003 and the Sales Navigator product page). 10k saved leads and accounts.
- **InMail:** 50 credits a month. Unused credits roll over to a 150 maximum. A credit comes back for each InMail answered within 90 days (**verified**, Help a101030 / a543695).
- **Relationship Explorer:** surfaces decision-makers, job changes and recent posts (**verified** description). Whether Core includes Account IQ / Lead IQ (AI insights) is **unconfirmed**.
- **SSI access** reportedly survives only for Sales Navigator users (see 3.1, **unverified**).
- **Ingram-style workflow** (**practitioner claim**): filter saved searches by "posted on LinkedIn in past 30 days", "following your company", "viewed your profile" and tenure windows, then run 15 minutes a day on the alerts.

### 5.3 Is it worth it? (Build Room inference)
- **Yes, once** the expert posts consistently, has a defined ideal-client list (titles, industry, geography), and is out of free search allowance. The free account has a monthly "commercial use limit" on people searches and profile browsing (resets on the 1st, exact number not disclosed; **verified**, Help a564226). It is worth it when outreach is a weekly habit: about 10-20 warm-signal DMs plus list maintenance.
  - Break-even maths: about $90-120 a month against one client worth $2k-$20k+. It pays for itself if it produces about one extra client a year. The real cost is time.
- **Not yet** if the expert isn't posting, has no clear ideal client, or will not do the daily 15-minute alert routine.
- **Free alternatives:**
  - Boolean search on free LinkedIn, within the commercial use limit
  - "Who viewed your profile" (free: last 90 days, few viewers shown)
  - notification bells on 20-50 key people
  - LinkedIn's built-in Saved posts
  - a manual "warm list" in the CRM
  - Google alerts for triggers (Welsh's method)
  - Premium Business at about $70 a month for full profile-viewer history (365 days), unlimited personalized notes and some InMails, as a middle tier
  - Scraping extensions and Google X-ray "workarounds" may violate the terms (**verified** prohibition on scraping tools).

**Diagnosis rules** (Build Room inference):
- Low profile views: comment volume or quality problem.
- Views up but few accepts or follows: profile or headline problem.
- Accepts but no replies: message problem (too long, too self-focused, no signal referenced).
- Replies but no calls: no clear, low-friction next step, or asked too early.
- Calls but no clients: offer or sales-call problem. Hand off to sales coaching.

### 6.3 Realistic timelines
- No large-sample data exists (**gap**). Practitioner and blog consensus:
  - "months of consistency, not a viral week" (Coachway)
  - first paid clients around months 7-12 and steady income at 6-18 months for consultants starting from zero (Escape the Cubicle; blog projection)
  - Welsh's first post got 12 views; a year later posts got about 30k (NineTwoThree)
- 30-60-day "first 5 clients" promises come from course sellers (survivorship bias).
- **Build Room inference for *established* experts** (existing reputation, offer and network): warm-network activation plus warm-signal DMs can produce calls in weeks 1-4. Content-driven inbound typically takes 3-6+ months of consistent posting and commenting. Set expectations on two clocks: outbound-warm (weeks) and inbound-content (months).

## 8. Templates (with evidence basis)

Wording is Build Room drafting. Each template cites the principle or data it rests on, and none is a verbatim quote of a source. Placeholders are in [brackets]. Keep DMs under about 400 characters where possible (LinkedIn InMail data). Connection notes max 200 characters (free).

### Comment templates
**K1. Add-a-layer comment** (Welsh formula: specific compliment + own insight + question; **practitioner claim**)
> "[Specific line from post] is the part most people skip. In my work with [client type] the version of this that bites is [concrete example/number]. Curious: do you see [X] or [Y] more often?"

**K2. Respectful contrarian / nuance** (Alić: add your own lesson; stand-alone-post quality; **practitioner claim**)
> "Agree on [point]. One nuance from [N years doing X]: it flips when [condition]. We saw that with a [client type] who [brief result]."

**K3. Mini-framework comment** (comments "valuable enough to stand alone as posts", Welsh)
> "Three things that made this work for my clients: 1) [..] 2) [..] 3) [..]. #2 is the one people underrate."

**K4. Reply on your own post: open a second exchange** (practitioner advice: ask a follow-up, don't just thank; Welsh replies in the first hour)
> "Thanks [Name]. When you hit [their mentioned issue], what did you try first?"

**Avoid:** "Great post!", emoji-only, pitches, AI-generated comments, pods (LinkedIn 2026 feed update; Help a524166; **verified**).

### Connection request templates
**C1. Blank request** to tier 3-6 cold or semi-warm people. Data: a note doesn't raise acceptance (5 datasets); Ingram favors blank; free note quota is about 5 a month.

**C2. Signal-referenced note** (tier 1-2 only; Belkins: notes lift replies 8.2% vs 5.3%; LinkedIn: referencing commonality lifts response)
> "Hi [Name], enjoyed your comment on [Person]'s post about [topic], especially [specific]. I work on the same problem with [client type]. Would be good to be connected."

**C3. Accepting an inbound request (only when they fit your ideal client)** (Braun: low pressure, permission to say no; Welsh: recipient-focused)
> "Thanks for connecting, [Name]. Saw you're [role] at [company]. What's taking most of your attention right now on [relevant area]? No agenda, I just like knowing who's here."

### DM templates (engagement -> conversation)
**D1. Commented on my post** (warm signal; LinkedIn: recent activity +45%; message within 24-48 h, Expandi cadence advice)
> "Hi [Name], thanks for your comment on my [topic] post. You mentioned [their point]. I'm curious, is that something you're dealing with at [company] now, or more an observation?"

**D2. Engaged with a related topic. Offer the asset in DMs instead of comment-gating** (MagicPost data on keyword bait; Welsh: offer before ask)
> "[Name], noticed you've been in a few threads on [problem]. I put together a short [checklist/teardown] on [specific outcome]. Want me to send it over?"

**D3. Trigger event: new role, post or announcement** (LinkedIn: new job in 90 days +62%; Welsh trigger method)
> "Congrats on the [new role / launch], [Name]. In the first 90 days most [role]s I work with run into [specific problem]. Is that on your radar yet, or too early?"

**D4. Profile viewer who fits your ideal client** (LinkedIn: treat profile views as a trigger, **verified** advice)
> "Hi [Name], saw you stopped by my profile. Appreciate that. Anything in particular around [your topic] you're working through? Happy to point you to something useful."

### DM templates (conversation -> call)
**D5. Earned call ask, after 2+ exchanges** (Ingram: "sell the meeting, not the product"; Braun: permission to say no)
> "Sounds like [restate their problem in their words]. I've helped [similar client] with exactly that. Would a 20-minute call to map your options be useful? If not, no problem, happy to keep swapping notes here."

**D6. Problem-poke question when the pain is unclear** (Braun "poke the bear"; **practitioner claim**)
> "Out of curiosity, how are you handling [specific problem] today? Most [client type] I talk to either [approach A] or [approach B], and both have trade-offs."

### Follow-up templates (max 2; Expandi: 3-message sequences beat 5+)
**F1. Day 3-5: add value, don't "bump"**
> "[Name], one more thought on [topic]: [one-line insight or link to a relevant post/case]. No need to reply if it's not timely."

**F2. Day 7-10: permission to close** (Braun: honest no beats fake yes)
> "Haven't heard back, so I'll assume [topic] isn't a priority right now. Totally fine. If it changes, just reply here."

**V1. Voice note, 30-45 s, to 1st-degree connections after a reply or a warm signal** (Help a548233; Barrows/"Morgan" 40% response, n = 125; Ingram 10-30-10)
> "[Name] — [why you're messaging: their comment/post, 10s]. [One insight tied to their situation, ~20-30s]. [Easy question, e.g. 'Is that close to what you're seeing?']"

## 9. Contradictions and gaps

**Contradictions**
1. **Note vs no note.** Five datasets show little or no acceptance lift (or a slight drop). One unsourced claim says 45% vs 15%. Notes lift replies in two datasets.
2. **Free personalized-note quota.** The help page says 5 a month. Third parties say 10, or 5-15 (testing). Character limit 200 vs 300.
3. **Sales Navigator price.** Two LinkedIn pages differ: Core $119.99 vs $99.99/mo.
4. **InMail length.** Under 400 characters (+22%) vs "under 500 characters / 100 words"; another LinkedIn analysis says 201-400 characters is +16%.
5. **Profile-view lift on InMail acceptance.** +78% vs +86% (two LinkedIn posts).
6. **Who viewed your profile (free).** Last 3 vs last 5 viewers.
7. **Comment length thresholds.** 5+ vs 10+ vs 15+ words; none has a methodology.
8. **Follow-up count.** 3 messages best (Expandi sales) vs up to 4 follow-ups rising (recruiting dataset).
9. **SSI status.** Reported off for non-Sales Navigator users from Sept 2026 vs vendor explainers still calling it free. No LinkedIn confirmation found.
10. **MagicPost keyword-bait penalty size.** "52% fewer impressions" vs "62% fewer people" in extracts from the same study.
11. **Belkins acceptance.** 26.4% (2025) vs 18.7% (2026) vs 25-27% (2026 note split). Different samples and definitions.
12. **Premium Business price.** $59.99 vs $69.99/mo.
