You are a hiring architect for small service businesses, operating
with the full knowledge base I just provided. You understand:

- The Scorecard (Smart & Street): mission, 3-5 measurable outcomes,
  competencies — and the Scorecard → Source → Select → Sell sequence
- Performance-based hiring (Adler): performance objectives over
  skill lists, and the single-accomplishment deep-dive
- Structured interviewing: same questions, behavioral STAR format,
  a written rubric before the interview, independent scoring before
  discussion (Kahneman's principle)
- Work samples as the most predictive selection tool, drawn from
  a real SOP and scored against its Definition of Done
- Topgrading's chronological walk and the "what will your manager
  say when we call" question, followed by mandatory reference calls
- The five reasons owners don't hire, and the answer to each
- The seven-part filter ad and the five-stage selection funnel
- Matching competencies to the role's variance: consistency for
  low-variance roles, judgment for high-variance ones

Your standards for this session:
- The role is built from the member's documented SOPs — never from
  a wish list. If they want to hire for something undocumented,
  say so and send them back to the SOP Creator for that process.
- No ad gets written before the scorecard exists
- Every outcome is measurable and has a timeframe; every duty gets
  rewritten as a result
- The ad's "not for you if" section is honest, specific, and
  written to make the wrong people leave
- Every interview question ties to a named competency and has a
  1 / 3 / 5 rubric written in observable behavior
- The work sample comes from a real SOP and is paid if it exceeds
  an hour
- No mini-founder language: you are hiring an operator of a system,
  and the competencies say so

Your output today is a complete hiring kit: scorecard, ad, screening
question, work sample with rubric, structured interview guide with
scoring, reference-call script, decision matrix, offer, and a first-
week plan that plugs into the four-run ramp from Week 1.

Ask ONE question at a time. Show recommendations before asking
open-ended questions.

PROMPT 1 — ROLE DEFINITION + SCORECARD
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Paste this after your System Prompt and Input Template:

---

Using the knowledge base and my SOPs from last week, define the role
and build its scorecard. Do not write the ad yet.

━━ THE BUNDLE ━━

Group my documented processes into a role. Rules:
- One role, one skill family. If my SOPs span two families
  (e.g., client delivery AND bookkeeping), tell me — and recommend
  which bundle to hire for first and why.
- Show the hours/month this bundle represents (from my numbers)
  and whether that supports part-time, full-time, or a contractor.
- Flag any process I want to hand off that ISN'T documented yet.
  It does not go in this role until it has an SOP.

━━ CONTRACTOR vs EMPLOYEE ━━

Based on the bundle, hours, and my budget, recommend one — with the
practical trade-offs for a business my size. If I said "not sure,"
make the call and explain it. (Note: employment classification rules
vary by location — tell me to confirm with a local accountant.)

━━ THE SCORECARD ━━

ROLE TITLE: [plain, searchable, honest]

MISSION (one sentence — why this role exists):

OUTCOMES (3-5, measurable, with a timeframe — results, not activities):
1. By [day/month N]: [specific, verifiable result]
2. ...
Derive each from an SOP's Definition of Done. Show me which SOP
each outcome comes from.

COMPETENCIES (5-8):
Split them:
   SYSTEM COMPETENCIES — what it takes to run these SOPs reliably
   ESCALATION COMPETENCIES — the judgment to know when reality
      doesn't match the document, and to ask instead of guess
   CULTURE COMPETENCIES — derived directly from "the person I'd hate
      to hire," inverted
For each: one sentence on what it looks like in THIS role.

VARIANCE CALL:
Is this a low-variance role (hire for consistency) or high-variance
(hire for judgment)? Use the Variance scores from my triage. This
decision shapes the ad and the interview.

━━ THE 90-DAY STANDARD ━━

Restate the outcomes as a 90-day review: what must be true at day 90
for this hire to be "working out"? This is the sentence I read on
day 90 before I decide anything.

━━ WHAT THIS ROLE IS NOT ━━

Three things a candidate might assume this role includes that it
doesn't. Be specific — this feeds the ad.

PROMPT 2 — THE HIRING AD
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

---

Now write the ad from the scorecard. Use the seven-part structure
from the knowledge base. This is a filter, not a pitch — I want fewer,
better applicants.

━━ THE AD ━━

[TITLE]

[THE ONE-PARAGRAPH TRUTH — what we do, who we serve, why this role
exists now. Written like a person, in my voice, no corporate language.]

WHAT YOU'LL OWN
[3-5 bullets — the scorecard outcomes in plain language. Results.]

WHAT A WEEK LOOKS LIKE
[The actual processes, tools, hours, and rhythm. Concrete enough
that a reader can picture Tuesday. Mention that every process is
documented — a real SOP with a checklist — because that is a genuine
selling point for the right person.]

THIS IS FOR YOU IF
[3-4 bullets, specific]

THIS IS NOT FOR YOU IF
[3-4 bullets — honest about the repetitive, deadline-bound, or
unglamorous parts. Derived from "the person I'd hate to hire."]

THE PRACTICAL PART
Hours · Pay range (state it) · Contractor/employee · Time zone
requirement · Start date · Trial structure (a paid 4-6 week trial
scoped to [SOP name], with the outcomes stated)

HOW TO APPLY
[Instructions + THE SCREENING QUESTION]

━━ THE SCREENING QUESTION ━━

Write one question that:
- Can only be answered well by someone who read the whole ad
- Reveals whether they can do a slice of the actual work
- Takes them 5-10 minutes and takes me 30 seconds to evaluate
Then give me the 3 things a strong answer contains and the 2 things
that mean "cut."

━━ WHERE TO POST ━━

Ranked for a role like this in a business like mine: my network
first (give me the exact message to send to 10 people), then the
specific marketplaces or boards that fit, and what to avoid.

━━ THE AD AUDIT ━━

Scan your own ad and show me:
- Any duty that snuck in where an outcome should be
- Any "mini-founder" language (initiative, self-starter, wears many
  hats, entrepreneurial) — rewrite it for an operator role
- Anything vague enough that the wrong person would apply anyway

PROMPT 3 — SCREENING CALL + PAID WORK SAMPLE
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

---

Build stages 1-3 of the selection funnel.

━━ STAGE 1: APPLICATION SCREEN ━━

A 2-minute scoring sheet for every application:
[ ] Answered the screening question (and how well: 1 / 3 / 5)
[ ] Followed the application instructions exactly
[ ] Writing is clear and specific
[ ] [2-3 role-specific checks]
Decision rule: advance if [threshold]. Expect to cut 60-80% here.

━━ STAGE 2: SCREENING CALL (15-20 min) ━━

Write the script. Open with 2 sentences on the role. Then the four
questions from the knowledge base, adapted to this role, each with:
- The question as I'll say it
- What a strong answer sounds like
- The red flag
Close with the "we will call your references" line and the exact
words for the Topgrading prediction question.
End with a decision rule for who advances.

━━ STAGE 3: THE PAID WORK SAMPLE ━━

Pick the SOP that best represents the role. Design the task:

THE BRIEF (what I send the candidate):
- The SOP itself, its checklist, and any sample inputs they need
- The task, scoped to 1-2 hours
- What to submit and by when
- The pay (state it — pro-rated to the role's rate)
- One instruction that tests escalation: include a deliberate gap
  or ambiguity in the inputs and see whether they ask, guess, or
  flag it. Tell me what the gap is.

THE SCORING RUBRIC (what I use — they don't see it):
For each of the SOP's Definition of Done criteria: met / partly / not
Plus:
- Followed the checklist? (evidence)
- Time taken vs. the SOP's estimate
- How they handled the deliberate gap: asked / flagged / guessed / missed
- Quality of the questions they asked, if any
Decision rule: advance to interview if [threshold].

━━ WHAT THE WORK SAMPLE WILL TELL ME THAT THE INTERVIEW CAN'T ━━

Two sentences. I want to remember why this stage is not optional.

PROMPT 4 — THE STRUCTURED INTERVIEW KIT
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

---

Build the interview I'll run with my top 2-3 candidates. 45-60
minutes. Same questions for everyone, in this order. I score on
paper before I form an opinion.

━━ INTERVIEW GUIDE ━━

OPENING (3 min)
[What I say — frames the role, the structure, and that I'll be
taking notes and scoring]

PART 1: THE ACCOMPLISHMENT DEEP-DIVE (15 min) — Adler
"What is the single most significant accomplishment in your work
life, and walk me through it?"
Then the follow-up ladder — write 8-10 probes in order:
situation → your specific role → the hard part → what you did
personally vs. others → results with numbers → what you'd do
differently → what you learned. Tell me what I'm listening for.

PART 2: THE CHRONOLOGICAL WALK (15 min) — Topgrading
For their last 2 roles, the five questions, with the exact wording
of the manager-prediction question. Tell me how to take notes so
the reference calls are easy.

PART 3: BEHAVIORAL QUESTIONS BY COMPETENCY (15-20 min)
For EACH competency on the scorecard:
- One "tell me about a time when..." question
- The STAR probes I use if they stay vague
- What a 1, 3, and 5 look like, in observable terms
Include at least one question that tests ESCALATION JUDGMENT
directly: a time they hit something the process didn't cover.

PART 4: THEIR QUESTIONS + THE SELL (5-8 min)
What I'm listening for in their questions. Then the 3 honest,
specific reasons this role is good for the right person.

━━ THE SCORING SHEET ━━

One page. Every competency, a 1-5 box, a "evidence" line, and a
rule at the top: SCORE BEFORE YOU DISCUSS. Include an overall
"hire / no hire / hire with reservations" at the bottom that I
fill in LAST.

━━ RED FLAGS ━━

The 6-8 signals specific to THIS role that should stop a hire
regardless of score — with the difference between "worth probing"
and "walk away."

━━ THE BIAS CHECK ━━

Three questions I ask myself before deciding, written to catch the
similarity bias, halo effect, and "great conversation" trap.

PROMPT 5 — REFERENCES, DECISION, OFFER + FIRST WEEK
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Run this once, at the end.

---

Finish the kit — everything from the last interview to the first
Monday.

━━ THE REFERENCE CALL SCRIPT (15 min, 2 calls minimum) ━━

- How I open (brief, and what I'm confirming)
- "They told me you'd say [X] — how does that match your view?"
- "On a scale of 1-10, how would you rate their performance?"
- "What would have made it a 10?"
- Two questions specific to my scorecard competencies
- The escalation question: "Tell me about a time they hit something
  they didn't know how to handle. What did they do?"
- The close: "Would you hire them again? For what kind of role?"
What I'm listening for, and the answers that change my decision.

━━ THE DECISION MATRIX ━━

A table: candidates down the side; the scorecard competencies, work
sample score, and reference signal across the top. The rule for
reading it: gut gets the last vote, not a veto over the numbers.
Write the sentence I say to myself if my gut and the matrix disagree.

━━ THE OFFER ━━

Write the offer message in my voice. Include: the role, the trial
structure and its stated outcomes, pay, hours, start date, tools
they'll get access to, and what week one looks like. Warm, clear,
no legalese. (Remind me that the actual agreement should be reviewed
for my location.)

━━ THE FIRST WEEK ━━

Plug into the four-run ramp from Week 1 for each SOP in the bundle:
   Day 1: [access, the Business File context they need, the SOPs
           and checklists, the 📹 recordings, who to ask]
   Days 2-5: which SOP runs when, and which ramp stage each is at
   Friday: the first check-in — the 3 questions I ask
Include what I must have ready BEFORE Monday, as a checklist.

━━ THE 30 / 60 / 90 ━━

Three short check-ins, each with: what must be true by now (from
the scorecard), the 2 questions I ask, and the decision available
at that point.

━━ THE HOURS COMING BACK ━━

Restate the Rent Calculator math: hours/month this role takes off
me at full ramp, what that costs, what it frees. Name the one
Entrepreneur-role thing I should do with the first 10 hours back.
