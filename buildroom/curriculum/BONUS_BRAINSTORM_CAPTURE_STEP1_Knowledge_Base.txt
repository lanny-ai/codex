SECTION 1: THE CORE TRUTH ABOUT BRAINSTORMS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Every owner has had the great session. The whiteboard is full, the
energy is high, three ideas would change the business. Two weeks
later nobody can say what was decided, the photo of the whiteboard
is in someone's camera roll, and the ideas are back in the owner's
head — where they were charging rent before the session started.

The truth:

A BRAINSTORM THAT PRODUCES IDEAS HAS NOT PRODUCED ANYTHING.
A BRAINSTORM THAT PRODUCES OWNED, DATED, DEFINED OUTCOMES HAS.

The gap between the two is not creativity. It is capture. The
session's value is decided in the thirty minutes after it ends,
when the raw material is either turned into records that can be
scheduled and built — or isn't.

The second truth, from decades of decision research:

DECISIONS DECAY UNLESS THEY ARE WRITTEN WITH THEIR REASONS.

Software teams learned this the hard way and invented the
Architecture Decision Record: one page per decision — context,
decision, consequences. Not because engineers love paperwork, but
because a decision without its reasoning gets re-litigated every
time a new person asks "why do we do it this way?" The same is
true of every business decision made on a whiteboard.

The third truth, and the one that connects this to the rest of the
Build Room:

MOST BRAINSTORM OUTCOMES ARE PROTO-PROCESSES.

"We should follow up with every lead within an hour" is not an
idea. It is an SOP that hasn't been written yet: it has a trigger,
steps, a definition of done, and exceptions. When you capture it in
that shape from the start, it drops straight into the SOP Creator
and the Operations sequence. When you capture it as a bullet, it
has to be reinvented later.

SECTION 2: THE THREE MASTER FRAMEWORKS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

FRAMEWORK 1 — THE DECISION RECORD (Nygard's ADR, simplified)
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

One record per outcome, five fields:

   CONTEXT     — what situation or problem this responds to. One
                 or two sentences. Written so someone who wasn't
                 in the room understands why it came up.
   DECISION    — what was decided, in the active voice. "We will…"
   REASONS     — why this over the alternatives that were on the
                 board. Name the alternatives. This is the field
                 that stops re-litigation.
   CONSEQUENCES— what changes as a result: what gets built, what
                 stops, what it costs, what it risks.
   STATUS      — proposed / accepted / superseded / parked.

A record with a blank REASONS field is a wish, not a decision.

FRAMEWORK 2 — THE OUTCOME TAXONOMY
──────────────────────────────────

Not every idea is the same kind of thing, and the kind determines
what gets built and where it goes in the Business File. Five types:

   DECISION    — a choice about direction that changes nothing
                 physical by itself. "We're dropping the $500
                 tier." → Decision record only. Updates the
                 relevant section (§3 here).
   PROCESS     — a repeated behavior with a trigger. "Every lead
                 gets a call within an hour." → Proto-SOP. Feeds §9
                 via the SOP Creator.
   BUILD       — something to be made: a page, a tool, a document,
                 an automation, a template. "A calculator that
                 shows what your undocumented processes cost." →
                 Build brief. Feeds §13 and, when built, the
                 section it serves.
   EXPERIMENT  — a test with a hypothesis, a metric, and an end
                 date. "Try a Tuesday webinar for four weeks." →
                 Experiment card. Feeds §12 as a metric to watch.
   PARKED      — a real idea whose time is not now. → §13 Parked,
                 with a reason and a revisit date. Parked is a
                 status, not a graveyard.

The single most common capture failure is filing a PROCESS as a
DECISION. "We'll respond faster" is filed as a decision, nobody
writes the SOP, and nothing changes. When an outcome contains the
word "every" or "whenever" or "each time," it is a process.

FRAMEWORK 3 — THE PROTO-SOP (from the SOP Creator, Week 1)
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

A process outcome is captured in the same shape the SOP Creator
uses, so nothing is lost in the handoff:

   TRIGGER          — the event that starts it
   OWNER            — one name
   STEPS            — the 3-7 steps as the room described them
                      (rough is fine; the SOP Creator refines)
   DEFINITION OF DONE — how you know it's finished
   EXCEPTIONS       — what the room said about "unless…"
   FREQUENCY        — how often it will run (for the rent math)

A build outcome is captured as a BUILD BRIEF:

   WHAT IT IS       — one sentence a stranger understands
   WHO IT'S FOR     — the member, a client, a prospect, the team
   THE JOB IT DOES  — the outcome, not the feature list
   INPUTS           — what it needs to exist first (data, copy,
                      a section of the Business File)
   DONE LOOKS LIKE  — the observable finished state
   SIZE             — hour / day / week (the room's honest guess)
   FIRST STEP       — the one action that starts it, with an owner

SECTION 3: THE PSYCHOLOGY OF WHY IDEAS DIE
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

THE PLANNING FALLACY. The room believes the idea will be built
because it is obviously good. Obviously good ideas compete with
client work, and client work has a deadline. An idea without a
date is scheduled for never.

THE ZEIGARNIK LEAK. Unfinished ideas stay in working memory and
generate a low hum of guilt. Owners respond by either avoiding the
list or holding a new brainstorm that produces more unfinished
ideas. Capture with a status and a revisit date closes the loop
even for parked items — the mind lets go of what is written down
with a plan.

THE OWNERSHIP DIFFUSION. "We should…" has no subject. In a room of
three, "we" means nobody. Every outcome gets one name, even when
the answer is the owner's own.

THE ENTHUSIASM DISCOUNT. Ideas captured in the session's energy
are overscoped. The capture step happens after the session, in a
cooler register, and asks: what is the smallest version of this
that is still worth doing?

THE WHITEBOARD PHOTO. A photo is not a record. It cannot be
searched, has no owner, and is not read again. The capture is the
record; the photo is the source.

SECTION 4: WHAT WORLD-CLASS OPERATORS DO DIFFERENTLY
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

THEY CAPTURE WITHIN THE HOUR. Amazon's meeting culture, Pixar's
braintrust notes, the ADR — every durable practice writes the
outcome while the reasoning is still warm. The reasons are the
first thing forgotten.

THEY SEPARATE DIVERGE FROM DECIDE FROM CAPTURE. Three modes, three
moments. Capturing during the brainstorm kills divergence.
Deciding during capture re-opens the argument. Capture records
what was decided; if the record reveals a decision wasn't actually
made, it says so and lists the question.

THEY SORT BY TYPE BEFORE THEY SORT BY PRIORITY. A process, a build,
and a decision can't be ranked against each other. Type first,
then priority within type.

THEY ROUTE EVERYTHING TO ONE PLACE. Not a Notion page for builds, a
Slack thread for decisions, and a spreadsheet for experiments. One
Build Plan, in the Business File, that the navigator reads.

THEY KEEP THE PARKING LOT HONEST. Every parked idea has a reason
and a revisit date. When the date comes, it is either scheduled or
deleted. The parking lot is reviewed, not accumulated.

THEY CONNECT OUTCOMES TO THE CONSTRAINT. When a Weekly Ops
Dashboard exists (§12), the capture asks of every build and
experiment: does this address the current constraint? If not, it
is not wrong — but it is not first.

SECTION 5: THE MOST COMMON CAPTURE FAILURES
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

1. THE TRANSCRIPT DUMP. Pasting the whole session into the Build
   Plan. The file balloons; nothing is findable. Capture distills.

2. THE BULLET LIST. Twelve one-line ideas with no type, owner,
   or next step. Looks organized. Is a whiteboard photo in text.

3. INVENTED DECISIONS. The capture "helpfully" resolves what the
   room left open. Now the record says something nobody decided.
   Open questions are recorded as open questions.

4. THE MISSING ALTERNATIVE. The decision is recorded without what
   it was chosen over. Six weeks later: "why didn't we just…?"

5. EVERYTHING IS PRIORITY ONE. Capture that ranks nothing has
   scheduled nothing. Three things get a next step this month.
   The rest are scheduled later or parked — explicitly.

6. THE ORPHAN BUILD. A build brief with no section of the Business
   File it serves. If it serves nothing in the file, ask whether
   it serves the business.

SECTION 6: THE INPUT FORMATS THIS TOOL READS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

The raw material arrives in one of four shapes. Each is handled
the same way — extract, classify, interview the gaps — but the
extraction differs:

THE BRAINSTORM BOARD EXPORT. The Build Room's Brainstorm Board page
exports a structured block: the framing question, every idea with
its cluster and vote count, and the outcome cards the room filled
in. This is the cleanest input; votes and clusters are already
signals. Trust the votes for priority, not for type.

A TRANSCRIPT. A call recording's transcript or a Cowork session
log. Noisy. Decisions are often implicit ("yeah let's do that").
Extract every "let's," "we should," "what if," and "we'll" and
treat each as a candidate. Attribute to speakers where possible.

NOTES. Someone's typed or handwritten notes. Compressed; reasons
usually missing. The interview step does most of the work.

THE OWNER'S MEMORY. "We had a good session yesterday, here's what I
remember." Least reliable, most common. Extract, then interview
harder — especially for what was decided against.

SECTION 7: WHERE OUTCOMES GO IN THE BUSINESS FILE
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

   DECISION   → the section it changes (§3 offer, §4 positioning,
                §6 funnel…) as a one-line note with the date, plus
                a §13 row marked shipped (decisions ship instantly)
   PROCESS    → §13 row (captured) + the proto-SOP in the Brainstorm
                log; graduates to §9 when the SOP Creator runs it
   BUILD      → §13 row (captured / scheduled) + the build brief in
                the Brainstorm log
   EXPERIMENT → §13 row + a candidate metric flagged for §12
   PARKED     → §13 Parked, with reason and revisit date
   OPEN QUESTIONS → §13 "Open questions carried forward"; they are
                the first agenda item of the next brainstorm

The full capture (records, proto-SOPs, briefs) lives in its own
document — the Brainstorm Log — referenced from §13. The file
carries the rows; the log carries the detail.

SECTION 8: THE OPERATING PRINCIPLES CLAUDE WILL APPLY
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

When working through Step 2 prompts, Claude will apply these
principles to every output, automatically:

PRINCIPLE 1: EXTRACT, DON'T INVENT
Every outcome traces to something in the source. Nothing is
recorded that the room didn't say. Gaps become questions.

PRINCIPLE 2: TYPE BEFORE PRIORITY
Every outcome is classified — decision / process / build /
experiment / parked — before anything is ranked.

PRINCIPLE 3: ONE NAME PER OUTCOME
No "we." No "the team." One owner, even if it's always the member.

PRINCIPLE 4: REASONS AND ALTERNATIVES ALWAYS
A decision is recorded with what it was chosen over. If the
alternatives weren't discussed, the record says so.

PRINCIPLE 5: OPEN STAYS OPEN
A question the room didn't answer is recorded as a question.
Capture never closes it.

PRINCIPLE 6: PROCESSES ARE CAPTURED AS PROTO-SOPs
Trigger, owner, steps, definition of done, exceptions — in the
SOP Creator's shape, so nothing is re-derived later.

PRINCIPLE 7: THREE GET A DATE
At most three outcomes get a next step this month. The rest are
scheduled later or parked, explicitly, with a reason.

PRINCIPLE 8: SMALLEST WORTHWHILE VERSION
Every build brief is asked: what is the smallest version still
worth shipping? That version is what gets scheduled.

PRINCIPLE 9: THE FILE CARRIES ROWS, THE LOG CARRIES DETAIL
§13 stays scannable. The full records live in the Brainstorm Log.
