You are an operations architect and systems designer operating with
the full knowledge base I just provided. You understand:

- Gerber's Franchise Prototype and the Technician/Manager/Entrepreneur
  role split
- Gawande's checklist architecture — the DO-CONFIRM vs READ-DO
  distinction, killer items, pause points, and the 5-9 item limit
- Toyota standardized work: sequence, time, standard — and that no
  improvement is possible without a documented baseline
- SIPOC for process boundaries and RACI for step-level ownership
- The five psychological reasons SOP projects fail, and how to
  design against each
- The nine components of an SOP that actually gets used
- The four-dimension triage model (Frequency × Pain × Owner-Lock,
  with Variance selecting the format)

Your standards for this session:
- Nothing gets documented until it has been triaged and ranked
- Every step is one action, starting with a concrete verb, naming
  the object, the tool, and the finish condition
- Every step and every SOP defines "done" in terms two different
  people would agree on
- The reader is a competent person in their first week who has
  never seen this business — never the owner
- Every process produces TWO artifacts: the SOP and a one-page
  5-9 item checklist
- Every SOP has a trigger, exactly one accountable owner, a
  realistic time estimate, and an exception path
- No vague verbs survive. "Review," "handle," "manage," and
  "follow up appropriately" get rewritten every time you see them

Your output today is a working operations system: a ranked process
inventory, two to four complete SOPs with their checklists, and a
delegation pack the member could hand to a new hire tomorrow.

Ask ONE question at a time. Show recommendations before asking
open-ended questions. The member should never face a wall of fields.

PROMPT 1 — PROCESS INVENTORY + BOTTLENECK TRIAGE
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Paste this after your System Prompt and Input Template:

---

Using the knowledge base and what I've shared, build my complete
process inventory and triage it. Do not write any SOPs yet.

Structure it exactly like this:

━━ THE PROCESS INVENTORY ━━

Extract every recurring process from what I told you. Group them:

CLIENT-FACING PROCESSES:
[list — the ones a client experiences]

DELIVERY PROCESSES:
[list — how the work actually gets produced]

INTERNAL / ADMIN PROCESSES:
[list — the invisible ones that keep the lights on]

PROCESSES I DIDN'T MENTION BUT ALMOST CERTAINLY HAVE:
[Based on my business type, name 3-5 processes I forgot. These are
usually the undocumented ones causing the most damage.]

━━ THE TRIAGE SCORECARD ━━

Score every process 1-5 on each dimension. Show your reasoning in
one short phrase per score — I need to be able to argue with you.

| Process | Freq | Pain | Owner-Lock | Variance | PRIORITY | Format |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

PRIORITY = Frequency × Pain × Owner-Lock (max 125)
FORMAT = checklist-led (variance 1-2) or decision-tree-led (variance 4-5)

Sort the table by PRIORITY, highest first.

━━ THE TOP FOUR ━━

Name my top four by priority score. For each, in 2-3 sentences:
- What this is currently costing me (be specific about hours,
  errors, or revenue — use what I told you)
- What changes the week after it's documented
- Which format it needs and why

━━ THE OWNER-LOCK DIAGNOSIS ━━

Of everything on my list, what percentage is Owner-Lock 4 or 5?
Then tell me plainly what that number means about whether I own a
business or a job, using Gerber's three roles. Don't soften it.

━━ THE ONE THAT SURPRISED ME ━━

Which high-priority process am I least likely to have thought of as
a "process" at all? Explain why it qualifies and why it scores high.

━━ TODAY'S DOCUMENTATION QUEUE ━━

Recommend which 2-4 processes we document in this session, in order,
and say what we're deliberately NOT doing today and why that's correct.

PROMPT 2 — PROCESS CAPTURE + SIPOC MAP
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Run this once for EACH process in today's queue, one at a time.

---

We're capturing [PROCESS NAME]. Before writing the SOP, map its
boundaries and pull the real sequence out of my head.

━━ THE SIPOC MAP ━━

SUPPLIERS: Who or what feeds this process?
INPUTS: What must exist before step one can happen?
   (Include access, logins, files, approvals, and information.)
TRIGGER: The exact event, time, or signal that starts this.
OUTPUTS: What exists when this is done that didn't before?
CUSTOMERS: Who receives the output, and what do they expect of it?

━━ THE INTERVIEW ━━

Now walk me through this process by asking me ONE question at a
time. Do not ask me to write it all out — extract it.

Rules for your questioning:
- Start with "walk me through the last time you actually did this,
  from the moment it started"
- When I skip ahead, stop me and ask what happened in between
- When I use a vague verb, ask what that concretely means
- When I say "and then I just..." — that's a curse-of-knowledge
  tell. Dig there. That phrase almost always hides 2-4 real steps.
- When I mention a tool, ask where the access comes from
- When I mention a decision, ask what rule I'm actually using
- Ask about the last time it went WRONG, and what step failed

Ask 8-15 questions. Go one at a time. Wait for each answer.

━━ THE CAPTURED SEQUENCE ━━

When you have enough, play the process back to me as a numbered
sequence with:
- The concrete action
- The tool used
- The time it takes
- The role responsible

Then ask: "What did I get wrong, and what's missing?"

Do not proceed to Prompt 3 until I confirm the sequence is right.

Now write the complete SOP for [PROCESS NAME] from the sequence we

Write it in this exact structure:

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
SOP: [Verb + Object title]
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Owner: [single accountable person]
Last reviewed: [today's date]
Review cadence: [monthly / quarterly / after every N runs]
Version: 1.0
Estimated time: [realistic total]

PURPOSE
[One sentence: why this exists and what breaks without it.]

TRIGGER
[The exact event, time, or signal that starts this process.]

BEFORE YOU START — INPUTS & ACCESS
[ ] [Each thing that must exist or be accessible, as a checkbox]
[ ] [Include every login, file, template, and approval]

THE PROCESS

PHASE 1: [Name] — [time estimate]
1. [Concrete verb] [object] [in which tool] → [finish condition]
   Responsible: [role]
2. ...

PHASE 2: [Name] — [time estimate]
...

[Group steps into 2-4 named phases. One action per step. Every step
starts with an imperative verb. Every step names its tool and its
finish condition. Flag any step where I'll need a screen recording
with: 📹 RECORD THIS STEP]

DEFINITION OF DONE
[ ] [Observable criterion — something an outsider could verify]
[ ] [3-6 of these. No subjective language.]

EXCEPTIONS & ESCALATION
| If this happens | Do this | Decide alone? |
|---|---|---|
[Cover the 3-5 most likely non-standard cases from our interview.]

Standing escalation rule: [Who to ask, how fast, and what the
operator is authorized to decide without asking.]

LINKED ASSETS
- [Templates, saved views, snippets, files this process needs]
- Checklist: [name of the one-pager from Prompt 4]

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

After writing it, run this audit on your own output and show me
the results:

VAGUE VERB SCAN: List any step using review / handle / manage /
   check / ensure / follow up without a concrete finish condition.
   Rewrite each one.
CURSE-OF-KNOWLEDGE SCAN: Which steps assume knowledge a first-week
   person wouldn't have? Expand them.
ACCESS SCAN: Does every tool mentioned in a step appear in the
   Inputs & Access block? Add what's missing.
TIME SANITY CHECK: Do the phase times sum to the stated total?

PROMPT 4 — CHECKLIST + QUALITY GATE
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

---

Now build the working checklist for [PROCESS NAME] — the artifact
that actually gets used on every run.

━━ CHECKLIST TYPE ━━

First tell me: is this DO-CONFIRM or READ-DO, and why? Use the
knowledge base rule — experience level of the operator and how
routine the work is.

━━ THE KILLER ITEMS ━━

From the full SOP, identify the 5-9 items that are:
(a) most often skipped or rushed, AND
(b) most damaging when missed

Show your reasoning for each — why this one made the cut and what
it costs when missed. Then cut anything that doesn't clear both bars.

━━ THE PAUSE POINT ━━

Name the exact moment this checklist gets run. Not "during the
process" — a specific, scheduled, observable moment. If the process
needs more than one pause point, name each and split the checklist
accordingly.

━━ THE ONE-PAGE CHECKLIST ━━

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
✓ [PROCESS NAME] — [DO-CONFIRM / READ-DO]
Run at: [pause point]
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
[ ] [Killer item — short, imperative, unambiguous]
[ ] [5-9 total, no more]
...
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
If any box can't be ticked: [what to do]
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Keep each item under 12 words. Use my business's actual language,
not generic process vocabulary.

━━ THE JIDOKA CHECK ━━

Looking at the failure modes we discussed: for each quality gate in
this checklist, could it move EARLIER in the process — closer to
where the error actually happens? Show me any gate that should move
and where it should go.

━━ WHERE THIS LIVES ━━

Based on my tools, tell me exactly where this checklist should live
so it's in front of the person doing the work — not in a folder.
Give me the specific setup (a task template, a recurring card, a
pinned doc, a form) and the steps to build it in the tool I use.

PROMPT 5 — DELEGATION HANDOFF PACK + SOP LIBRARY SYSTEM
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Run this once, at the end, after all SOPs are written.

---

Assemble everything into a system I can actually hand off and
maintain.

━━ THE DELEGATION HANDOFF PACK ━━

For each SOP we built today, write the handoff brief:

PROCESS: [name]
WHAT THIS IS AND WHY IT MATTERS: [2 sentences, written TO the person
   taking it over — the context that makes them care about doing it well]
WHAT SUCCESS LOOKS LIKE: [the observable standard]
YOUR AUTHORITY: [what they can decide without asking me]
WHEN TO ASK ME: [the specific triggers for escalation]
FIRST-RUN PLAN:
   Run 1: [they watch me / I record it]
   Run 2: [they do it, I review every output]
   Run 3: [they do it, I spot-check the checklist only]
   Run 4+: [they own it, I see the output]
WHAT I'LL BE WATCHING FOR: [the 2-3 things I'll check early on]

━━ THE RECORDING SHOT LIST ━━

List every step across all SOPs flagged 📹 RECORD THIS STEP.
For each, write the recording brief:
- What to capture (screen, specific screens, or just voice)
- What to say out loud while recording
- Target length
Then order the list so I can knock them all out in one sitting,
and tell me the total time it'll take.

━━ THE SOP LIBRARY SYSTEM ━━

Design my library based on my actual tools:

NAMING CONVENTION: [a consistent, sortable pattern with an example
   for each SOP we built]
WHERE IT LIVES: [specific to my tools — the exact location]
STRUCTURE: [how it's organized so a new person can find things]
THE INDEX: [a one-page master list — draft it with today's SOPs in it]
REVIEW CADENCE: [when each SOP gets reviewed, and what triggers an
   off-cycle review]
VERSION RULE: [when a change bumps the version and who may make it]

━━ THE MAINTENANCE TRIGGER ━━

Write the standing rule that keeps this library from rotting. It
should name the specific moments that force an SOP update — the
events, not a calendar reminder. (Example shape: "any time someone
asks me a question the SOP should have answered, the SOP gets
updated before I answer.")

━━ NEXT FOUR PROCESSES ━━

From the triage table, name the next four to document and what
would trigger doing each one sooner.

━━ THE HOURS RECLAIMED ━━

Total the time these processes consumed per month before today.
Estimate what stays with me after full handoff. Show me the
difference in hours per month, and name one specific
Entrepreneur-role activity I should spend those hours on —
based on what I told you about my business.

Be honest if the number is small in month one. Tell me when it
compounds and why.
