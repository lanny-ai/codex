# Q&A as objection handling

Most people who stay to the end and do not buy have a question nobody answered. The Q&A is written before the webinar, from the room's real objections, and every answer ends by pointing to the right door.

## 1. Where the objections come from

In this order: §2 (objections and the avatar's own words), §15 (Sales Conversations: the objections the member hears on calls), the chat and questions from past runs (`webinar/runs/`), and the member's own list. Never invent an objection the member has not heard; ask.

## 2. The bank (`qa-bank.md`, written by `webinar_plan.py render`)

Each entry in `webinar.json` `qa_bank`: `objection` (time, money, confidence, spouse, or another the member names), `question` in the room's words, `answer` in the member's words (three to five sentences), and `door` (which door from `close.md` it points to, or "no door: here's a free next step").

The four the check requires (`qa_bank_covers`), after Michael Stevenson's scripted Q&A:
- **Time.** "I don't have time." Usually a priority question, not a time question. Answer with how long the first step really takes (measured, not guessed) and what the program removes from their week.
- **Money.** "I can't afford it." The payment plan with its total, from `close.md`; the lowest door if there is one; and the honest "if money is genuinely tight, don't. Here's what to do on your own." Never a guilt line.
- **Confidence.** "I'm not sure I can do this" (for this audience often "I'm not technical" or "I'm too old for this"). A classified story of someone like them, or the member's own; what support exists; what happens if they get stuck.
- **The spouse or partner.** "I need to talk to my husband / wife / business partner." Respect it: "Good. Here's what to show them" (the one-pager), and when the decision has to be made by, if there is a real deadline. Never "if you have to ask permission…".

Others worth writing when they come up: "Can I do it myself?" (yes, and here is how; the offer is for people who want it faster or with help), "Will this work for my kind of business?", "What if it doesn't work?" (the guarantee as signed off, word for word), "Is this a subscription?" (the renewal terms, from `close.md`).

## 3. Running it live

- The moderator collects questions in the chat (or the webinar Q&A panel) from the start and brings the ones from the bank forward first.
- Answer generously, then route: "If that's you, the [door] is built for exactly that."
- Take live questions one at a time, reading the question aloud (with the asker's first name only if they are happy with it).
- A question that needs a long answer: "Stay on after and I'll walk you through it", and do.
- On a recorded run, the segment is titled honestly: "The questions people most often ask". Never "live questions".

## 4. The takeaway close and the hard stop

- "This may or may not be for you" [founder's method], with a respectful "this isn't for you if…" list: two or three honest reasons someone should not buy.
- The link one last time, a thank-you, and stop at the time promised. A webinar that runs over loses the people with somewhere to be, and this audience notices.
- The founder's own instruction to himself: go straight to the link; don't talk past the close.
