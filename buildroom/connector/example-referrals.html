Lead Gen Machine: Module 3, the Referral System

Only this module is drafted, because you said you most want clients from referrals from dental CPAs, and your 90-day goal is four new practices. The step is recorded as partial for that reason.

1. Two kinds of referral

  • Partners: dental CPAs. Your preferred source, and already working with two. This is the main part of the draft.
  • Clients: owner-dentists you already serve. Northline works with 14 practices today. Secondary.

2. Partner system: dental CPAs

Ideal partner [draft]: a CPA or small firm with several dental-practice clients, who does the tax work and does not want to do monthly bookkeeping. Whether that describes your two current partners is [needs you].

Why a CPA would refer [draft]: your own line says it. "I don't do taxes. I make sure your tax person never has to guess." You are not after their work, and you make their year-end easier: your program hands their client's CPA a year-end package.

Where to find more [draft, no list built]:

  1. Ask each of your two current CPA partners who else they know that serves dentists.
  2. Ask your current clients who their CPA is. Each one whose CPA is not already a partner is a warm introduction.
  3. LinkedIn, where you already post. No search was run.

First message to a CPA you do not know, via a client you share [draft]:

"Hi [name], I keep the books for [client], whose taxes you do. I wanted to introduce myself, since you will be getting the year-end package from me. I only work with dental practices and I do not do taxes. If it would help, I can send you a sample of the package so you know what to expect."

First message to a CPA with no shared client [draft]:

"Hi [name], I run Northline Bookkeeping. I do monthly bookkeeping only for independent dental practices, and I do not do taxes. I work beside the practice's CPA and hand over a year-end package. If any of your dental clients turn up in spring with books you have to rebuild, I would be glad to show you what I send. No ask beyond that."

Partner conversation, three questions [draft]:

  • "What do your dental clients' books look like when they reach you?"
  • "What would you need from a bookkeeper to be comfortable sending someone?"
  • "How would you want to hear back about a client you sent?"

Keeping in touch [draft]: after each referred practice's first close, a short note to the CPA saying it is done. A check-in each quarter. No commission is proposed; whether you ever pay one is yours to decide.

3. Referral kit: what a CPA needs in order to send someone

  • Who to look for: an owner-dentist with one or two locations, usually 5 to 15 staff [yours], whose books are done by a spouse or the office manager [your intake notes], or who has had a tax surprise or is applying for a practice loan [your intake notes].
  • Not a fit: DSOs, groups over three locations, anyone who wants the cheapest option. [your intake notes]
  • Two sentences they can forward [draft]: "Priya Raman at Northline does bookkeeping only for dental practices. Her clients' books are closed by the 10th every month and she hands me a clean year-end package."
  • What happens next: a 20-minute fit call. Needs you: A booking link
  • What you will do: tell the CPA the outcome either way.

5. When a referred lead arrives [draft]

  • Reply the same day, copy the person who referred them, and offer the 20-minute fit call.
  • On the call, skip the long opening. Use the Sales Conversations draft from the Situation stage on.
  • If it is not a fit, say so to both people.
