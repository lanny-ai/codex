Sales script: the 20-minute fit call

Scenario [draft]: a warm referral. An owner-dentist sent by a dental CPA, which is where you said you most want clients from. If most of your calls come another way, the opening changes and little else does.

Before the call: read anything the CPA told you. Have a blank Practice Scorecard in front of you. The aim of the call is a decision about fit, not a pitch.

Connection

"Thanks for making the time. [CPA's name] said you two had been talking about the books. Before I tell you anything about how I work, can I ask a few questions about how it runs today? If it turns out I am not the right fit, I will say so."

Tone: unhurried. You are deciding too.

Situation

  • "Who does the books right now, and when do they get done?"
  • "When do you usually see numbers for a month?"
  • "What does your CPA get from you at year end, and how does that go?"
  • "One location or two? How many on the team?"

Problem Awareness

  • "If I asked you your overhead percentage today, what would you say? How sure are you?"
  • "How do you decide whether it was a good month?"
  • "What happened the last time a number surprised you?"
  • "Have you worked with a bookkeeper before? What was that like?"

Let them talk. Write down their exact words; they belong in your language bank afterwards.

Consequence

  • "What decisions are you putting off because you do not trust the numbers?"
  • "If nothing changes for another year, who keeps doing the books, and what does that cost them?"

Do not put a dollar figure on it for them. You do not give tax advice and you do not estimate savings.

Solution Vision

"Here is what it looks like with me. I take the monthly bookkeeping off the practice completely. The first three weeks are cleanup: we fix the last 12 months and connect your practice software. From month two your books are closed by the 10th, you get a one-page Practice Scorecard, which is collections, overhead percentage, hygiene production and owner pay, and we spend 30 minutes on the phone going through it. Once a quarter we review overhead against a target. At year end I hand your CPA a package. I do not do taxes; I work beside your CPA."

Then: "What part of that would matter most to you?"

Qualifying & Transition

  • "I work with owner-dentists with one or two locations. Is that you for the next couple of years, or are you growing past that?"
  • "I am not the cheapest option, and I do not try to be. Is that all right to hear before we talk about price?"
  • "Who else needs to be part of this decision?"

If they are a DSO, a group over three locations, or shopping on price alone: say it is not a fit, and stop.

Commitment

"The program is $1,450 per month, with a one-time setup and cleanup fee of $2,000. It is month to month after the first 3 months. And there is one guarantee, which I will read to you exactly: 'If your books are not closed by the 10th, that month is free.'"

Then stop talking.

If yes: "Good. The next step is Needs you: your onboarding first step. I will send it today."

If not yet: "What would you need to see or know to decide?" Agree a date to speak again. Do not chase.

Objection set

Only the first is on record. The others are common-sense drafts for you to replace with what you really hear.

"I've tried a bookkeeper before. She didn't understand dental." [one prospect, private call]

Label: "It sounds like the last one treated your practice like any other small business."

Question: "What went into the wrong place?" Then: "Lab fees and payroll coding are the first two things I clean up, and the scorecard reports hygiene production separately. I managed a three-chair practice for nine years before I started Northline."

"That is more than I pay now." [draft]

Label: "It sounds like the number is higher than you expected."

Question: "What are you comparing it with?" Do not discount. Restate what is included and the guarantee, word for word. If price is the only test, they are not a fit; you have said you do not serve people who want the cheapest option.

"My spouse or office manager already does it." [draft, from your intake notes]

Label: "It sounds like it is getting done, and you do not want to take it away from someone."

Question: "How do they feel about doing it?"

"Will this lower my tax bill?" [draft]

"I do not give tax advice and I will not put a figure on that. What I do is make sure your CPA has clean numbers on time."

"I need to think about it." [draft]

"Of course. What is the part you are weighing?"

Accusation audit, to open the Commitment stage if the call has been wary [draft]: "You may be thinking this is one more bookkeeper who will not understand a dental practice, and that you will be explaining lab fees to me in three months."

Pre-call routine [draft]

Two minutes. Read the referral note. Write down the one thing you want to learn. Put the guarantee wording where you can see it so you say it exactly.
