# Voice: keeping it the author's

## Capture it
- The intake's `voice` answer, and §17 of the Business File when it exists.
- **A sample of the author's real words** is worth more than their description of them: a transcript of them teaching, a talk, emails they wrote themselves. Add it to the source packet and read it before drafting.
- The outline's VOICE line records pace, humour, favourite words, and what they refuse to say.
- The style guide (from the memo step) lists the voice markers to preserve, recurring phrases to keep, tics to trim, and what must stay consistent.

## Hold it
- Use the author's exact framework names and phrases, as they gave them.
- Write the way they talk when teaching. If they're blunt, be blunt. If they tell stories, open with one.
- The member reads the sample chapter edit and says whether it sounds like them. That decision governs every later edit.

## Hard rules
- **Never an em dash or an en dash.** Use a period, a comma or a colon. `book_check.py` blocks them.
- Plain prose. No markdown, no bullet lists in the manuscript. A subhead is a line starting `@@ `.
- Nothing invented (`honesty-rules.md`).

## AI-writing tells
These are measured as a **density per chapter**, never as a verdict on one sentence. A word on this list is fine when the author would say it. `book_check.py` reports a chapter when its density is high; the fix is to rewrite the passage in the author's own words.

**Vocabulary** (flagged at two or more per 1,000 words): delve, tapestry, intricate, pivotal, underscore, landscape and journey (figurative), foster, testament, crucial, showcase, leverage (as a verb), unlock, revolutionize, game-changer, robust, seamless, holistic, realm, beacon, transformative, empower, elevate, harness, myriad, plethora, multifaceted.

**Stock phrases** (any): "In today's fast-paced world" · "In an era of" · "In a world where" · "stands as a testament" · "plays a crucial role" · "underscores the importance" · "It's worth noting" · "Let's dive in" · "navigate the complexities" · "When it comes to" · "At its core" · "At the end of the day" · "Here's the thing" · "The truth is" · "In conclusion" · "Whether you're a … or a …" · "Imagine a world where" · "Picture this" · "more than just a" · "As we'll see" · "As mentioned earlier" · "take it to the next level".

**Sentence patterns:**
- "It's not X, it's Y" and "This isn't about X. It's about Y."
- "not just X but Y"
- three-item lists, when more often than about one every 300 words
- a trailing "-ing" clause: ", highlighting…", ", ensuring…"
- the colon reveal: "The result? …"
- a question the writer answers at once
- "Studies show" or "Experts agree" with no source (this one is a FIX, not a tell)
- sincerity markers: truly, genuinely, honestly, incredibly

**Paragraph and chapter patterns:**
- most paragraphs ending on a short punchy line
- every paragraph about the same length
- three or more chapters opening or closing with the same words
- most chapters opening with a question, or with "Imagine"

## What actually fixes it
1. More of the author's own material: a real story with a real detail beats any rewrite.
2. Read the passage aloud in the author's voice. Cut what they wouldn't say.
3. Replace an abstract claim with what happened.
4. Vary how chapters open: a person, a place, a mistake, a number the author gave.
