# The proof playbook

The founder's rule, from his own case-study loop: **a win isn't an asset until it's in the proof bank with the client's permission status recorded.** Everything here serves that rule. [founder's method]

## 1. The loop

1. **Trigger.** Any win signal: a first win in the first two weeks, a testimonial-shaped email, a result mentioned on a call, a number the client reports, a live comment at an event, a dashboard "wins to harvest" line.
2. **Capture.** Send the 3-question interview. Ten minutes of the client's time, at most.
3. **File.** One entry per story, with before, result, verbatim words, class and permission.
4. **Deploy.** Replace one composite or generic proof block somewhere with the named result. One story, one placement, immediately.

Run it weekly for ten minutes; the stories compound.

## 2. The interview and the permission ask

Three questions, in the member's voice, for the member to send by hand [founder's own, paste-ready]:

> 1. What was a normal Tuesday like before, honestly?
> 2. What's the one number or thing that changed, and by how much?
> 3. What did you do with the first afternoon you didn't have to be at the desk?

Adapt question 3 to the member's offer (the first afternoon off, the first new client, the first month the numbers worked). Then:

- **The permission ask:** "Comfortable with your name and words on our site? Anonymous works too."
- **The honesty valve:** "If something's NOT working, tell me that instead." A client who says so is worth more than a quote; the member fixes it first.

Ask within a week of the win, while it is fresh. If the client already wrote it (an email, a message, a review), quote it verbatim and still ask permission before it goes anywhere public.

## 3. Permission states

| State | Meaning | May be used |
|---|---|---|
| **named** | The client agreed to their name (and company, if they said so) and their words | Anywhere, as written |
| **anonymous** | The client agreed to their words and result without their name | As "a [client type]" with no identifying detail |
| **pending** | Asked, not yet answered | Nowhere public; the member may read it on a call as "one client told me…" only if the words are their own |
| **declined** | The client said no | Never, in any form, forever; the entry stays so nobody asks again |

Written permission (an email or message reply) is kept with the entry, with its date. Chat comments and live words at an event need the same ask before reuse. [FTC Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule, in force since October 2024; the Endorsement Guides]

## 4. Proof classes

Every entry carries one class, so each tool knows where it may be used [founder's classification, extended]:

- **A** a free-event or free-tool result, no money: a transformation, a confidence shift, a thing built in the room.
- **B** a free-event or free-tool result with money: revenue booked or earned by acting on the free material.
- **C** a freedom or impact story: time back, a life change, in the person's words.
- **D** a paid-program result: a client of the member's paid offer. The strongest proof for the offer, and **never presented as what a free event or free tool produces**.
- **E** an outside example: someone not the member's client, cited publicly. "What is possible in the world", never "what my clients get".
- **H** the member's own counts and results ("308 people finished the tool"; the member's own business numbers), with the typical result beside them.
- **X** a composite or illustration: labelled `[composite result based on typical client outcomes]` wherever it appears; never presented as a single real person.
- **R** retired: on §1's retired-lines list, or a paraphrase of one. Never used.
- **U** uncertain: the facts are not confirmed (who, what, when, the number). Not used until confirmed.

## 5. Honesty rules (non-negotiable)

- **Verbatim means verbatim.** Spelling may be tidied; words, order and emphasis may not.
- **Quotes are attributed to the thing they were about.** A quote about a training is not a quote about the paid program. The founder corrected his own offer page for exactly this.
- **Results claims need the client's numbers, not the member's estimates**, before they go public. Income claims carry the typical result beside them, or stay out. [FTC: "results not typical" does not cure a misleading claim]
- **A declined permission is recorded and respected forever.**
- **Composites are always labelled.**
- **No rewards for positive reviews**; thanking a client after the fact is fine, conditioning anything on a good word is not.
- **Nothing on §18's never-say list**, and no retired line or paraphrase of one.

## 6. Where each class may go

| Asset (owning tool) | Classes allowed | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Offer page proof section (Offer Page Copy) | D named or anonymous; C; H with typical result | The strongest D story leads |
| Follow-up emails (Decision Machine, Copy Engine) | D, C, B; one story per email | Email 3 is the usual proof slot |
| Ads (Ad Studio) | D or B named only; H with typical result | Platforms and the FTC watch ads hardest; nothing anonymous that implies a real person |
| Video testimonials (Video Director) | D or B named, consent form on file | The director records; this skill decides who to ask |
| LinkedIn posts (LinkedIn Engine) | C, D, B with consent; H | The post gate checks the class and permission |
| Launch Day 2 proof and invitation emails (Event Launcher) | A and B for a free event; D only labelled as a paid-program result; E as "what's possible" | The launch gate re-checks before the cart emails |
| Sales calls (Sales Conversations) | Any consented story, told in the member's words | Pending stories as "one client told me…" only |

When the right class is missing, say so plainly and route to the honest alternative: the member's own story with a damaging admission, the method shown live, or the H numbers with their typical result. Never stretch a story to fill the gap.

## 7. Evidence

The loop, interview, permission states and honesty rules are the founder's own (his case-study library and his July 2026 proof bank). The legal lines rest on the FTC's Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule (16 CFR 465) and Endorsement Guides, summarised, not legal advice. Members in regulated sectors follow their regulator's rules on testimonials as well.
