# The proof playbook

The founder's rule, from his own case-study loop: **a win isn't an asset until it's in the proof bank with the client's permission recorded, including where it may be used.** Everything here serves that rule. [founder's method]

## 1. The loop

1. **Trigger.** Any win signal: a first win in the first two weeks, a testimonial-shaped email, a result mentioned on a call, a number the client reports, a live comment at an event, a public review.
2. **Capture.** Send the interview and the scoped permission ask. Ten minutes of the client's time, at most.
3. **File.** One entry per story with its ID, origin, kind, the client's words, and permission with its scope.
4. **Deploy.** Only once permission is `named` or `anonymous`: replace one composite or generic proof block with the real result. One story, one placement. While permission is pending, the story waits (a §13 row), and the weekly check deploys it when the answer comes.

Run it weekly for ten minutes; the stories compound.

## 2. The interview and the permission ask

Five short questions, in the member's voice, for the member to send by hand (email, text or a two-minute call). The founder's three are the spine; two are added so the result can be used honestly. [founder's own, extended]

> 1. What was a normal [working day] like before, honestly? (Adapt: a Tuesday at the desk, a day on site, a month-end.)
> 2. What's the one number or thing that changed, by how much, and over what period? How do you measure that?
> 3. What did you do with [the first afternoon / the first month / the first client] that this gave you?
> 4. What almost stopped you from starting, and what changed your mind?
> 5. Anything that's NOT working, or that you'd warn someone about? (The honesty valve. A client who answers this is worth more than a quote; the member fixes it first.)

Then **the scoped permission ask**, which names the places so the answer can be recorded as scope:

> "Can I use your words and result? Say yes or no to each: on my website · in my emails · on LinkedIn or social posts · in paid ads · on video or audio. With your name and company, or anonymous? May I use your photo or logo? You'll see the exact wording before it goes anywhere, and you can withdraw any time."

The right of review is a promise, kept: the client sees the final wording in place before it is published. Ask within a week of the win. If the client already wrote it (an email, a message, a review), quote it verbatim and still send the scoped ask before it goes anywhere.

**Material connections.** If the client got the service free or discounted, is a beta client, a partner, a relative, an affiliate, or someone the member pays or trades with, the entry records it and any public use discloses it ("a beta client", "a partner's client, used with both their permission"). [FTC Endorsement Guides, 16 CFR 255.5; Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule, 16 CFR 465]

## 3. Permission: state and scope

**State:**

| State | Meaning | May be used |
|---|---|---|
| **named** | The client agreed to their name (and company, if they said so) and their words, for the channels in scope | In the channels in scope, as written |
| **anonymous** | The client agreed to their words and result without their name, for the channels in scope | As "a [client type]" with no detail that would identify them (in a small niche, loosen the role and place too) |
| **pending** | Asked, not answered | Nowhere: not on a page, not in an email, not on a call. An unconfirmed endorsement is still an endorsement |
| **declined** | The client said no | Never, in any form, forever; the entry stays so nobody asks again |
| **withdrawn** | Permission given, then taken back | Removed from every placement within 10 business days; the client is told when it is done; print or video that cannot be recalled is noted and not reprinted or re-published |

**Scope,** recorded per entry: `site`, `emails`, `social` (LinkedIn and other posts), `ads`, `video` (and audio), `events` (read aloud or shown live); plus `name: yes|no`, `company: yes|no`, `photo_or_logo: yes|no`. A story may be used only in the channels its scope allows. Video and audio need the Video Director's consent form as well; a yes to "video" here is the invitation to send it.

Written permission (the client's reply) is kept in `proof/consents/` with its date. A spoken yes on a call is recorded as a dated note and confirmed by email the same day. Chat comments and live words at an event need the same ask before reuse. Non-US clients may ask for their data to be erased; treat that as `withdrawn` for all channels.

## 4. Origin and kind

Every entry carries an **origin** (the class letter other Build Room tools read) and a **kind**.

**Origin** [founder's classification, extended]:
- **A** a free-event or free-tool result, without money.
- **B** a free-event or free-tool result with money: revenue booked or earned by acting on the free material.
- **D** a paid-program result: a client of the member's paid offer. The strongest proof for the offer, and **never presented as what a free event or free tool produces**. A first win in week two of a paid program is still D.
- **E** an outside example: someone not the member's client, cited publicly. "What is possible in the world", never "what my clients get".
- **H** the member's own counts and results, with the typical result beside them.
- **X** a composite or illustration, labelled `[composite result based on typical client outcomes]` wherever it appears.
- **R** retired: on §1's retired-lines list, or a paraphrase of one. Never used.
- **U** uncertain: who, what, when or the number is not confirmed, or the words have not been matched to their source. Not used until confirmed.

**Kind:** `money` (a revenue, cost or profit figure), `result` (a non-money number: hours, clients, calls, days), `transformation` (a change the client describes without a number), `freedom` (time back, a life change). The Event Launcher's class "C" is kind `freedom` on any origin.

**Legacy material.** Quotes collected before the proof bank existed (a testimonial package, a page already live, a slide) start as **U**: the words are matched to their source (a recording, an email, a chat log) and the speaker confirmed before the origin is set. A headline written *for* a client is not their quote. A quote already live without a recorded consent gets the scoped ask now, with a date by which it comes down if no answer arrives.

## 5. Honesty rules (non-negotiable)

- **Verbatim means verbatim.** Spelling may be tidied; cuts are shown with `[…]`; words, order and emphasis may not change.
- **Quotes are attributed to the thing they were about.** A quote about a training is not a quote about the paid program. The founder corrected his own offer page for exactly this.
- **Every results claim carries what a typical client gets, or stays out.** Money, hours, clients, calls: all of them. The client's own number, never the member's estimate. [FTC: "results not typical" does not cure a misleading claim]
- **A declined or withdrawn permission is respected forever.**
- **Composites are always labelled.**
- **Material connections are disclosed.**
- **No rewards for positive reviews**; a thank-you after the fact is fine, conditioning anything on a good word is not.
- **Nothing on §18's never-say list**, and no retired line or paraphrase of one.
- **Re-confirm yearly.** A story older than a year is checked with the client ("still true? still happy for us to use it?") before it is used in a new place.

## 6. Where each origin may go

Scope decides the channel; origin decides the honesty of the claim. A story goes into an asset only when **both** allow it.

| Asset (owning tool) | Origins allowed | Scope needed | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Offer page proof section (Offer Page Copy) | D named or anonymous; H with typical result | `site` | The strongest D story leads |
| Follow-up emails (Decision Machine, Copy Engine) | D, B; one story per email | `emails` | Email 3 is the usual proof slot |
| Ads (Ad Studio) | D or B **named** only; H with typical result | `ads`, plus `photo_or_logo` for a photo | Cited by ID in the Ad Studio's copy manifest; nothing anonymous that implies a real person |
| Video testimonials (Video Director) | D or B named | `video` plus the Video Director's consent form | The director records; this skill decides who to ask |
| LinkedIn posts (LinkedIn Engine) | D, B, A with consent; H | `social` | The post gate checks origin and permission |
| Launch Day 2 proof and invitation emails (Event Launcher) | A and B for a free event; D only labelled as a paid-program result; E as "what's possible" | `events` (and `emails` for the invitations) | The launch gate re-checks before the cart emails |
| Sales calls (Sales Conversations) | Any `named` or `anonymous` story, in the member's words | `events` or `emails` (either shows the client expects it to be told) | Pending stories are not told |
| Composites (X) | Anywhere a labelled illustration helps | none (no real person) | The label travels with the words |

When the right origin or scope is missing, say so plainly and route to the honest alternative: the member's own story with a damaging admission, the method shown live, or H numbers with their typical result. Never stretch a story or a scope to fill the gap.

## 7. Public reviews

Google, Trustpilot and platform reviews are proof too. Record the count and rating in §18, quote a public review only with the reviewer's permission for anything beyond a link to it, never edit one, and never gate who is asked to review by how happy they are. [FTC Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule]
