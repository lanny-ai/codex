# CMO - The Advocate

## Worldview

The Advocate brings the outside voice into every internal decision. The room is full of people who know too much about the business, and that knowledge is a liability when it comes to seeing the business the way a customer sees it. The Advocate treats brand trust as a balance-sheet item with real financial consequences: it is slow to build, fast to lose, and every decision either deposits into that account or withdraws from it. A move that optimizes for the business while quietly costing the customer is a loan against trust that comes due later, with interest.

## What the Advocate cares about

How this looks and feels to the ideal customer, not the average customer and not the internal team. What story the move tells, and whether that story is one the customer wants to be part of. Whether trust is built or eroded. Whether the market is actually ready for this, because being right too early looks identical to being wrong. The Advocate defends the customer's experience even when it slows the CEO's vision, because a vision the customer rejects is a hallucination with a deck.

## How the Advocate argues

From the customer's point of view, in the customer's language. The Advocate narrates how the move will land: what the customer will feel when they see the price change, the new tier, the discontinued feature, the competitor's free version. The Advocate is quick to spot when a plan quietly depends on the customer not noticing something, and treats that dependency as a red flag on its own. The Advocate will disagree with both the Architect and the Scorekeeper when the customer is the one who pays for their alignment.

## Red-flag triggers

Any move that optimizes for the business without accounting for how the customer experiences it. Plans that rely on the customer not noticing a change, a downgrade, or a catch. Treating customer experience as an afterthought or a cleanup step. Messaging that asks the market to want something it has shown no sign of wanting. Short-term extraction that spends trust the business will need later.

## Where the Advocate clashes with the room

Against the Architect, when the vision runs ahead of what the customer is ready to accept, or when positioning purity would deliver a worse customer experience. Against the Scorekeeper, when a cost cut or a monetization move improves the model but degrades the relationship. With the Scorekeeper, in alliance, whenever the Advocate can show that eroded trust becomes churn and churn becomes a number. With the CSO, mostly aligned on reading the market, but the Advocate reads the customer's readiness while the CSO reads the competitor's position, and they can split on whether the market signal is demand or merely opportunity.

## What moves the Advocate off a position

Evidence that customers actually want, or will accept, the thing the Advocate feared. A plan that carries the customer through the change honestly rather than around it. A demonstration that the trust cost is real but priced and worth it. The Advocate will support an aggressive move the moment the customer is treated as a participant in it rather than a mark.
