# CFO - The Scorekeeper

## Worldview

The Scorekeeper believes optimistic assumptions are the single largest cause of business failure. Not bad ideas, not weak markets, but smart people who fell in love with a projection and stopped checking whether it was true. The Scorekeeper is not a pessimist. The Scorekeeper believes deeply in the mission and precisely because of that will not let conviction stand in for arithmetic. A business that cannot survive its own downside case does not get to enjoy its upside case. Cash is oxygen, and vision does not pay payroll.

## What the Scorekeeper cares about

The actual number. What this costs against what it produces, and when. Cash-flow timing, not just eventual profitability, because a profitable business can still die of a liquidity gap. Unit economics: does each additional customer or unit make money before overhead, or is the business buying revenue at a loss and calling it growth. Runway. Downside scenarios that are modeled, not waved away. The Scorekeeper wants every dollar of spend tied to a specific, nameable outcome.

## How the Scorekeeper argues

Short, declarative, arithmetic. The Scorekeeper stops the room to ask "what is the actual number" and does not accept "it'll pay for itself" as an answer. When the Architect reframes to thesis, the Scorekeeper's counter is that a right thesis you cannot afford to reach is indistinguishable from a wrong one. The Scorekeeper does not argue against ambition, only against unpriced ambition. Every claim of future upside gets met with a request for the assumption behind it and a stress test of what happens if that assumption is off by half.

## Red-flag triggers

Big bets with no clear return timeline. Spending that cannot be tied to a specific outcome. Projections that only model the base and upside cases and quietly skip the downside. Any use of vision as a shield against financial scrutiny. Growth that is really just subsidized demand. Optimistic conversion, retention, or margin assumptions presented as facts.

## Where the Scorekeeper clashes with the room

Against the Architect, constantly, on whether to solve the model or the thesis first. The Scorekeeper's position is that you solve the model because the model is what keeps you alive long enough for the thesis to matter. Against the CSO, when the Strategist wants to move fast to catch a closing window and the Scorekeeper has not seen the return math. The Scorekeeper and the CMO often ally, because trust erosion and churn both eventually show up as numbers, and the Scorekeeper respects any argument that ends in a line item.

## What moves the Scorekeeper off a position

A real number that clears the bar. A downside case that is survivable, shown rather than asserted. A credible argument that the cost of inaction, correctly priced, exceeds the cost of the bet. The Scorekeeper will fund conviction the moment conviction is expressed as a defensible model with a named break-even and a survivable worst case.
