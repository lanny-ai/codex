PROMPT 1 — DUNFORD'S 5-COMPONENT POSITIONING BUILD
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

---

Work through April Dunford's 5-component positioning framework in sequence.
Don't skip or reorder. Each component builds on the last.

COMPONENT 1 — COMPETITIVE ALTERNATIVES:
Based on what I've shared, what would my best-fit client realistically do
if I didn't exist? List the 2-3 most honest alternatives.
For each: what does the client get from that alternative that they like?
What frustrates them about it? Why do they ultimately look for something different?

COMPONENT 2 — DIFFERENTIATED ATTRIBUTES:
What do I have that is genuinely absent from each of those alternatives?
Be specific. Verify each attribute: is this truly different from what
alternatives offer, or does it just FEEL different from the inside?
Flag any that are table stakes (expected, not differentiating).
Keep only what is real and verifiable.

COMPONENT 3 — DIFFERENTIATED VALUE:
For each real differentiator: "So what? What does this actually mean for the client?"
Map each attribute to a specific, tangible value created for a specific type of buyer.
Format: Attribute → Value it creates → For a client who cares about ___

COMPONENT 4 — BEST-FIT CUSTOMERS:
Based on the differentiated value above — who cares MOST about this?
What are their specific characteristics?
Who benefits most from what I specifically do differently?
Who would find my differentiation irrelevant or unimportant?

COMPONENT 5 — MARKET CATEGORY ANALYSIS:
What market category am I currently positioned in?
What assumptions does that category create in the buyer's mind?
Are those assumptions working for or against me?

Analyze 3 alternative categories I could position in:
For each: what competitive set does it create? What assumptions does it trigger?
Does it make my value MORE obvious or less obvious?

RECOMMENDATION: Which category makes my differentiated value most obvious
to my best-fit customer? Why?

PROMPT 2 — DIFFERENTIATION EXCAVATION
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

---

Now excavate my real differentiation — the kind competitors can't easily claim or copy.

ROUND 1 — THE GENERIC TRAP:
List 5 things I probably say (or currently say) that every competitor
in my space also says. Be direct. These need to be replaced.

ROUND 2 — THE FOUR SOURCES OF REAL DIFFERENTIATION:
Evaluate me against all four sources from the knowledge base:

SOURCE 1 — METHODOLOGY DIFFERENTIATOR:
Do I have a specific approach or process that produces different results?
If so: what is the mechanism? Can I explain WHY it works differently?
If not: what would need to be true to develop this?

SOURCE 2 — PROOF DIFFERENTIATOR:
Do I have a specific track record in a specific niche or for a specific outcome?
If so: how do I articulate it? (Numbers, client types, specific results)
If not: what's the closest thing I have and how do I build toward this?

SOURCE 3 — CONSTRAINT DIFFERENTIATOR:
Could I deliberately limit who I work with to signal premium and create scarcity?
What would that look like in practice?
How would it change the perception of the service?

SOURCE 4 — VILLAIN DIFFERENTIATOR:
What is fundamentally wrong with how the market currently serves my ICA?
Write my "villain statement":
"Most [category] providers do [X]. We believe that's wrong because [Y]. So we do [Z] instead."

THE UNFAIR ADVANTAGE:
What do I specifically have that someone starting today couldn't replicate for 3+ years?
How do I turn this into positioning language?

THE CATEGORY OF ONE:
Using everything above — write one sentence that describes a position
ONLY I can honestly claim. Specific. Verifiable. Ownable.

---

Write the complete positioning statement system:

THE INTERNAL POSITIONING STATEMENT (Dunford format):
"For [best-fit customers], who [specific situation],
[my business] is the [market category] that [primary differentiation],
because [reason to believe / mechanism]."

Write 3 versions using different differentiators.
Apply the "could a competitor use this?" test to each.
Identify which one passes the test AND is most compelling.

THE POSITIONING LADDER:
Rank the following positioning angles from strongest to weakest for MY specific situation,
with a brief explanation:
- Who it's for (niche specificity)
- Outcome delivered
- Method/process
- Speed/efficiency
- Credentials/track record
- Personality/relationship style
- The villain/philosophy

Your recommended position: the single strongest position I should own.
State it clearly. Give your reasoning. Commit to it.

THE POSITIONING STRESS TEST:
Apply these three tests to your recommended position:
Test 1 — Competitor test: Could any competitor honestly claim this?
Test 2 — Clarity test: Could my ICA understand this in 5 seconds?
Test 3 — Conviction test: Am I willing to say explicitly who this is NOT for?

If any test fails: rewrite until all three pass.

THE VALUE PROPOSITION (5 versions using the 5-layer architecture):
For each version, check all 5 layers: clarity, relevance, differentiation, credibility, resonance.
- Version 1: Outcome-forward
- Version 2: Problem-escape forward
- Version 3: Differentiation-forward ("the only X that Y")
- Version 4: Proof-forward (credibility anchor)
- Version 5: Identity-forward (who they become)

After the 5 versions:
Apply the competitor test to each.
Identify the strongest. Explain why it wins.

THE WEBSITE HERO SECTION (3 lines):
Line 1 — Headline (6-10 words): The bold, specific claim

Line 3 — CTA (4-6 words): What they should do next

The one I'd use if I had to pick right now

PART A — ANTI-AUDIENCE (the conversion multiplier):
Profile the client I should NOT be working with:
Give them a name. Describe their situation. Explain specifically
why they're a bad fit — for them AND for me.

THE COMPLETE QUALIFICATION SYSTEM:
"This is NOT for you if..." (5 specific statements — honest, not harsh)
"This IS for you if..." (5 specific statements — mirror of the above)
"Signs we're a perfect fit:" (3 specific situations that signal ideal match)
"Signs we're probably not right for each other:" (3 specific situations)

PRE-QUALIFICATION SCRIPT:
2 sentences I say at the START of a sales call to qualify leads before
investing time with a bad fit. Professional, direct, respectful.

THE CONFIDENCE PARAGRAPH:
3-4 sentences explaining (to reinforce my own conviction) why being
specific about who I'm NOT for is a revenue-positive decision, not
a limiting one. This is for me to believe it before I say it publicly.

PART B — THE MESSAGING BIBLE (complete assembly):
Compile everything from this session into a single, structured document:

1. Internal Positioning Statement (final chosen version)
2. Market Category (chosen + why)
3. Core Differentiators (final 2-3, specific and verified)
4. Best-Fit Customer Profile (1 paragraph)
5. Value Proposition (final chosen version)
6. Website Hero Section (3 lines)
7. Tagline (final chosen)
8. Villain Statement
9. "For You If / Not For You If" lists
10. Pre-Qualification Script

This Messaging Bible is the reference document for every piece of
content, every sales conversation, and every marketing decision going forward.
