# Market sophistication and the belief to move

Two short reads that sit beside the positioning. They decide what the first line of any page, email or ad should claim. Both come from Eugene Schwartz's *Breakthrough Advertising*; the wording and the method here are Build Room OS's own.

They are judgements made from evidence the member has. They are not measurements, and a session cannot verify them. Record the evidence beside each one, and say so when there is none.

## 1. Market sophistication: how many promises like this has the buyer already heard?

| Stage | What the buyer has seen | What the lead line should do |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Nothing like it. The promise is new to them | Say the promise plainly |
| 2 | A few offers making the same promise | Make the promise bigger or more specific, with proof |
| 3 | Many. The promise alone no longer lands | Lead with **how** it works: the mechanism, the method |
| 4 | Many mechanisms too | Sharpen the mechanism: faster, simpler, for exactly this person |
| 5 | They have stopped believing any of it | Lead with the buyer and their situation, not the promise. Identify, then earn the claim |

### How to place the member's market

Ask, one at a time, and use only what the member can point to:

1. "When a best-fit client finds you, what have they usually already tried?" (§2 switching forces often holds this.)
2. "What do the three alternatives they consider say on the first line of their page?" Read the pages with the member if they are to hand. Never describe a competitor's page from memory.
3. "When you say your main promise out loud, do people lean in or roll their eyes?"
4. "Do they ask *what* you do, *how* it works, or *why they should believe you*?" What is being asked is the stage: what (1 to 2), how (3 to 4), why believe (5).

Record: `stage N · evidence: what they have tried, what the alternatives' first lines say, the question buyers ask`.

If the evidence is thin, say so, record the best reading as provisional, and note what would settle it: five sales calls listened to for the first question asked.

### Mistakes to avoid

- **Calling every market stage 5.** It feels sophisticated. Most local and niche service markets sit at 2 or 3.
- **Judging by the member's own fatigue.** The member has read every competitor. The buyer has not.
- **Using the stage to justify a bigger claim.** A stage 2 lead is more specific, not more inflated. Every claim still has to be true and provable from §3 and §18.

## 2. The belief to move

§2 records where most buyers start (unaware, problem-aware, solution-aware, product-aware, most aware). The useful next question is what one thing they must come to believe to take the next step.

| They start | They must come to believe |
|---|---|
| Unaware | That the situation they are in is a problem, and it has a name |
| Problem-aware | That it can be solved, and not by trying harder at what they already do |
| Solution-aware | That this kind of solution fits them better than the others they have looked at |
| Product-aware | That it will work for someone like them, and that the risk is covered |
| Most aware | That now is the time, and what to do next |

Write it as one sentence in the buyer's words, drawn from §2 sourced language where there is any: "I'm not bad at marketing; I've been using a method built for a different kind of business."

Record: `the sentence · from: §2 awareness stage`.

## How the other tools use these

- **Copy Engine:** the stage picks what the headline leads with; the belief is the job of the first third of the piece.
- **Ad Studio:** the awareness stage picks the column of the concept grid; the sophistication stage picks whether the hook is the promise, the mechanism or the person.
- **Offer Page Copy, Lead Capture, Webinar Builder:** the belief to move is what the opening has to establish before the offer is named.

None of these tools changes a claim to fit a stage. The stage changes which true thing is said first.
