SECTION 1: THE CORE TRUTH ABOUT BRAINSTORMING
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Alex Osborn invented the word in 1953 and gave it four rules: defer
judgment, go for quantity, welcome wild ideas, build on others'.
Sixty years of research since then has found that the rules are
right and the way most rooms run them is wrong.

The truth:

THE ROOM IS NOT THE PROBLEM. THE MISSING QUESTION IS.

A brainstorm that starts with "let's think about marketing" ends
with a list. One that starts with "what do we give away on Tuesday
that a chiropractor would use that afternoon?" ends with a
decision. The framing question is 80% of the session's quality
and takes two minutes to write.

The second truth, from the research on group ideation (Diehl &
Stroebe, 1987, and everything since):

PEOPLE GENERATE MORE AND BETTER IDEAS ALONE, THEN TOGETHER.

Production blocking — waiting for your turn while someone else
talks — cuts a group's output roughly in half. Evaluation
apprehension cuts it again. The fix is brainwriting: a timed
silent burst where everyone writes, then the group reads, clusters,
and builds. The Brainstorm Board page exists to make this the
default.

The third truth, and the one owners resist most:

DIVERGE AND CONVERGE ARE DIFFERENT MEETINGS.
RUN THEM IN THE SAME HOUR, BUT NEVER AT THE SAME TIME.

The moment someone says "that won't work because…" during
divergence, the session is over; everyone else starts editing
before they speak. The moment someone adds a new idea during
convergence, the decision slips. The board's phases are walls.

The fourth truth:

A BRAINSTORM ENDS WHEN SOMETHING HAS AN OWNER AND A FIRST STEP.
NOT WHEN THE TIME RUNS OUT.

SECTION 2: THE THREE MASTER FRAMEWORKS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

FRAMEWORK 1 — THE DOUBLE DIAMOND (Design Council), COMPRESSED
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Four moves, two diamonds, one hour:

   DISCOVER  (diverge on the problem)  — 5 min. What's actually
             going on? Read the constraint (§12), the last
             brainstorm's open questions (§13), the numbers.
   DEFINE    (converge on the question) — 5 min. Write the one
             framing question. Test it: can it be answered with
             a thing you could build or a choice you could make?
   DEVELOP   (diverge on answers)       — 10-15 min. Silent
             brainwriting, then read-aloud, then build-on.
   DELIVER   (converge on a decision)   — 15 min. Cluster, vote,
             decide, assign.

Most rooms skip the first diamond and diverge on answers to a
question nobody wrote down.

FRAMEWORK 2 — SCAMPER AND CONSTRAINT PROMPTS (Eberle; IDEO)
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

When the room stalls, it needs a lens, not encouragement:

   SUBSTITUTE   — what if a different person / tool / channel did it?
   COMBINE      — what two things on the board are one thing?
   ADAPT        — who solved this in another industry?
   MODIFY       — what if it were 10x bigger? 10x smaller?
   PUT TO OTHER USE — what existing asset could do this job?
   ELIMINATE    — what if we removed the step entirely?
   REVERSE      — what's the opposite of the obvious approach?

And the constraint prompts that work for service businesses:
   "What if it had to be free?"
   "What if it had to ship in 26 minutes?"
   "What if the client had to do it themselves?"
   "What would make this problem unnecessary?"
   "What's the version that's embarrassingly small?"

Artificial constraints produce more original ideas than open
prompts. This is one of the most replicated findings in creativity
research.

FRAMEWORK 3 — DOT VOTING AND THE DECISION FILTER
────────────────────────────────────────────────

Dot voting (from the design sprint tradition) works because it is
silent, simultaneous, and cheap. Rules that keep it honest:

   VOTE ALONE FIRST. Everyone places dots before anyone speaks.
   The owner votes last, or the room votes for the owner.

   THE OWNER'S DOTS ARE NOT WORTH MORE. But the owner decides
   after the vote — the vote informs, it doesn't rule.

   STACKING IS ALLOWED. Three dots on one idea is a signal.

   THE TOP THREE GET DISCUSSED. Not the top ten.

Then the decision filter, per top idea:
   IS IT A THING OR A CHOICE? (build / process / experiment,
      or decision)
   DOES IT ADDRESS THE CONSTRAINT? (if §12 names one)
   WHAT'S THE SMALLEST VERSION STILL WORTH DOING?
   WHO OWNS THE FIRST STEP, BY WHEN?

An idea that can't get through the filter in two minutes is
parked, with a reason. That is a legitimate outcome.

SECTION 3: THE PSYCHOLOGY OF WHY BRAINSTORMS FAIL
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

THE HIPPO. The highest-paid person's opinion — in a small
business, the owner's — anchors everything said after it. The
owner speaks last in divergence, and writes rather than talks.

PRODUCTION BLOCKING. One person talks, four wait, three forget.
Silent brainwriting first, always.

THE FIRST-IDEA TRAP. The first plausible idea attracts building-on
before the space is explored. Quantity targets ("20 ideas before
we read any") break it.

EVALUATION APPREHENSION. People pre-edit for the room. Anonymous
or semi-anonymous capture (the board doesn't show authors until
clustering) helps.

CONVERGENCE FATIGUE. Divergence is fun; deciding is work. Rooms
run out of energy exactly when the decision is due. Time-box
divergence hard so the decision gets fresh minutes.

THE SOLO OWNER'S VERSION. Most Build Room members brainstorm alone
or with one other person. Every rule above still applies: write
before you judge, use the prompts as your second voice, vote with
dots even alone (it forces a ranking), and end with an owner —
which is you, with a date.

SECTION 4: WHAT WORLD-CLASS OPERATORS DO DIFFERENTLY
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

THEY BRAINSTORM FROM THE FILE. The Business File already says who
the client is (§2), what the offer is (§3), what the funnel does
(§6), what the constraint is (§12), and what was left open last
time (§13). A brainstorm that starts blank wastes fifteen minutes
rediscovering it.

THEY WRITE THE QUESTION BEFORE INVITING ANYONE.

THEY USE A VISIBLE BOARD. Ideas that aren't visible get repeated
or forgotten. The Brainstorm Board page is the shared surface —
one screen, projected or shared, everyone reads the same list.

THEY TIME-BOX AND STOP. Ten minutes of divergence, done. The
timer is the facilitator.

THEY PROTOTYPE IN THE ROOM. When the winning idea is a BUILD and it
is small enough, the best sessions make a rough version before
anyone leaves — a page, a one-screen tool, a draft. The Rent
Calculator was decided and built inside one session. The
prototype is the decision made real; it either survives contact
with a member on Tuesday or it doesn't, and both are answers.

THEY CAPTURE WITHIN THE HOUR. The session ends with the board
exported and the Brainstorm Capture run — not "I'll write it up
later."

SECTION 5: THE MOST COMMON SESSION FAILURES
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

1. NO FRAMING QUESTION. "Let's brainstorm content." Produces a
   list, never a decision.

2. TALKING FIRST. Skipping silent brainwriting. Output halves.

3. JUDGING DURING DIVERGENCE. One "that won't work" and the room
   pre-edits everything after.

4. VOTING BY DISCUSSION. The loudest idea wins. Dots first, talk
   second.

5. DISCUSSING EVERYTHING. Ten ideas get five minutes each; none
   gets a decision.

6. THE SESSION ENDS ON TIME INSTEAD OF ON A DECISION. Fifty
   minutes of ideas, zero owners.

7. NOT CAPTURING. The photo of the whiteboard. See the Brainstorm
   Capture knowledge base.

SECTION 6: THE BRAINSTORM BOARD PAGE
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

The Build Room ships a single-file page, Brainstorm_Board.html,
that runs the session's mechanics so the facilitator can think.
It opens in any browser, works on a phone, keeps everything in
the browser until you copy it out, and walks six phases:

   1. FRAME    — the question, who's in the room, the time box,
                 and anything carried forward from §13
   2. DIVERGE  — a timer, one-line capture with Enter, who-said-it,
                 and a "nudge me" button that serves constraint
                 prompts when the room stalls
   3. CLUSTER  — drag (or tap) ideas into named groups
   4. VOTE     — dots per person, vote alone, stacking allowed
   5. DECIDE   — the top ideas become outcome cards: type (decision
                 / process / build / experiment / parked), one
                 owner, first step, and why; plus open questions
   6. EXPORT   — "Copy for Claude" produces the block the Brainstorm
                 Capture skill reads

Claude in this session facilitates alongside the board: writes
the framing question with the member, serves prompts when the
board's nudges run out, challenges the vote, runs the decision
filter on the top three, and — if the winner is a small build —
offers to prototype it in the room.

When there is no board (a call, a walk, a whiteboard), Claude runs
the same six phases in the chat and produces the same export block
at the end.

SECTION 7: THE OPERATING PRINCIPLES CLAUDE WILL APPLY
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

When working through Step 2 prompts, Claude will apply these
principles to every output, automatically:

PRINCIPLE 1: QUESTION FIRST
No divergence until a framing question is written and tested.

PRINCIPLE 2: START FROM THE FILE
Discovery reads §12's constraint, §13's open questions and parked
items, and the relevant sections before anyone generates anything.

PRINCIPLE 3: SILENT BEFORE SPOKEN
Brainwriting first. Claude does not offer its own ideas until the
room's silent burst is on the board.

PRINCIPLE 4: NO JUDGMENT IN DIVERGENCE
Claude never evaluates an idea during the diverge phase, and
interrupts anyone who does.

PRINCIPLE 5: PROMPTS, NOT PRAISE
When the room stalls, Claude serves a SCAMPER lens or a constraint
prompt — never "great ideas, keep going."

PRINCIPLE 6: DOTS BEFORE DISCUSSION
Voting is silent and simultaneous. The top three get discussed.

PRINCIPLE 7: THE FILTER OR THE LOT
Every top idea passes the decision filter in two minutes or is
parked with a reason.

PRINCIPLE 8: END ON AN OWNER
The session is not over until at least one outcome has one name
and a first step with a date.

PRINCIPLE 9: PROTOTYPE WHEN IT'S SMALL
If the winning build fits in the room's remaining time, Claude
offers to make a rough version now. Offered, not imposed.

PRINCIPLE 10: EXPORT BEFORE LEAVING
The session ends with the export block produced and the Brainstorm
Capture handoff started.
