# Frank Kern Copywriting Framework

This is the persuasion-first framework distilled from Frank Kern's teaching. Use this whenever you write copy that will go to an end customer — landing pages, headlines, opt-in forms, thank you pages, delivery emails.

## The Core Philosophy

You are not writing marketing. You are writing the way one human talks to another. Plain English. Short sentences. Short paragraphs. Confident but not arrogant. Direct but not pushy.

The best copy doesn't feel like copy. It feels like a friend who happens to be really good at explaining things.

## The 8 Strategic Inputs

Every piece of copy should be grounded in these eight inputs. In the Lead Capture skill, most of these come from the Magic Wand extraction. You won't need to ask the member — you already have the answers.

1. **Objective** — What's the ONE thing this piece of copy must accomplish? For Lead Capture: get the right ICA to opt in.

2. **Argument** — What's the core point? "It is worth giving your email to get this because X."

3. **Audience** — Who is this for? Their world, beliefs, current motivations. (From the ICA Profile.)

4. **Beliefs & Counterarguments** — What does the audience currently believe that's COUNTER to taking action? What objections pop into their heads? (From the Belief Map and ICA Primary Objection.)

5. **Tone & Style** — Casual? Authoritative? Article-like? Sales-letter-like? Who is the writer to the reader — expert, peer, guide? (From the Compass Communication Style.)

6. **Credibility & Empathy** — What proof exists? Case studies, numbers, personal experience. And what shows genuine understanding of their situation? (From the Compass Zone of Genius + any Blueprint proof elements.)

7. **Structure** — The flow of the piece. (For Lead Capture: standard high-converting opt-in page anatomy.)

8. **Call to Action** — What exactly should they DO? Be specific. And what makes it time-sensitive (if anything)?

## Voice & Style Rules (NON-NEGOTIABLE)

These rules apply to every word of customer-facing copy:

### Sentence Structure
- Plain English. No fancy language.
- Short sentences. Often just 5-12 words.
- Short paragraphs. Often just 1-2 sentences.
- Vary rhythm — but lean short.

### Tone
- Conversational. Like one person talking to one person.
- Never corporate. Never "we believe" or "our solution."
- "I" and "you" pronouns dominate. The reader feels addressed personally.
- Inclusive: "Doesn't it make sense for us to..." beats "You need to..."

### Pattern Interrupts
- Subvert expectations regularly.
- If they expect a high price, say "Nothing."
- If they expect a disclaimer, say "Screw the disclaimers."
- If they expect another bullet, write one stark sentence on its own line.

### Damaging Admissions
- Acknowledge downsides honestly.
- "This is expensive." "This isn't for everyone." "You won't get rich overnight."
- Honesty builds massive trust. Counterintuitive but always true.

### Subheads
- Create curiosity or make a promise.
- "What The Hell This Has To Do With YOU?" — Yes.
- "Benefits of Our Program" — Never.

### Emphasis
- Strategic use of CAPS and **bold** for key phrases and numbers.
- Not every other word. The emphasis loses power if overused.

### Questions as Commands
- "Have you ever wondered what would happen if...?"
- Functions as a directive without feeling like one.

### P.S. Sections (for emails and longer pages)
- P.S. is the second most-read element after the headline.
- Use it for urgency, an unexpected objection handler, or a final emotional hook.

### Forbidden
- **NEVER use em dashes.** Use commas, periods, or ellipses instead.
- Never "I hope this email finds you well" or similar corporate filler.
- Never lead with "In today's competitive market..." or any stock opening.
- Never use words like "leverage," "synergy," "utilize" when "use" works fine.

## Persuasion Rules

### Lead with the Most Compelling Element
- Open with the bold claim, the shocking result, or the curiosity hook.
- Never lead with introduction, credentials, or scene-setting.
- The first sentence's only job: make them read the second sentence.

### Stack Proof Early and Often
- Specific numbers. Named clients (when possible). Concrete results.
- "27 of my coaching clients" beats "many of my clients."
- "Booked 14 calls in 30 days" beats "got great results."

### Address Objections BEFORE They Form
- Read the Belief Map. Find the #1 objection.
- Handle it in the body of the page, not the FAQ.
- The reader should never finish a sentence thinking "but wait..."

### Stories + Facts
- The most persuasive combination is narrative + data.
- One short story. One specific number. Repeat.

### Qualify the Reader
- Make the offer feel like it's not for just anyone.
- "This isn't for tire-kickers. It's for [specific person] who [specific commitment]."
- Counterintuitively, qualification increases conversion among the right people.

### Future-Pace
- "Imagine what happens when..." or "Picture yourself..."
- Take them to the post-purchase moment in their imagination.
- The brain experiences vivid imagined outcomes almost as real ones.

### Genuine Urgency
- Real reasons to act now: limited cohort, real deadline, dated bonus.
- Never manufactured scarcity. The reader smells it instantly.

### Crystal-Clear CTA
- "Click here to download the guide" beats "Submit."
- The button text should describe what happens when they click.

## Applying This to Lead Capture Pages

### The Headline
- Lead with the specific outcome OR mirror the ICA's exact situation.
- 5-10 words ideal. Test against the five-second test.
- Should answer "what do I get?" instantly.

### The Opening Hook (first 2-3 sentences after headline)
- Mirror their situation. Use their language from the extraction.
- Make them feel seen, not sold to.
- Plain English, short sentences. No selling yet.

### The What-You-Get Section
- Outcomes, not features.
- Verbs first: "Get..." "Discover..." "Book..." "Finally..."
- Each bullet specific enough to be measurable or visual.

### The Form Micro-Copy
- One sentence above the form.
- Tell them exactly what happens after they submit.
- Set the expectation of speed: "I'll send it in the next 2 minutes."

### The CTA Button
- Action + outcome.
- "Send Me The Guide" beats "Submit."
- "Show Me The 5-Page Funnel" beats "Get Started."

### The Trust Statement
- One short line below the button.
- "No spam. Unsubscribe anytime." or equivalent.
- Removes the final friction point.

## When You Get Stuck Writing

- Pull a relevant example from the extraction (the ICA's own language).
- Generate 3 versions, pick the one that feels most like the Compass voice.
- Simplify: "If you were telling a friend this at a coffee shop, what would you say?"
- Remember: it doesn't have to be perfect on the first pass. Get something on the page.

## What Frank Would Say

If the copy feels boring, generic, or like every other landing page on the internet — it's wrong. The whole point is to sound like a real person who actually understands the reader's problem and has a useful answer.

If you find yourself writing "Welcome to..." or "In this guide you'll learn..." — stop and rewrite. That's not how humans talk.

Plain English. Short sentences. Honest claims. Specific proof. Direct CTA.

That's the whole game.
