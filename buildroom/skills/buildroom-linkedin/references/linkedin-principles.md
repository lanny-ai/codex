# LinkedIn in 2026: what the evidence says

The rulebook behind every recommendation this skill makes. Each line is tagged: **[LinkedIn]** (LinkedIn's own announcement, help page or executive on record), **[data: who, sample]** (a third-party dataset), **[practitioner: who]** (a named expert's method), **[folklore]** (repeated widely, no traceable source). Say which kind of claim it is when you tell the member. Never present a practitioner claim or folklore as a LinkedIn rule.

Full sources and dates: `research_notes/World class organic LinkedIn pipeline/` in the Build Room repository, and the summary report `reports/World class organic LinkedIn pipeline.md`.

## The eight things that matter

1. **The feed now matches posts to people by topic, not by who they are connected to.** In March 2026 LinkedIn rebuilt feed retrieval and ranking around language models and a "Generative Recommender" that reads each member's history of interactions, to understand "what a post is about" and how it fits a member's professional interests. [LinkedIn, Mar 2026] Van der Blom's 2026 data puts network-based distribution at about 10%, down from 70% in 2024, and finds that sticking to 2 to 3 core topics for 90 days goes with about 27% more reach. [data: van der Blom 2026, ~1.3M posts, figures relayed secondhand]
   → **Pick 2 to 3 pillars tied to the offer and stay on them for at least 90 days.** The headline, About section and posts all say the same thing.
2. **Organic reach has fallen; conversations have not.** Reach for active creators fell about 60% over two years [data: van der Blom 2026]; the median personal-profile post gets about 860 impressions [data: AuthoredUp, 3M+ posts, Mar 2025 to Feb 2026]. At the same time LinkedIn reports comments up 24% to 30% a year [LinkedIn], and since August 2026 it is reported to rank comments for each viewer and show more live discussions in the feed [LinkedIn via press; one trade source].
   → **Write for a small, relevant audience and for comments, not for likes or virality.** A post with 800 impressions from the right 800 people is a good post.
3. **Fake engagement is detected and penalised.** Pods, automated or AI comments and "comment to agree" prompts are reduced, their comments removed from "Most relevant", and accounts can be restricted. [LinkedIn, 2025–2026] Keyword-gated lead magnets ("comment GUIDE") fell to about half the author's usual reach from late July 2026. [data: MagicPost, 684k posts; the study's two figures, 52% and 62%, disagree]
   → **No pods, no automation, no bait.** Offer the resource openly (`account-safety.md`).
4. **Formats on personal profiles, ranked by reach relative to the author's own average:** PDF document carousels 1.39x reach and 1.30x engagement (only about 5% of posts use them); single image 1.20x and 1.33x; text only 1.07x and 0.78x; native video 0.86x (median video reach down 36% a year); polls 1.78x reach but 0.37x engagement. [data: AuthoredUp, 3M+ posts; some recaps credit the same multipliers to van der Blom 2025] LinkedIn is pushing video (watch time +36%) and down-ranks "videos that don't match the post text". [LinkedIn]
   → **Default to text with an image, and one document carousel a week.** Video for trust with people who already follow; polls only for genuine market research. Not three carousels in a row. [practitioner: van der Blom]
5. **Cadence: 2 to 5 posts a week is enough.** Buffer's study of 2M+ posts found the per-post gain from 2 to 5 posts a week over 1, with smaller gains above; van der Blom now recommends 2 to 4. [data: Buffer 2025; practitioner: van der Blom] Half of a post's impressions arrive within 48 hours, so the "golden hour" is overstated. [data: Metricool 2026, 673k posts] A LinkedIn VP says 2 to 5 posts a week, and that posting more than once a day "won't hurt your total reach"; the old 18-hour spacing rule dates from 2023. Two-week gaps showed no engagement effect in one small study. [LinkedIn via press; data: Ordinal] Midweek is strongest, but studies disagree on the exact days (Buffer's 2026 data ranks Wednesday, Thursday and Friday highest and Monday and Tuesday lowest; older studies say Tuesday to Thursday) and on the hour, so post in the buyer's working hours and let the member's own analytics decide. [data: Buffer 4.8M posts, Sprout, Hootsuite]
   → **Three posts a week is the default for a busy expert.** Consistency beats volume.
6. **Links in posts: disputed.** LinkedIn says there is no penalty when a post leads with value [LinkedIn, Sep 2025]; van der Blom measures about 19% less reach for one link in the body [data]; Metricool 2025 found link posts got 14% more interactions [data, 577k posts]. 
   → **Keep most posts complete without a link.** When a link is the point, test body versus first comment on the member's own account.
7. **AI-written text is a reach risk, not a rule break.** LinkedIn allows AI-assisted writing, asks members to review and edit it, added a "Seems like AI slop" report button in July 2026 (over a million clicks in a month), and replaced its own AI post writer with a proofreader that keeps the author's voice. [LinkedIn] Within the same author, posts that read as AI got 22% less engagement. [data: LigoSocial, 134,522 posts]
   In May 2026 LinkedIn said it identifies generic content correctly 94% of the time in testing, and that AI-looking posts without a clear point of view are "less likely to be widely distributed beyond a person's immediate network"; AI is fine as long as posts "represent your voice and your perspectives". Its product chief says flagged posts get about 40% fewer views. [LinkedIn, May and Jul 2026]
   → **Capture the member's own words first (voice notes, interview answers), then shape them.** The member's 10 to 25 years of specific stories are the one thing a model cannot fake.
8. **The first lines and the length.** LinkedIn publishes no "see more" cut-off; plan on about 140 characters (about three short lines) on mobile and about 210 on desktop, and remember line breaks move the fold. [practitioner planning figures] Longer posts that carry a whole idea do best: AuthoredUp's 372,000-post study peaks at about 1,300 to 2,500 characters, Ordinal (248,000) at 2,000 to 3,000, and the weakest bands are about 600 to 1,300 characters (AuthoredUp) and 600 to 1,500 (Ordinal). Image posts and carousel captions do best shorter, about 800 to 1,000, because the visual carries the idea [data: AuthoredUp, 709,000 posts]. [data] Story openers lead in AuthoredUp's 310,000-post hook study; questions come last. [data]
   → **Open with the reader's situation or the claim inside the first 140 characters; then give the full idea.** Short punchy posts are fine as a change of pace, not the default.

## Other formats

- **Articles** get about 0.65x the reach of a text post [data: AuthoredUp]: use them only as a home for a long piece you will link to.
- **Newsletters** invite the member's connections and followers only with the first edition [LinkedIn Help]: start one when there are about 500 followers, so that single invitation counts.
- **LinkedIn Live** must be scheduled as an event since 22 June 2026 [LinkedIn]: worth it for a member who already runs workshops.

## Personal profile, not company page

Personal profiles get about 63% higher engagement than company pages [data: Metricool 2026] and company-page posts reach about 1.6% of followers [data: Ordinal]. A solo expert posts from the personal profile; a company page is optional credibility. Creator Mode was retired in early 2024 and its tools (Follow button, Featured, newsletter, Live) went to everyone. [LinkedIn]

## What converts readers into clients

- Only about 5% of B2B buyers are in the market at any moment (the 95:5 rule; B2B Institute and Ehrenberg-Bass), so most posts build memory: the member is the obvious choice when the need arrives. [data/model]
- 91% of "hidden" buyers say strong thought leadership makes them more open to outreach; 85% value demonstrated understanding of their specific challenges. [data: Edelman–LinkedIn 2025, ~2,000 respondents]
- Proof posts (client stories with numbers and a timeframe) are the bridge from authority to buying. [practitioner consensus]
- Warm signals beat cold volume: LinkedIn's own InMail data shows higher acceptance when the person recently posted (+45%), changed jobs (+62%) or when the sender viewed their profile first (+78%). [LinkedIn, 2022 data]
- A connection note barely changes acceptance but lifts replies (8.2% versus 5.3%). [data: Belkins 2026; five datasets agree on acceptance]
- Messages under about 400 characters get about 22% more responses. [LinkedIn Talent, 2021–22 data]
- Three-message sequences beat five or more (9.8% versus 5.0% replies). [data: Expandi H2 2026, 2,966 campaigns]

## Timelines (two clocks)

- **Warm outbound** (people who already know the member, plus warm signals): conversations and calls in weeks 1 to 4 for an established expert. [Build Room inference]
- **Content inbound:** months. Practitioners agree on 3 to 6+ months of consistent posting and commenting before inbound is reliable; there is no large-sample study. [practitioner consensus] Courses promising "5 clients in 30 days" rest on survivorship bias.

Say both clocks at the start, so the member judges the right thing at the right time.

## Folklore: do not repeat as fact

- "360Brew is LinkedIn's feed algorithm." It is a real LinkedIn research paper; LinkedIn has not confirmed it runs the feed, and the March 2026 announcement does not use the name.
- "The first 60 minutes decide everything" (golden hour). Contested; half of impressions arrive within 48 hours.
- "Comments of 15+ words count double", "saves are worth 5x a like", dwell-time multipliers, "editing in the first 10 minutes kills reach", "pods cause a 97% drop", "AI posts get 2.8x less reach", "vertical video gets 34% more engagement". No traceable source.
- "Turn on Creator Mode", "add 3 to 5 hashtags". Both obsolete: Creator Mode is gone and hashtags no longer add reach.
- "Personalised notes double acceptance." Five datasets say no.
