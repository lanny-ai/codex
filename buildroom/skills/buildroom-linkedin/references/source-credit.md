# Source credit

This skill adapts methods taught publicly by the people below, and data published by the organisations below. It does not reproduce their paid courses or reports; the summaries come from their public writing, interviews and third-party write-ups, gathered in October 2026 (research notes in the Build Room repository, `research_notes/World class organic LinkedIn pipeline/`).

**Practitioners**
- **Justin Welsh** — the Who/What/Outcome headline; the banner as prime real estate with one call to action; Featured limited to one or two links; the content matrix (sub-topics × styles); hub and spoke; trigger-based, low-pressure DMs; the comment formula (specific compliment, own insight, question).
- **Lara Acosta** — SLAY (Story → Lesson → Actionable → You) and the case for stories over pure teaching.
- **Jasmin Alić** — the first three lines carry the post; comments as distribution; "sell from your content, not in it".
- **Matt Barker** — problem, agitate, solve; relatable content over pitches.
- **Richard van der Blom** (Just Connecting, *Algorithm Insights* 2025 and 2026) — interest graph over network, topic consistency, cadence of 2 to 4 posts a week, 25 to 30 minutes of commenting a day, format and link data (figures relayed secondhand; the reports are paid).
- **Sahil Bloom** — named mental models; curiosity hooks that close their loop.
- **Chris Donnelly** — the save-worthy free-resource carousel.
- **Alex Hormozi** (*$100M Leads*) — hook, retain, reward; give away the method, sell the implementation.
- **Josh Braun** — permission to say no; "poke the bear" questions.
- **Morgan J. Ingram** — sell the meeting, not the product; behaviour-filtered lists; short video and voice messages.
- **Harry Dry** — the three tests for a line of copy.
- **Frank Kern and Gary Halbert** — the copy systems every draft passes through (references copied byte-identical from the Copy Engine).
