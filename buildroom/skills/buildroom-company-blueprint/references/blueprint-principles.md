# The ideas, in plain words

## Three kinds of work

Every owner does three kinds of work, usually in the same hour.

| Kind | The question it answers | Looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Direction | Where is this business going? | Choosing the offer, the market, the next year |
| Organising | How does it run the same way every time? | Roles, procedures, numbers, reviews |
| Doing | What needs doing today? | Client work, sales calls, invoices |

Most owners of small service businesses spend nearly all their time on doing, because they are good at it and it is urgent. The business then grows only as far as their hours. The blueprint is organising work: a few sittings spent so that some of the doing can move to someone else.

## Working on the business

Two tests for anything the skill produces:

- **Could a properly trained person run this without asking the owner?** If not, it is a note to self, not a system.
- **Is this less work for the owner, or more?** A blueprint that the owner must maintain alone every week is another job. Keep it small enough to be used.

## Build it as if someone else will run it

Even if the member never hires anyone, writing the business as if another person will run each part makes it clearer what the parts are. It also makes selling, holidays and illness survivable. Nobody is being told to franchise.

## The test of whether something can be taught

Before calling a position or procedure done, ask:

1. Could a new, capable person learn it from what is written?
2. Is the result it should produce stated?
3. Is it clear who owns it?
4. Can someone else see whether it was done well?
5. Are the usual exceptions covered?
6. Would it still work on a day the owner is away?

A "no" is a gap to name, not a reason to stop. `blueprint.py check` covers what a script can see of these.

## How the pieces connect

The chart says which seats exist. A position contract says what each seat is for. Its measures say how anyone would know it is going well. Its procedures (the SOP Creator, §9) say how the work is done. Training (§11) teaches the procedures. The weekly view (§12) shows the numbers. The review asks why a number moved. Each piece names the next, and that is the point: documents that do not point to each other are not a system.

## What is ours and what is borrowed

Borrowed, in our own words: the idea that craft skill is not business-building skill, the three kinds of work, working on the business, and designing the business as if it will be run by others. These are long-standing ideas in small-business writing; `source-credit.md` says where from.

Ours: the member's-own-numbers rule, the refusal of benchmarks, the hand-off ranking, the five-step read, the cause-before-person review, and the link to the Business File. None of these is an official part of anyone else's programme, and the skill must not present them as such.
