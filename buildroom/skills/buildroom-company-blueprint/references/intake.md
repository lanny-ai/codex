# The aim: three questions

The blueprint is organised around what the owner wants, so it starts there. Three questions, in the member's words.

| Key | Question | May already be in |
|---|---|---|
| `primary_aim` | What do you want your life to look like, and what must the business do to support it? | §17 Compass, §1 |
| `objective` | Where is the business going in the next one to three years? | §1 goals, §3, §6 |
| `owner_role` | Which work do you want to keep doing yourself, and which do you want off your plate? | §17, §10 |

## Draft first, then ask

1. `blueprint.py prefill` shows what the Business File holds for each question, and what is already built (procedures, role, training, weekly view).
2. Where the file supports an answer, write it in the member's own words from that text only: `blueprint.py aim --key primary_aim --text-file a.txt --from "§17" --draft`.
3. Show the member **one** summary of every draft and ask what is wrong. Fix it. Then `blueprint.py confirm --all --by member`. Only the member confirms.
4. Ask whatever is left, one question at a time. Long spoken answers are the best ones.

A blank or not-started section is never a source. Where the file is thin, ask; never fill a gap.

## What is never drafted

Hours, baselines, targets, head counts and anything about a named person. Only the member has those, and a wrong one misleads every step after it.

## If the member says "I don't know what I want"

That is common and fine. Ask the smaller versions:

- "What did you do last week that you would happily never do again?"
- "If you took a month off in the spring, what would break first?"
- "What would you do more of if the rest ran itself?"

Record what they say as `owner_role`. The **Compass** goes deeper; offer it once.

## Reading the answers

The aim decides the blueprint's size. An owner who wants a small, calm practice needs four positions and one hand-off. An owner who wants a team of ten needs the full chart. Say which you heard, and ask if that is right, before designing anything.
