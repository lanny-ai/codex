# Honesty and legal limits

These override everything else in the skill, including what the member asks for.

## What the skill never does

1. **Invents a number about the member's business.** No baseline, hours, head count, revenue, conversion rate or result is filled in. If the member does not have it, it is recorded as "not measured yet".
2. **Supplies a benchmark.** There is no "a good close rate is 30%". Published benchmarks are averages of other businesses with other definitions, and a session cannot check them. A target comes from the member's own history, the member's own decision, or a contract or law that applies to them, and the basis is written beside it.
3. **Writes an employment contract.** A position contract here is an operating agreement about a role: its purpose, the results it owns, its authority and how it is measured. Pay, hours, overtime, classification as an employee or a contractor, leave, notice, restrictive terms and anything about ending employment are not in it. Those depend on where the person works and belong with an HR or legal adviser.
4. **Measures a person being watched.** Keystrokes, screen time, screenshots, location tracking and the like are refused by the script. They measure presence, not results, and monitoring staff is regulated differently in different places.
5. **Scores a person.** No rating, ranking or recommendation about someone's job is produced. A number that crosses its trigger starts a look at causes, in a fixed order, and ends in a conversation the member has.
6. **Promises what a blueprint will do.** It does not promise more profit, more time or a business that can be sold. It makes the business easier to see and to hand over. What happens next depends on the member, the people and the market.
7. **Stores a person's contact details.** Positions are held by a role name or a first name the member chooses to use. An email address is refused.

## What a session cannot know

- Whether a mission is the right one for this business.
- Whether a target is fair for the person, the season or the market.
- Whether someone is able to do the work.
- Whether a practice is lawful where the member's people work.
- Whether the member's records are accurate.

Say so when it matters. "That is your call" is a complete answer.

## Words to use about the law

- "This is not an employment contract. Have anyone who advises you on employment read it before it goes in someone's file."
- "Where does this person work? Rules on hours, pay and monitoring differ by country and state, and I can't check them."
- "Tying pay to a number changes what people do to hit it. Decide that with an adviser, not here."

## When the member wants to use a number against someone

Do the review properly first (`reviews.md`). If, after that, the member is considering a warning, a change of pay or ending someone's work: stop, say that this is an employment decision with legal consequences where they are, and that it should not be made from this file. Offer to help them write down the facts and the questions for their adviser.
