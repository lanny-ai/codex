# Ad principles — the rulebook

Distilled from the Build Room advertising research (October 2026, five research streams). Most figures below came from search summaries of the primary sources, not the sources read in full, and nearly all the effectiveness evidence comes from large advertisers and consumer brands. For a coach or a plumber, every rule here is a **prior to update with the member's own numbers, not a law**. Figures marked † must be checked against the primary source before they appear in anything a member's customer reads.

## 1. What separates world-class from competent

**Competent** work is clear, on brief and well made. **Great** work adds a non-obvious insight and an execution a competitor could not run. Three things produce it:

1. A **single-minded, specific idea**: one proposition, stated so a stranger could falsify it.
2. **Distinctive, human execution**: a real person in a real place, a small story, the brand's own assets.
3. **A disciplined loop**: distinct concepts tested, keyed, logged, and the next brief written from the result.

## 2. Why creative is the job

- Vendor sales-effect studies put creative at about 47–49% of advertising-driven sales lift †, more than reach, targeting and recency combined. Marketers guess 19% †.
- The creative advantage **compounds only when one platform runs long.** Peter Field's IPA work shows the creativity multiplier collapsing (from about 12x to below 4x †) when ideas are burned in short activation bursts. An AI studio can produce endless novelty; endless unrelated novelty does not compound.
- **Rule:** hold the brand platform (face, colour, name, line, promise) fixed for months. Rotate executions (hooks, formats, settings) as frequency climbs.

## 3. Brand and response in one ad

A business spending $1,500 a month cannot fund a separate brand budget. It does not need one: **every response ad carries the brand** (Binet's "false dichotomy" point).

- Pick one to three **distinctive brand assets** (face, colour, name, line, shape) that are unique to the member, and put at least one in the first glance of every ad. Small brands should choose high-uniqueness assets and repeat them, not change them (Romaniuk). Shape-based assets score strongest in recent benchmarks †.
- Write around **category entry points**: the situations that send someone looking ("burst pipe at 10pm," "the month after a launch flopped"), not generic quality claims.
- Only about 5% of B2B buyers are in market at any moment † (Dawes's 95:5). Most people who see the ad are not ready. Memorable beats clever; a share of value-first ads with no ask builds the pool that buys later.

## 4. The classic rules, translated to the feed

| Tradition | Rule | In the feed |
|---|---|---|
| Hopkins | Be specific; key every ad; test cheaply | Numbers, timeframes, named results instead of adjectives; every ad named and UTM-keyed |
| Ogilvy | The headline does most of the work | The first glance of a static, the first line of primary text before "see more," and the first second of video are the headline |
| Caples | Test headlines; story and transformation | Hooks by tactic, tested; the before-and-after told by the real client |
| Schwartz | Match the message to awareness | Unaware/problem-aware: lead with the situation. Solution-aware: the mechanism. Product-aware: the offer and proof |
| Bernbach | Picture and words are one idea; honesty | Write the visual and the line together; the damaging admission ("we're three people, so you talk to the owner") |
| Sugarman | The first line exists to get the second read | Hook, hold, click |
| Halbert | Market first; look personal | An urgent-problem audience; native, post-like creative |
| Kern | Value first, retarget the engaged | 20–40% of variants are helpful content with no ask |

Randomised evidence: in about 105,000 Upworthy headline tests †, word choice alone moved click-through on identical content. Copy differences are real and cheap to test. TikTok/Kantar put roughly 90% of ad-recall impact in the first 6 seconds † of video; front-loading is real.

## 5. The four conflicts, resolved

1. **Brand vs response** → default to direct response (offer, CTA, a measurable result) with brand assets embedded in every ad.
2. **Emotion vs reason** → emotion opens (the felt situation, identity, a story), reason closes (specifics, proof, mechanism, risk reversal).
3. **Long vs short** → awareness sets length, not a rule.
4. **Clever vs clear** → clear wins the first line. A clever line that hides the benefit costs a small business money.

**When traditions disagree, make both and let keyed results decide.**

## 6. Human beats synthetic

- System1/Orlando Wood: features like living characters, faces, dialogue and a sense of place build memory; flat product shots and text-over-image work for short-term response and retargeting. A lean, not a law.
- NIQ's EEG study: consumers spotted most AI ads, called them "annoying, boring, confusing," and showed weak memory even for high-quality ones. The one not flagged had been iterated heavily by a professional.
- Disclosure studies: an AI label lowers perceived authenticity and purchase intent, **most for high-involvement purchases**. Coaching, consulting and professional services are exactly that: the buyer is buying the person.
- **Rule:** the real practitioner and real proof are visible; AI makes the scenery, objects, plates, resizes and variants.

## 7. Proof

- Products with five reviews showed 270% higher purchase likelihood than those with none †, and purchase likelihood peaked at 4.0–4.7 stars †, not 5.0. For services, which cannot be inspected before purchase, proof plausibly matters more.
- Guarantees help only when credible; never invent one.
- A specific, verifiable small result beats a vague large one.

## 8. Meta: what is fact and what is folklore

| Claim | Status |
|---|---|
| Andromeda is Meta's retrieval stage; GEM is its ranking model | Meta engineering posts |
| Conceptually different ads (angle, persona, format, setting) give the system more to match; near-duplicates and even ads shot in one location get grouped | Directional inference from Meta's architecture plus practitioner reports |
| "60% similarity threshold," "8–15 creatives per ad set," "Entity ID" | Folklore; not Meta's |
| "3-second rule" | A measurement convention, not an attention law; front-loading is still right |
| "80%+ watch with sound off" | 2016 publisher data, never official; design for mute anyway |
| Creative testing tool: 2–5 ads (some report 10), equal split, about 20% of budget | Practitioner reporting of the tool †; verify in the member's account |
| Conversion likelihood falls about 45% by the fourth exposure to the same ad | Meta's analytics team † |

Never present folklore as Meta's rule. In briefs, label each platform statement **fact**, **guidance** or **our hypothesis**.

## 9. Small-budget reality

At $300–$3,000 a month, statistical testing is mostly out of reach. So: a monthly cycle, two or three genuinely different concepts, judged on the account's own baseline and cost per **qualified** lead from the CRM. See `launch-kit.md` for the test plan.

## 10. What the studio refuses on principle

Fabricated proof, synthetic customers, photoreal AI heroes, prices or URLs drawn by an image model, cosmetic "variants" sold as new concepts, and claims the member cannot back. These are not style preferences; they are why the studio's ads are safe to run and worth remembering.
