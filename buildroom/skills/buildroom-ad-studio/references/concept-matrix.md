# The concept matrix

The research's production taxonomy (Motion's stack, Schwartz, Ehrenberg-Bass) turned into the session's Phase 1 and Phase 2.

## The four layers of an ad

| Layer | What it is | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Angle** | The reason to care, in one phrase, reused across ads | "Your no-shows are a follow-up problem, not a lead problem" |
| **Mechanic** | The cognitive or emotional move | Reframe · confession · comparison · proof · curiosity gap · enemy |
| **Hook** | The angle's execution in the first glance or line | "I lost $14,000 to no-shows last year. Then I changed one email." |
| **Format** | The vehicle | `photo-hook` static · `us-vs-them` · `quote-card` · founder video (later) |

A **concept** is an angle plus a mechanic plus a format, as one picture-and-words idea. A **variant** keeps the concept and changes one execution element (hook, picture, ratio, colour, CTA wording). Give each concept a family ID (`C1`, `C2`, `C3`) and each variant an ID inside it (`C1-H2`). Never sell a variant as a new concept.

## The matrix (Phase 1)

Rows: personas (from §2; usually one primary and one or two secondary situations). Columns: Schwartz awareness stages. Cells: candidate angles.

| Persona / situation | Unaware | Problem-aware | Solution-aware | Product-aware | Most aware |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| … | | | | | |

How awareness sets the ad:

| Stage | Lead with | Length | Typical hook tactic |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unaware | A situation they recognise | Short, story-led | Curiosity, problem call-out |
| Problem-aware | The problem in their words, and that it is solvable | Short to medium | Problem call-out, confession |
| Solution-aware | Why this mechanism, not the others they tried | Medium; proof | Bold claim with proof, comparison |
| Product-aware | Proof and the offer | Short; specifics | Offer, case result |
| Most aware | The offer and the next step | Shortest | Offer |

Cold paid traffic is mostly unaware to problem-aware. Retargeting is solution- to most aware.

**Category entry points.** List two to four buying situations (why, when, where, with whom they go looking). These become settings and opening lines: "The Sunday night you realise Monday has no calls booked." Situations beat generic quality claims.

Mark the cells this cycle covers. The open cells are next month's brief.

## Hook tactics

Motion's 2026 hit rates, as priors to try, not a ranking († summary figure; verify before quoting it to anyone, see `ad-principles.md`): offer-only 9.3%, confession 8.7%, curiosity 7.8%, bold claim 7.2%. Problem call-out is the service-business staple. Write each concept's three hooks on **three different tactics**, so the test teaches something.

| Tactic | Shape | Guardrail |
|---|---|---|
| Offer | "The 30-minute install call: we build your follow-up live." | Only the real offer and terms |
| Confession | "I ignored my own follow-up for two years." | Must be true and the member's |
| Curiosity | "The one email that brought back 9 old leads." | The ad and page must pay it off |
| Bold claim | "Most coaches don't have a lead problem." | Must be defensible; proof nearby |
| Problem call-out | "Booked calls, then silence?" | The wrong-fit reader should self-select out |

## Diversity check before showing the table

- Do any two concepts share angle **and** mechanic? Merge or replace one.
- Would the three ads look alike in the feed (same layout, same picture, same colour block)? Change one format or setting.
- Have any of these angles already been tested (§18 learning library)? Then it is an iteration; say so.

## Meta and the matrix

Covering different cells is how the member feeds Meta's retrieval system different ads for different people. That is a reasoned inference from Meta's published architecture, not a disclosed rule. Do not claim a similarity percentage, a required creative count, or that a word in the ad targets a job title.
