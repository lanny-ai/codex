# Compliance — Gate G4, consent, disclosure

This skill supports careful production. It is not legal advice, does not certify an ad as compliant, and cannot promise a platform will approve it. Say "reviewed for these checks," never "compliant," "Meta-approved" or "legally cleared." Where a member's offer is regulated (health, money, housing, employment, legal), tell them to check the current rules for their market and Meta's special ad categories before running anything, and keep claims narrow.

Dates and figures below come from the October 2026 research and were not all read in the primary text; † marks those to verify before quoting them to a member's customer.

## The rules that shape the studio

| Rule | What it requires | Studio default |
|---|---|---|
| **FTC Consumer Review and Testimonial Rule, 16 CFR Part 465** (effective October 21, 2024) | Bans fake reviews and testimonials, including AI-generated ones and those from people without real experience; civil penalties up to about $53,088 per violation †. The FTC's first warning letters went to property managers, injury law firms and an accounting firm † — the studio's own customers | Never generate a testimonial, review, rating, result or customer. Only verified, consented quotes from the proof vault |
| **FTC Endorsement Guides** | Material connections disclosed; typical results not misrepresented; claims substantiated | Atypical results labelled with context; every number has a source |
| **New York synthetic performer disclosure law** (effective June 9, 2026 †) | Conspicuous disclosure when an ad shown in New York features an AI-created human not based on a real person; $1,000 then $5,000 per violation † | No photoreal generated people as heroes. Any generated background figure gets an on-image disclosure ("Includes AI-generated imagery") in every ratio |
| **EU AI Act Article 50** (applies from August 2, 2026 †) | Deepfakes labelled so they are immediately recognisable; some marking duties deferred † | Same on-image label for EU delivery; keep provenance metadata |
| **Meta advertising standards** | Self-disclosure for political and social-issue ads; Meta labels output of its own AI tools; whether it auto-labels third-party AI is disputed † | Political, election and social-issue ads are refused. Never strip C2PA or other provenance metadata from generated plates |
| **Copyright** | US Copyright Office (January 2025): purely AI-generated output is not copyrightable; prompts alone are not enough | The member's defensible assets are the human-authored ones: copy, real photography, the composition, brand marks. The ledger and copy manifest document that authorship |
| **Likeness and voice** | State right-of-publicity and digital-replica laws (Tennessee's ELVIS Act, California's) were not researched | Written consent before showing, editing or cloning any real person, including the member's own staff. Block prompts naming celebrities, public figures, brands or characters |

## G4 checklist (per ad, on the exported files)

1. **Proof is real.** Every quote, number, rating, review count, logo of a client and before/after traces to a proof vault entry with source, date and consent. Quotes are verbatim; trimming that changes meaning is not allowed.
2. **The picture makes no claim the business can't back.** A generated "full calendar," "dashboard," "team of ten" or "award" is a claim. Remove it.
3. **Results in context.** A best-case result is labelled as such ("Results vary; this client had…") when the ad implies it is typical.
4. **Never-say claims absent** (§18), and no guarantees, prices, discounts, deadlines or bonuses that §3 does not hold as real terms.
4a. **Guarantees word for word.** Wherever a guarantee appears (hook, sub, primary text, description, landing headline), it is the exact §3 sentence, including when the clock starts and what it covers. A shortened guarantee is a new claim. If the hook needs something shorter, the hook says something else and the exact sentence goes in the sub or primary text. The member signs off the guarantee wording before the first ad that carries it ships.
4b. **Approximations keep their qualifier.** "About 30 minutes a week" in the source stays "about 30 minutes" on the image, not "30 minutes".
5. **Wrong-fit respected.** The copy does not promise outcomes to people §18 says the offer is wrong for.
6. **Sensitive attributes.** No copy asserting or implying the viewer's health, finances, age, race, religion, sexuality or other sensitive traits ("Struggling with your debt?" names the viewer's finances; "For owners rebuilding after a slow year" does not).
7. **AI disclosure** applied where a generated person appears, and noted in the disclosure sheet for every ad that contains generated imagery.
8. **Consent on file** for every real person shown: the member (§18 likeness consent) and any client or staff (vault entry).
9. **No deceptive interface.** No fake buttons, play icons, notifications, close controls, chat bubbles or system warnings.
10. **Destination matches.** The CTA's promise is what the page delivers; offer and terms match.
11. **Audit trail.** The ledger records each generated plate's provider, model, prompt reference and job ID; the copy manifest records the approved words. Together they are the audit log.

## The proof vault

A folder or document the member keeps (location in §18). One entry per proof asset:

```
P-07 · testimonial · "We went from 2 booked calls a week to 9 in the first month." · [client first name + initial, role, town, as consented] · source: Google review 2026-08-14 (link) · consent: written, 2026-09-02, ads allowed · photo: yes (client-photo.jpg, consent same)
```

Types: testimonial, review count and rating (with platform and date), real number (with how it was counted), case study, client logo (with permission), founder photo or footage, deliverable screenshot. Without a vault entry, the proof does not go in an ad.

## The disclosure sheet (in every kit)

One row per ad:

| Ad file | Generated imagery? (what, provider, model) | Real people shown (consent ref) | AI label applied on image? | Claims and their proof IDs | Political/issue check | Reviewer verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Plus the platform note for the member: when uploading to Meta, answer the AI-content and special-category questions truthfully; do not remove the on-image label to pass review.

## Affiliate disclosure

When the setup step sends a member to a provider through the Build Room's referral link, the disclosure line goes next to the link, every time, in the same words: *"This is my affiliate link. If you sign up through it, I earn a commission, at no extra cost to you."* The plain sign-up route is always offered too.
