# Storyboard and shot grammar

The storyboard turns the approved beats into shots the member can film and the edit can assemble. It is a table, not drawings.

## The storyboard table

| # | Time | Shot (framing · angle) | Who does what | Line (exact) | B-roll / cutaway | On-screen text | Sound | End state |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0–2.5 s | MCU, eye level, member centred | Looks up from the laptop to camera, starts speaking on the first frame | "How many hours a week do you spend babysitting your AI?" | — | captions | voice | facing camera |
| 2 | 2.5–6 s | same | continues | "…" | cutaway at 3.5–5.5 s: generated desk scene, slow push-in | captions | voice + music under | — |

Rules:

- **One dominant action per shot.** If a shot needs two things to happen, it is two shots.
- **Start on action.** The member is already speaking or moving on the first frame. Trim every breath and "um" before the first word.
- **End state stated.** What is in frame when the shot ends; the next shot starts from there or the cut makes the change clear.
- **Cutaways cover the voice, not replace it.** B-roll sits over continuous speech (an L or J cut). It shows what is being said, never something else.
- **No cut hides the proof.** A demonstration, a screen recording of a real result, or a client's sentence plays through.

## Shot grammar (what each framing is for)

| Framing | Use it for |
|---|---|
| **CU** (face fills the frame) | The emotional line, the confession, the guarantee said to camera |
| **MCU** (head and shoulders) | The default for talking to camera: intimate and readable on a phone |
| **MS** (waist up) | Showing hands, a whiteboard, a product, a gesture |
| **Insert / detail** | The deliverable, the screen, the number on the page |
| **Wide** | Where they are (place builds memory); used briefly |
| **Screen recording** | Real software, real dashboards, real results; never mocked up |

Angle: eye level by default. A slightly high angle feels intimate; low feels powerful; avoid both unless there is a reason.

Movement: a held phone is fine. One motivated move per shot at most (a slow push-in on the line that matters). No speed ramps, whip pans or zooms for their own sake.

## Pacing

- A new visual idea roughly every 2–4 s in an ad, every 4–8 s in content; never a fixed cut rhythm. A strong continuous take can hold.
- Dead air over about 1.2 s inside the speech is cut (the review flags it).
- The end card holds at least 2.5 s, longer if the copy is longer.
- Cuts per minute are a diagnostic in the review, not a target.

## The four layers in every shot

Plan each separately: **picture** (A-roll, B-roll, card), **spoken words** (the script, exactly), **on-screen words** (captions plus any title or end card, from the manifest), **sound** (voice first, music under, effects only with a reason).
