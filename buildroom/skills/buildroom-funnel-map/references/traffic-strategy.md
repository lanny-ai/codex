# Traffic strategy — proposing the channels instead of asking

_Why this exists: the funnel map's first stage asks "where does traffic enter this funnel?" Most members don't know, and asking turns the session into a guessing game. The file already holds the three things that decide the answer: who the avatar is and where their attention lives (§2), what the offer costs and how it sells (§3), and what is already producing leads (§1). So the session proposes a ranked traffic strategy from those, and the member confirms, edits, or rejects. Added 2026-10-10 from member feedback in the live Funnel Map session._

The rules below are practitioner heuristics for service businesses, coaches, consultants, and small agencies, not research findings. State them as recommendations with reasons. The member's own lead-source history in §1 outranks every rule here.

## The three inputs, and what each decides

| Input | From | What it decides |
|---|---|---|
| **Avatar and attention** | §2 one-line profile, demographics, psychographics, awareness stage; §1 niche | *Where* the person already spends attention and *how aware* they are, which decides organic vs. direct-response vs. relationship channels |
| **Offer economics** | §3 investment, engagement structure, guarantee; §1 price range | *Whether paid traffic can work at all* (a $997 front-end can fund ads a $97 one cannot), and how long a sale takes, which decides whether the channel must warm or can convert cold |
| **Existing sources** | §1 lead sources today; §14 if present | *What is already working*, which is always the first channel to scale before adding a new one |

A fourth input is time: the member's weekly hours for marketing (ask once if §14's weekly habit is empty). A channel that needs ten hours a week is wrong for someone with two.

## The channel menu

Every proposal picks from this menu and says which type it is. Type matters because it sets the cost, the time to first lead, and which Lead Gen Machine module executes it.

| Channel | Type | Cost | Time to first lead | Fits when | Executed by |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Warm network reactivation** (past clients, contacts, list) | relationship | time only | days | there is a list or a contact history at all; always the first channel for an established business | Lead Gen Machine module 1 |
| **Referral system** (clients, partners, peers) | relationship | time only | weeks | clients get results worth talking about; high-ticket offers | Lead Gen Machine module 3 |
| **Partnerships and joint ventures** (other people's audiences, events, co-hosted trainings) | relationship | time, sometimes revenue share | weeks | the avatar already gathers around someone else; the offer is complementary, not competing | Lead Gen Machine module 3; §1 partners |
| **Direct outreach** (email, LinkedIn, DM, phone) | outbound | time, list tools | days to weeks | a definable list exists and the offer solves an identifiable, urgent problem; B2B | Lead Gen Machine module 2 |
| **Live events and webinars** (own or borrowed) | education | time, platform | weeks | the offer needs explanation; the avatar is problem- or solution-aware but not product-aware; high-ticket | Lead Gen Machine module 4; Decision Machine for follow-up |
| **Content: long-form** (YouTube, podcast, newsletter, blog) | organic | time, months of it | months | the member can sustain a weekly cadence and the avatar searches or subscribes; compounding, slow | Lead Gen Machine module 4; Video Engine elective |
| **Content: short-form social** (LinkedIn, Instagram, TikTok, X) | organic | time | weeks to months | the avatar is on that platform daily and the member can show up there in their own voice | Lead Gen Machine module 4 |
| **Search** (Google Business Profile, local SEO, AI-overview visibility) | organic / intent | time, some tooling | months | the avatar searches for the service by name; local or category businesses | content module; the OSCortex deep dive for AI visibility |
| **Paid search** (Google Ads) | paid / intent | money, daily | days | people search for exactly this; the front-end or close rate can fund the cost per lead | outside the Build Room; needs a measured funnel first |
| **Paid social** (Meta, LinkedIn, YouTube ads) | paid / interruption | money, daily, plus creative | days | a proven offer with a measured funnel, a lead magnet or event that converts, and a front-end or lifetime value that covers the cost | outside the Build Room; needs §6 metrics and a scorecard first |
| **Communities and groups** (Skool, Facebook groups, forums, associations) | organic / relationship | time | weeks | the avatar already belongs to one; the member can contribute before asking | Lead Gen Machine module 4 |
| **Speaking and podcast guesting** | borrowed audience | time, pitching | weeks to months | the member has a point of view and a story; the avatar listens to those shows | Lead Gen Machine module 3 or 4 |

## Fit rules (apply in this order)

1. **Scale what works before adding what might.** If §1 names a source that produced real clients, it ranks first, with the strategy being "do more of it, measured."
2. **Match the awareness stage.** Unaware or problem-aware avatars need education channels (content, events) before a direct ask. Solution- and product-aware avatars can take outreach and search.
3. **Price gates paid.** Under roughly $500 front-end with no backend: no paid traffic. $500 to $3,000: paid only after organic proves the message. Above $3,000, or an annual contract: paid search or paid social can work once the funnel is measured, never before.
4. **Relationship channels first for high-ticket.** Anything over a few thousand dollars sells on trust; warm network, referrals, and partnerships beat cold anything.
5. **Hours decide cadence channels.** Long-form content and short-form social are commitments. Propose them only when the member can name the hours and the day.
6. **Measurement before money.** No paid channel until §6 metrics exist and the Funnel Scorecard can read them. A paid channel into an unmeasured funnel is money into the dark.
7. **Two or three, not seven.** Propose the top three. One primary, one secondary, one to test. A member who runs five channels runs none.

## The proposal format

Present this before Stage 1 of the funnel map, as a recommendation the member reacts to, one channel at a time:

```
TRAFFIC STRATEGY — [business]

What the file says: avatar [one line, §2] · awareness [§2] · offer [name, price, §3] ·
sources today [§1] · weekly marketing hours [asked]

1. PRIMARY — [channel] ([type])
   Why it fits: [the §1/§2/§3 reason, in one or two sentences]
   The play: [the specific strategy, not the channel name: e.g. "reactivate the 340 past
   contacts with a three-touch sequence pointing at the install call"]
   Cost: [time or money] · First lead: [days / weeks / months]
   Runs through: [Lead Gen Machine module N / session] · Measured as: [the §6 stage it feeds]

2. SECONDARY — ...
3. TEST — ...

Not now, and why: [one line per channel the member might expect, e.g. "paid social:
funnel unmeasured; revisit after four scorecard runs"]
```

Then: "Confirm, swap, or strike each one. Which is the one we build the map's entry around today?"

## What it writes

- **§6 Traffic strategy** line: `[primary channel] (type) · [secondary] · test: [channel] · first lead expected: [window] · status: proposed / confirmed / running`.
- **§6 Stage 1** of the map is built around the confirmed primary channel: what kind of person arrives (warm, cold, referred), what they are thinking, and which source tag they carry.
- **§13** one `captured` row per channel the member confirmed that needs a Lead Gen Machine module to execute, with that module as the next step.
- **§14** nothing. The Lead Gen Machine owns execution; this phase only decides which channels deserve it.

## What this phase is not

- Not a media plan, ad budget, or creative brief. Paid channels are named and gated, never built here.
- Not the Lead Gen Machine. It picks; the machine runs.
- Not a promise. Time-to-first-lead windows are typical ranges stated as such.
