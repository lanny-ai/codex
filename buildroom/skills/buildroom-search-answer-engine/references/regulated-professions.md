# Regulated professions: what a member may and may not publish

A summary for the skill, not legal advice. Every member in one of these professions checks their own regulator and, where they have one, their compliance officer, before a review ask or a page goes live. The skill asks "is your profession regulated in what it may say about clients or results?" in Phase 0 and applies the matching row to every ask, reply, page and scope note.

| Profession | May | May not | Check with |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Financial adviser (SEC-registered)** | Show client testimonials and third-party reviews on the site only with clear, prominent, on-the-page (not hyperlinked) disclosures: that it is a testimonial or endorsement, whether the person is a client, any compensation, any conflict; keep records; a written agreement for a compensated promoter above $1,000 in a year | Pay or give anything for a review; solicit only happy clients; bury the disclosure; show reviews without compliance sign-off (the SEC's December 2025 risk alert named online reviews and buried disclosures as recurring failures) | compliance officer; SEC Marketing Rule; the state securities regulator |
| **Broker-dealer representative (FINRA)** | Testimonials with the disclosures that it may not represent other clients' experience, is no guarantee of future performance, and whether it was paid (above $100) | Anything else; the firm's advertising review applies | the firm's compliance department; FINRA Rule 2210 |
| **Lawyer** | "We handle X cases"; past results with a "results depend on the facts of each case" line; client reviews where the state allows | "Specialist", "expert" or "certified" without the named certification (Florida requires a "not board certified" line for any expertise claim); "best" or "top" (Texas and others); unfiled ads where the state requires pre-filing (Florida 20 days, Texas within 10 days of first use) | the state bar's advertising rules |
| **Medical, dental, any HIPAA-covered provider** | Publish health information with a named, credentialed author, a reviewer, a reviewed-on date and a scope note; reply to reviews generically; use a client testimonial on the site only with a written HIPAA authorisation | Confirm a reviewer is a patient; mention diagnosis, treatment, dates, insurance or cost in a reply, even if the patient did (OCR settlements of $10,000 and $50,000 for exactly this); make diagnostic claims on a page | the practice's privacy officer; HHS OCR guidance |
| **Therapist, counsellor, psychologist** | Credential pages, method pages, colleague endorsements; leave unsolicited reviews up; reply without confirming a care relationship | Ask current clients (APA 5.05) or current or former clients (ACA C.3.b) for testimonials or reviews; marketing surveys of clients | the licensing board; the professional code |
| **Real-estate agent or broker** | Describe the property and the service | Describe the ideal occupant in any page, post or caption ("perfect for young families", "adults only", "safe neighbourhood", "exclusive", school-quality claims, religious selling points) | the state licensing law; the MLS rules; fair-housing guidance |
| **Insurance agent** | Genuine, current testimonials that apply to the policy sold, reproduced accurately, with any paid spokesperson disclosed (New York, Florida) | Payout or "never had a claim denied" claims; guarantees; "lowest rates" | the state insurance department's advertising rules |
| **Coach, consultant, trainer (unregulated)** | Everything the honesty rules allow | Earnings or results claims without the typical result; any claim about what AI does for clients without substantiation (FTC Section 5 actions in 2026) | the Proof Engine's rules; §18's never-say list |

## Health, legal and money topics on any page

Google's raters put trust first on these topics. Every such page carries, visibly: the author's name, credentials and licence state; a reviewer line where the author is not the licensed professional; a reviewed-on date; and a scope note in plain words:

> This is general information for [state] residents, not [legal / medical / financial / tax] advice, and reading it does not make you a client or patient. For your own situation, talk to a [lawyer / doctor / adviser] who knows it.

`answer_check.py` requires the note when `regulated` is set and warns when the text touches these topics without it.

## Reviews in these professions, in one line each

Adviser: compliance first, disclosures on the page. Lawyer: state bar wording. Clinic: ask freely, reply generically, never confirm. Therapist: do not ask. Real estate: ask freely, watch the words. Insurance: ask freely, no claims about payouts.

## AI-assisted writing

No US federal or state law found (October 2026) requires a small business to label AI-assisted web copy; Colorado's act was repealed and replaced with a narrower one effective 2027 aimed at automated decisions; Utah's duty concerns AI interacting with consumers; California's SB 942 binds AI providers. YouTube requires disclosure of realistic synthetic video and auto-labels it. The honesty rules apply to every claim regardless of who typed it.
