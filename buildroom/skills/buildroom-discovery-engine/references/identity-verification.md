# Phase 0 — Subject Identity Verification

_Adapted from the founder's OSCortex deep-dive methodology (vault, 2026-06-16), where this phase is marked "Skip this and you will waste two hours of work. Do not skip it." The lesson came from a real case: a subject was researched on the domain of his email address, which turned out to be a personal mail-forwarder. His real websites and LinkedIn were never found, and the whole study had to be thrown away._

Run this before any research, for any subject that is not the member's own business already described in §1. It takes about fifteen minutes and it is the difference between a discovery brief and a confident document about the wrong company.

## 0.1 Pull the basics

From the member, the client, or the stated info: full name · stated business name(s) · stated website · email address · geography (city + country).

## 0.2 Verify the website

**Never assume the email domain is the website.** Patterns that are usually not websites: personal mail-forwarders (`firstname@lastname.tld`), administrative addresses (`info@`, `office@`, `admin@`), generic providers (`@gmail.com`), personal ISPs (`@comcast.net`). Treat the email domain as a clue, not a fact.

1. Fetch the stated website. Confirm it loads and is actually theirs.
2. If only an email was given, fetch its domain. A 403, an empty page, a parking page, or `noindex` meta tags is a **signal that you have the wrong domain**, not evidence the business is invisible. Search harder.
3. Search `"[Full Name]" "[business name]"`. Find the real site.
4. Look for several sites: old and new, brand and personal, one per country. Note them all, marked primary / secondary / legacy.

## 0.3 Find their LinkedIn

Search `"[Full Name]" LinkedIn`. Note the profile URL. The legal entity name may differ from the brand.

## 0.4 Find their communities

YouTube, Skool, Discord, Substack, podcast, X. Note each.

## 0.5 Write the verified identity block

Put this at the top of the discovery brief before anything else:

```
SUBJECT VERIFICATION
- Full name:
- Aliases / brand names:
- LinkedIn URL:
- Canonical website(s): primary · secondary · legacy
- Email domain (≠ website):
- Geography:
- Stated business:
- Communities: YouTube · Skool · other
- Third-party associations (orgs, boards, awards, certifications):
```

## 0.6 If you cannot disambiguate, stop

If ten minutes of searching cannot settle which of two or more candidates is the subject, stop and ask the member. A short "which of these is the right business?" beats a long brief built on a guess. Never research on a guess.
