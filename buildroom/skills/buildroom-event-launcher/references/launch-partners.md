# Launch partners: borrowing an audience, done properly

Adapted from Amber Spears' talks at Flight Club ("8-Figure Launches", March 2018; "Affiliate Marketing", July 2019), paraphrased with credit, and from the Build Room founder's own partner launches. Her talks are about recruiting and managing partners at large scale; this file scales them down to a service business with a list of a few hundred to a few thousand and no affiliates yet. Do not reproduce her slides, scorecards, contest copy or scripts, and do not imply her endorsement.

## 1. Before asking anyone

- **Prove the offer first.** Partners promote what converts; "test data" is what opens doors. Run the event once to your own list (or a pilot group) and know your numbers: registrations per send, show-up, buyers per 100 attendees. [Amber Spears] For a service business the partner-facing number is registrants → attendees → booked calls → clients, not affiliate earnings per click.
- **Start 8–12 weeks out.** Her rule is 90 days because good partners are booked; podcast and guest slots book 4–8 weeks ahead. [Amber Spears] `launch_plan.py timeline --partners` schedules it.
- **Know what you can offer back.** A small list cannot repay a big partner's mailing. Offer what you have: a guest training slot for their audience, a podcast or newsletter feature, introductions, done-for-you content, a fair commission, or a co-hosted session. [Amber Spears lists these alternatives]

## 2. Recruit in waves

1. **Your warm circle**: past clients, peers who already like your work, people you have helped.
2. **Five to ten hand-picked partners** whose audience is your buyer, approached one to one, never by mass email. [Amber Spears: the biggest relationships, personally]
3. **Introductions**: ask every yes for one introduction to someone like them. [Amber Spears]
4. **Your own list and social**, alongside.

The founder's own approach when he grew by partners: lead with a give ("I'm looking for offers to promote") and vet partners by what their promotions actually produce, with evidence. [founder]

## 3. Agree it in writing

One page per partner [Amber Spears; founder]:
- what they promote, on which dates, through which channels;
- the commission or the swap, and when it is paid (after the refund window closes, and quickly);
- who owns the leads they send, and what you will and will not send them;
- **who teaches live** (their name is not used to imply they will appear unless they will);
- the disclosure they will use ("I earn a commission if you buy" near the link, in every promotion) [FTC Endorsement Guides];
- what a good result is **for them**, asked plainly and written down; [Amber Spears]
- what happens if it goes badly (a refund reverses the commission; GoHighLevel does not do this automatically).

Partner-sent emails pass the same gate as yours: the founder's July camp went out in a partner's voice that let attendees believe the partner was teaching, and the reminders were in the partner's hands and misfired. [founder] Agree who sends what, from which account, and get the final copy and send times before they go.

## 4. Equip them

A partner kit: two emails, two social posts, a graphic, their tracking link, the dates, the who-teaches line, the disclosure. Personalise it for the top three. [Amber Spears: custom assets for top partners] If there is a leaderboard or prize, keep it small and honest, and reward momentum (registrations they send), not only sales. [Amber Spears]

## 5. During and after

- **Track daily**: registrations by partner, show-up by partner, buyers by partner. [Amber Spears: daily activity numbers]
- **Answer partners fast**, biggest first. [Amber Spears]
- **Pay quickly and do what you promised.** Make a partner whole when something you control goes wrong. "My duty is to my list and my customers first" applies to them too. [Amber Spears]
- **Thank them with results**: a short note with what their audience got out of it, and a spotlight where you can.
- **Grow the partnerships that worked**; one great partner is worth ten lukewarm ones.

The member's partner list, deals and terms stay in the launch folder and their CRM, never in the Business File beyond a one-line summary in §20's fill plan.
