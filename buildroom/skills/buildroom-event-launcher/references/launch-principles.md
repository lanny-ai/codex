# Launch events in 2026: what the evidence says

The rulebook behind every recommendation. Each line is tagged: **[founder]** (the Build Room founder's own method and data, from eight runs of his free 3-day boot camp and his seminar years), **[launch practice]** (widely taught partner-launch practice, no single source), **[data: who, sample]** (a third-party dataset; vendor data says so), **[practitioner: who]** (a named expert's method or self-report), **[folklore]** (repeated widely, no traceable source). ## The ten things that matter

1. **A free first win before the event changes everything after it.** The founder's July 2026 camp put a self-serve tool in front of the event; about 500 people finished it before Day 1, and the tool became the bridge to the offer. [founder] → Every launch gets a first-win tool that takes minutes, starts with an easy question, and is finished before Day 1.

2. **Show-up is the biggest lever and decays every day.** Webinar platforms report 33–60% of registrants attending, depending on definition [data: ON24, Livestorm, Univid, Goldcast; vendors]; creator funnels run about 25% live [practitioner: Scale For Impact, 114 webinars]. Multi-day events lose people daily: the founder's July camp went 969 registered → 380 → 257 → 133, with 67 at the close [founder]; the one measured challenge figure kept 51.8% from Day 1 to Day 5 [practitioner: Heart, Soul & Hustle]. The founder's own rule of thumb is about 80% of the previous day, and his July camp fell short of it [founder; the vault disagrees with itself here, so plan on 50–80%]. → Engineer show-up for every day, not just Day 1.

3. **Reminders help, modestly; stack them.** Independent evidence comes from healthcare: SMS reminders raise attendance by a ratio of about 1.14, and two reminders beat one [data: Cochrane review, n=5,841; trial n=54,066]. Vendor claims of SMS "doubling" attendance are not independently supported [data: vendors]. → Calendar file + confirmation + day-before + morning + 15 minutes + a night-before email for Days 2 and 3; SMS only with consent. Expect relative lifts of 10–30%, not miracles.

4. **Charging something raises attendance less than folklore says.** Median no-show is 28% for free in-person events against 17% for paid; within one event, payers show up only 2–4 points more often [data: Pheedloop, 1,070+ events, April 2026]. → A paid entry ($27–$97) suits a small list that wants committed buyers; it is not a cure for show-up.

5. **Price sets conversion; above about $2,000, sell the call.** Of webinar attendees, about 8–15% buy at $97–497, 5–10% at $500–1,500 and 1–4% at $2,000+ [practitioner: Scale For Impact]. → High-ticket offers close on an application or a call, booked at the event.

6. **Small lists can launch.** 1–2% of a list buying is normal and about 5% strong [practitioner: Denise Duffield-Thomas; Nicola Moors]; a 474-person list produced 25 sales and a waitlist converted 34.9% [practitioner: Nicola Moors]; Stu McLaren is quoted as saying about 200 people is enough for a founding-member launch [practitioner, secondhand]. → Do the math honestly; a hand-raiser waitlist before the cart is worth building.

7. **Sales cluster at a real deadline.** 40% of one launch's sales landed in the final two days [practitioner: Denise Duffield-Thomas]; affiliate data puts 40–60% on the last day [practitioner: Matt McWilliams, 2016]. "Half of all sales on the last day" as a universal rule is [folklore]. → Plan the final day's live touch and emails, and make the deadline real; fake scarcity is now enforced against in the US, UK, EU and Australia [rules research].

8. **A tiny first purchase makes the bigger one easy.** In July 2026, 102 people paid $1 for a personalised brief, which made the core program a one-click upgrade; 21 of the 102 bought it, and in his follow-up analysis 23 of the 41 known to have seen the price bought (56%), while 51 paid $1 and never saw the offer [founder]. → If the member uses a small commitment step, make sure everyone who takes it sees the offer.

9. **Follow-up is where the money leaks.** The founder's ten follow-up workflows sat unpublished after July with 2,181 people waiting in them [founder]. Replays are about half of webinar viewing [data: ON24, Contrast; vendors], and promoting the replay to non-attendees adds about 15% more viewers [practitioner, via Demio]. → Follow up by where each person stopped, the same day, with one next step each.

10. **AI behind the scenes, the host visibly live.** 65% of consumers say brands use too much AI content and 57% trust brand messaging less [data: Gartner, n=1,006, September 2026]; visible AI in marketing lowers trust for 31% and raises it for 7% [data: Klaviyo, n=8,000]. The founder's AI-made Day 3 recap video was his best single asset of the year, and he told the room how it was made [founder]. → AI drafts, recaps and personalises; the host shows up live and says what AI made.

## The founder's rules (keep these)

- **Belief on Day 1, proof on Day 2, the offer on Day 3.** "Day 1 is clarity. Day 2 is revenue. Day 3 is momentum." [founder]
- **Everyone builds something in the first 20 minutes.** "Move one guaranteed-win build into the first 20 minutes." [founder]
- **Sell the free seat, not the offer, in the invitation.** "Every CTA is the same: save the free seat, then show up live." [founder]
- **Do not manufacture urgency.** "The real 'left behind' fear is enough." [founder]
- **Radical transparency.** Tell the room when you are using a persuasion technique. [founder]
- **Do not gate content to force attendance.** His live-only access code "just created friction" and was removed on air. [founder]
- **Let ready buyers buy.** Slowing down someone who wants to pay is a leak. [founder]
- **Offer a plan, never a deeper discount; plans must not punish.** "$997, or 3 payments of $349. $50 for the convenience." [founder]
- **Access is the acceptance test.** A sale is delivered when the buyer has reached the thing, not when they have paid. [founder]
- **Feedback is a gift.** Debrief in two passes, what worked first, the person self-assesses first, push for specifics. [founder]

## Partner-launch practice

How to recruit and manage launch partners; it does not cover running the event or the close.
- **Prove the offer before you scale it** with a small test; partners need data. [launch practice]
- **Start early**: big partners book up months ahead; for a small business, 8–12 weeks. [launch practice]
- **Recruit in waves by leverage**, biggest relationships first, one-to-one, then introductions from every yes. [launch practice]
- **Ask every partner what a good result looks like for them**, and follow up against that. [launch practice]
- **Put every deal in writing**, including what happens if it goes badly. [launch practice]
- **Have the tech and tests ready before launch day**; launches break servers. [launch practice]
- **Your list and customers come first**; disclose weaknesses up front and make partners whole. [launch practice]
- **Track daily activity**, not only results. [launch practice]

## Folklore: do not repeat as fact

- "Half of sales happen on the last day" as a universal law.
- "SMS doubles attendance."
- "Paid challenges complete at 35–55%" (a vendor claim with a sales motive).
- "Attendance is always 47%."
- "Gamification multiplies engagement 30x."
- "Webinars are dead." They are not; formats change.
- Resetting countdowns and "live" labels on recordings as harmless tricks. They are enforcement targets.
