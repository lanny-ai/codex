# Week 1: Warm Network Activation

## Knowledge Base

### The Core Truth
Your next client almost certainly already knows you. Alex Hormozi built his first $4,000/month by messaging everyone he already knew. Warm outreach achieves response rates up to 45% vs. cold email's 1-5%. Word-of-mouth generates 2x+ sales of paid advertising (McKinsey). Referred customers have 37% higher retention (Harvard Business School).

### Framework 1: Hormozi's Core Four + Warm First Principle
From $100M Leads — four ways to let people know about your business: (1) Warm outreach, (2) Content posting, (3) Cold outreach, (4) Paid ads. Sequencing: "Start with warm outreach. It's the fastest path to first revenue."

**Process:** Compile contacts (aim for 1,000). Apply Rule of 100 — contact 100/day, first 4 hours. Follow up 2-3 times with non-responders.

**The ACA Framework (Acknowledge, Compliment, Ask):**
"Hey [Name], I know you're connected to some serious people in [industry]. [Compliment — raise their status as a connector.] I just launched [specific service] that helps [specific ICA] get [specific result]. Who's the one person you know right now who might benefit? I'd love to help them out."

Why it works: compliments them (Cialdini liking principle), removes risk (they're connecting not buying), frames them as valuable resource, makes saying yes easy and status-elevating.

### Framework 2: Warm Lead Activation Spectrum
Five tiers from hottest to coolest:

**Tier 1 — Former/Current Clients (Hottest):** Already experienced your value. Best action: personal re-engagement call. Ask: "Are you still working on [problem]? Do you know anyone who is?"

**Tier 2 — Professional Contacts Who've Seen Your Work:** Colleagues, collaborators, mentors. Best action: personal message referencing shared history. Use ACA framework.

**Tier 3 — Personal Network Who Know and Like You:** Friends, family. Most overlooked. Best action: simple honest explanation of what you're building + specific ask.

**Tier 4 — Digital Connections:** LinkedIn, followers, email list. Warm because they chose to connect. Best action: value first, ask second.

**Tier 5 — Content Engagers (Coolest warm):** People who liked/commented/shared. Best action: direct personal message referencing the specific content they engaged with.

### Framework 3: Warm Introduction Architecture
Three types: (1) Direct Introduction — contact actively makes intro. (2) Permission Introduction — contact gives permission to use their name. (3) Social Introduction — contact comments/tags/shares with endorsement.

**How to ask properly:** Don't say "Do you know anyone?" (too vague). Instead: check their LinkedIn connections, identify 5-10 specific ICA matches, send the list, provide pre-written intro email they can send with one click.

**Pre-written intro template:** "Hey [Prospect Name], I wanted to connect you with [Your Name]. They help [specific ICA] with [specific outcome] and I thought of you immediately because [specific reason they're a fit]. Worth a quick 20-minute conversation — I think it could be valuable."

### Framework 4: Network Map Methodology
**Full Contact Audit sources:** Phone contacts (A-Z), email sent folder (3 years), LinkedIn, social media, past colleagues/employers, former clients/prospects, communities/groups, events, neighbors/local business owners.

Most people think they know 150-200 people. After audit: typically 500-2,000.

**Contact Scoring:** A = Currently relevant ICA or directly connected. B = Tangentially connected, could refer if asked well. C = No direct connection but trusts you.

Focus first week entirely on A contacts (most have 20-50 un-activated).

### Psychology of Warm Outreach
1. Trust is pre-installed — conversation starts 3-5 steps ahead of cold
2. People want to help people they like (Cialdini liking principle)
3. The Status Trap — ask for connection and advice, not sales
4. Specificity enables referrals — generic descriptions produce zero referrals
5. Reciprocity compounds (Yale SOM) — referrers become MORE loyal advocates

### Common Failures
1. Being too vague about what you need
2. Treating warm contacts like cold prospects
3. Asking once and giving up
4. Never asking at all
5. No tracking
6. Neglecting past clients (40% likely to buy again — UserGems)

### Message Templates
**Template A — Reconnect + Curiosity (Tier 3):** "Hey [Name] — it's been too long. Hope you're doing well. I've been heads-down building [specific thing] for [specific type of person]. Quick question — do you still know people in [specific industry]? Working on something I think could be genuinely useful for them."

**Template B — Direct Referral Ask (Tier 2):** "Hey [Name] — I wanted to reach out because I'm actively growing [service] for [specific ICA]. You know so many people in that space and I immediately thought of you as someone who might know a few people who'd benefit. Who's one person you know right now who's dealing with [specific problem]? I'd love to have a quick conversation with them."

**Template C — Warm Intro Request:** "Hey [Name] — I was looking at your LinkedIn connections and noticed you're connected to [Specific Person] at [Company]. Based on what I know about their situation, I think there could be real value in a conversation. How well do you know them? If it's a solid connection, I'd love to make it easy — I can write a 2-sentence intro you could forward to them if you're open to it."

**Template D — Past Client Re-engagement:** "Hey [Name] — I was thinking about the work we did together on [specific project] and wanted to check in on how things are going with [the specific outcome we worked on]. Are you still working through [original challenge] or has that evolved?"

### Operating Principles
1. Fastest result first — warm network produces conversations faster than any other channel
2. Specificity enables referrals — name specific ICA, problem, and outcome
3. Connection over transaction — sounds like conversation, not pitch
4. Make it frictionless for them — provide everything they need to act
5. Track everything — separates system from hope
6. ACA first, pitch never — ask for connection, not purchase
