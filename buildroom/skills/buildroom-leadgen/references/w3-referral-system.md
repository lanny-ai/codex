# Week 3: The Referral System

## Knowledge Base

### The Core Truth
Most happy clients WANT to refer you. They just don't know how. 92% of consumers trust friend/family recommendations over any advertising (Nielsen). Word-of-mouth = 20-50% of purchasing decisions (McKinsey). Referred customers: 16% more profit, 37% higher retention (HBS). Up to 20-30% of conversions from referrals with a formal program (Buyapowa).

Key insight: clients think about you most while working together — by the time they talk to someone who needs you, you're out of mind. The system creates deliberate, timed moments.

### Framework 1: The Referral Trigger Map
**Moment 1 — First Win (highest energy, often missed):** Client first experiences meaningful result. Ask feels natural. Most businesses miss by waiting until end.
**Moment 2 — Milestone Completion:** Phase/deliverable complete. Client evaluating. Ask = natural extension.
**Moment 3 — Offboarding:** Standard but late. Enthusiasm dimmed. Best with keep-in-touch structure.

Proactive timing: ask when happy, not when desperate.

### Framework 2: Referral Psychology (Cialdini Applied)
**Reciprocity:** Real delivery creates natural obligation. Best trigger = extraordinary service.
**Social Proof:** Sharing client results publicly triggers "I should tell [Name]."
**Commitment/Consistency:** "Happy with how things are going?" (yes) → "Open to sharing?" (yes) → "Who's one person dealing with [problem]?" (name)
**Liking:** Relationship depth = biggest referral predictor.

### Framework 3: Referral Partner System
Non-competing businesses serving same ICA. Approach peer-to-peer: "I noticed we serve a similar client. I've had clients who needed [their service] and had no one to refer to. Worth exploring mutual referrals?"

No commissions initially. Trust first.

### Framework 4: Referral Friction Audit (Yale SOM / UChicago)
1. Don't know WHO → specific description of who to look for
2. Don't know WHAT to say → exact 2-sentence text
3. Don't know what NEXT → clear next step
4. Feels AWKWARD → frame as favor to person being referred
5. No MOMENT → build into delivery at trigger points

### Who Refers and Why
Best referrers: enthusiasm + broad network + willingness to ask. Growth-oriented clients refer more than problem-avoidance clients.

### Common Failures
1. Asking too late. 2. Asking too vaguely. 3. No frictionless mechanism. 4. Not asking at all. 5. Not tracking sources. 6. Transactional framing.

### Operating Principles
1. Timing is primary. 2. Specificity enables action. 3. Near-zero friction. 4. Relationship over transaction. 5. Partners multiply clients. 6. Track every source.

## Prompt System

### System Prompt
You are a referral system architect. You understand the Trigger Map, Psychology Toolkit, Partner System, Friction Audit, and common failures. Output: complete referral system built into delivery, frictionless, tracked. Every ask timed, every ask gives everything needed to act in under 2 minutes, warm human language.

### Input Template
```
MY BUSINESS: What I do and for whom / core result
MY CLIENTS: Current? How many/long? Best client? Past clients in contact?
MY DELIVERY: How engagements run / what "first win" looks like / when it ends
PAST REFERRALS: Any before? Who sent, how, trigger?
PARTNER POTENTIAL: Other services my ICA needs that I don't provide?
```

### Prompt 1 — Referral System Design + Trigger Map
(A) 3 peak moments in their delivery. (B) Built-in trigger per moment. (C) Client Referral Kit: who to look for, 2-sentence intro, what to expect, what you'll do. (D) Post-referral acknowledgment messages.

### Prompt 2 — Referral Ask Message Library
5 scenarios: (1) Current client at First Win. (2) Client at Milestone. (3) Past Client re-engagement. (4) Warm contact, never a client. (5) Passive mechanism (content/case study that triggers referrals without asking).

### Prompt 3 — Referral Partner System
(A) Ideal partner profile. (B) 3 places to find them. (C) Outreach messages (LinkedIn, community, warm intro). (D) Onboarding conversation. (E) Monthly/quarterly check-in.

### Prompt 4 — Handling Referred Leads
First touchpoint, pre-call prep, first conversation structure (more trust changes approach), what to do if lead doesn't convert.

### Prompt 5 — Tracking + System Summary
(A) Tracking system. (B) Quarterly review. (C) One-page summary: moments, language, kit, partners, acknowledgment, tracking, maintenance.
