## The thesis
1. **Find the stage first** [founder's method]. A booked date gets the talk written. The original tool's one job was to find a gig, and it started by learning the speaker in depth.
2. **Clients first, fees second** [Build Room decision]. For a service business the room is the prize. A free talk to the member's own buyers beats a paid one to strangers.
3. **Depth in, quality out** [founder's method]. Seventeen questions, answered out loud and at length. The Business File already answers several.
4. **The right twenty people** [practitioner]. Audience fit counts three times as much as anything else in the ranking.
5. **Hosts choose relevance** [data]. Organisers pick speakers who fit their audience and teach something; canned talks and a pitch dressed as a talk are what they complain about.
6. **No selling from most stages** [host policy]. Local clubs, chambers and associations forbid it in writing. The honest route is a useful free resource the room asks for, with the host's permission.
7. **Measure your own** [data]. No independent numbers exist for reply rates, fees or conversion at this level. The member's own counts are the benchmark.

## Where service business owners get booked
| Stage | Who decides | How far ahead | What they want | Pay |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rotary and service clubs | The program chair | Weeks to a few months | About 20 minutes plus questions, useful to members, no sales | Unpaid |
| Chambers of commerce | Staff or a committee | Varies | An educational session; often members only, sometimes one slot every two years | Unpaid; the real cost is membership |
| Public libraries | A programme librarian, by form | Two to nine months | A public-interest programme, no selling | Unpaid or a small honorarium |
| Associations and trade conferences | A volunteer committee scoring a rubric | Calls close about six to eight months before; state and chapter events four to six | A title, a description, learning objectives, a bio, no sales content | Breakouts usually unpaid; free registration is common |
| Company lunch-and-learns | Whoever runs training or the team | Unknown; ask | Something their staff can use | Sometimes |
| Podcasts as a guest | The host or a producer | Weeks to months | A pitch that shows you have listened, and a topic their listeners want | Unpaid |
| Online summits and guest webinars | The organiser | Months | A topic, and often a promise to email your list | Unpaid; read the terms |
| Speakers bureaus | The bureau | n/a | Speakers who already have demand and video | Out of scope here |

All of it [host policy] from individual hosts' pages except where marked unknown. Each host differs: read their page, then ask.

**What follows from the lead times.** A member starting now is pitching local stages for the next few months and association stages for the second half of next year. Start local, and put the association calls on the calendar.

## What hosts ask for
- Local and association forms ask for a title, a short description, two or three takeaways or learning objectives, and a bio [host policy].
- **A video reel is not shown to be needed for local or association stages.** Bureaus and large conferences want one [practitioner]. A short phone recording of the member speaking is enough to start; don't hold up pitching for a reel.
- Committees score fit with the theme, clear learning objectives, the speaker's competence, and no sales content [host policy].

## Folklore to correct, gently
- "You need a reel, a website and a book before you pitch." Not for local and association stages.
- "Speakers earn $5,000 a talk." The averages in circulation describe established speakers on bureau lists. Fee tables online are unsourced.
- "Ten to fifty percent of the room will buy." Every such figure is self-reported by someone selling a course.
- "Send hundreds of pitches." Podcast producers block senders of templated pitches; a short list, pitched well, does better.
- "Results not typical covers you." It hasn't since 2023 (see `fees-and-terms.md`).
- "A paid directory will bring bookings." No independent evidence either way for new speakers.
- "Being selected for an award or a summit is an honour." If there is a fee, it is a sale.
- "Words are only 7 percent of the message." A misreading of two small studies about mixed feelings, never about talks. What the member says matters most.
- "People fear speaking more than death." An old list, not a finding. Nerves are common and ordinary; that is all that needs saying.
- "Every club and college pays speakers." Most local stages don't pay at all.

## What a script can and can't check
`pitch_check.py` reads text: length, placeholders, figures and claims against the member's own material, quotations against the proof bank, retired lines, pressure, whether it names the host and offers a talk. It can't tell whether a credential is true, whether this is the right person to write to, or whether the talk is good. `speak_pipeline.py` keeps the order honest: a source for every stage, vetting before a pitch, two follow-ups at most, counts instead of estimates.
