# Watchers — Method Guide

Reconstructed from the Build Room session of 2026-06-17 (host Lanny Morton, AI Momentum Labs), which taught the Podcast Watcher and then generated the Watcher Master live, plus the sibling Video-to-Vault sessions of 06-10, 06-11, 06-24, 06-29 and 06-30 and the 08-05 and 08-06 sessions. These Zoom transcripts carry no timestamps, so quotes are cited `[date Lnnn]` by transcript line. *(inference)* marks a reading, not a statement.

The generator skill beside this guide (`SKILL.md` and `source-types.md`, `filter-templates.md`, `vault-protection.md`, `code-templates.md`) is the founder's Watcher Master, unchanged. `video-to-vault/` holds the Week 2 YouTube skills and `podcast-watcher-README.md` the Week 3 podcast watcher's README, unchanged. This guide is what the sessions taught around them.

## 1. What a watcher is, and the lesson underneath

A watcher is a recurring pipeline that points at one external source, pulls its content as text, filters it against the member's goals, and files structured notes into the Obsidian vault, so Claude Code works from curated data instead of the open internet.

"How many of you would like a tool that would grab all of the knowledge out of a podcast channel? Alright, well that's what I'm giving you today." [06-17 L141] "The question to ask yourself is, where's the good stuff that I want to get? And I gotta be honest, I cheated on this one. I was like, where's the best data? And it was like, number one, podcasts." [06-17 L145]

"If you have a source, regardless of that source, you can build a tool to grab the knowledge from that source." [06-17 L146] "I'm giving you the podcast thing, but that's not really the main point. The main point is, point your target wherever you want, and you can pretty much get anything." [06-17 L147] "One's teach the fish, one's give the fish. The fish isn't important here. The important thing is the first part." [06-17 L148]

Why a knowledge base at all: "The reason why we want to operate from a knowledge base at a core level is we're narrowing the information in. If you're in chat, you're using all of the information." [06-17 L181] The month: "Week 1 was set up. Week 2 was YouTube scraping. Week 3 was a bunch of other sources gathering data." [06-24 L537–540] Watchers are how the brain grows without the owner: "What are the recurring ways in which I want it to grow that don't require my presence?" [06-29 L669]

## 2. The pattern, in one sentence and one prompt

"Finding reputable sources, creating a filter of what you want and what you don't want, and then make it recurring, and then you can set it and forget it in a way, and grow your knowledge base organically." [06-11 L873]

The whole thing spoken to Claude Code, for a YouTube channel: "I want you to watch his channel, and I want you to create a repository of the information that's contained within the channel, and I want you to go grab it for the last year. Use the channel watch tool, not the individual video watch tool. Once you've done it for the last year, I want you to do it once a week, and I want you to check for new videos once a week. And add it into the brain. And it's that simple." [06-30 L177–180]

The founder's own filter for the Anthropic channels: "How to use Claude Code effectively. That's it." [06-17 L201]

## 3. How the Watcher Master came to exist

The master prompt, dictated live: "Create a tool that will automatically go out into the web, or some source, to find information from a channel that I choose, take that information, filter it based on my filtering preferences, and I want you to recommend filtering preferences so it's easy, and grab content that matches a particular filter or criteria, and then add it into my obsidian.md knowledge base. Keeping it so that the information that is added is protected from my proprietary information, so it doesn't create or creep into stuff that's important, that's come from and generated by me." [06-17 L414–425]

The AI proposed a skill instead of a prompt: "Build it as a Build Room skill and not a prompt, because if we do a Build Room skill, then you can install it once and generate watchers for any source you want indefinitely. So the podcast watcher becomes one output of this master generator, so would Twitter." The founder: "Oh, that's better. A single skill that you can add any source to. How neat. I didn't even think of that." [06-17 L442–446] Delivered at close as "a watch anything" [06-17 L938–939], with the caveat "I just did that on the fly, so if you have any feedback on that, please let me know." [06-17 L940]

## 4. Filters: let the AI write them from the goals

"I wouldn't just say, take all of this and bring it into my knowledge base." [06-17 L195–196] "If you just take everything then that defeats the whole purpose of having a knowledge base." [06-17 L198] "Even on a good channel, I guarantee you, she's had a stinker of a guest." [06-17 L207] "I will always put filters on it, even if it's a credible source." [07-30 L1454]

The prompt: "I'm about to scrape this channel. What are some good filters I should put so it maintains my Obsidian knowledge base and keeps it pristine? That would be elite level right there." [06-17 L304] Restated: "Create the filtering based on what's important in my business, and in me achieving my goals, and that will be relevant to the Obsidian knowledge base, and improve the knowledge base, and it won't deter from it." [06-17 L313] "Let AI tell you what the filters should be, because then you know it's going to give you these killer filters that you've never even thought of." [06-17 L304] A member's wording the founder praised: content must "not contaminate that which is uniquely mine." [06-17 L333]

In the Build Room, the goals the filters come from already live in the Business File: what the member sells and to whom (§1, §2), the offer (§3), the positioning (§4), the current constraint (§12), and the Build Plan (§13). The interests profile is pre-filled from them.

## 5. Protect what is yours

The master prompt's non-negotiable: ingested content stays "protected from my proprietary information." [06-17 L425] The generator hard-codes this as the four pillars in `vault-protection.md`: external content writes only to its own folder, the watcher never reads existing notes, every note is tagged external and unprocessed, and nothing is promoted into the member's own notes except by hand. A member who bulk-dumped a whole drive into the vault "was regretting it. I put some guardrails in." [06-23 L517]

## 6. Model, tokens, schedule

"If there's heavy-duty thinking involved and it's important, use Opus. When it's a task that's just transactional, then use a low model." [06-17 L734] "This shouldn't be a process that requires a heavy model, because it's very transactional." [06-17 L731] "Just run this on Sonnet, keep, like, watch my tokens." [06-17 L745] A member's rule: a new thread per source, because long threads re-read their history and usage compounds.

Weekly for channels: "Every week, I want you to watch all of their videos, and you're looking for stuff related to Claude Code, and just add it automatically." [06-11 L872] Nightly for the founder's own Zoom sweep: "Every night at midnight, it has a recurring task to go into my Zoom. Claude Code's gonna grab it, it's gonna put it into Obsidian, it's gonna make any necessary connections." [08-06 L1318–1320] Scheduling is spoken, not clicked: "You just have to say the words, run this three times a day at nine." [05-20 L786] "The real key is automating everything and make it recurring tasks, so you don't have to think about it anymore." [08-06 L1317]

## 7. Keys and costs, as stated

The YouTube skills need no key: captions come through `yt-dlp`. "Not with those tools, you don't." [06-23 L514] The podcast watcher pays for transcripts when a show publishes none: a member hit "I have to open this thing, podcast watcher .env, and paste my OpenAI API," and the founder: "It's probably using Whisper, I bet." [06-17 L854–856] The podcast watcher's README puts a twenty-podcast setup at roughly three to five dollars a month in API cost plus Whisper at $0.006 a minute when needed. The X API is the expensive source, about $100 a month; `source-types.md` says which sources are free.

The `.env` recipe, again: "Please create a .env template that I can easily edit and put my API keys in. The platforms I use that I have an API key to are…" [06-17 L871] Rename `.txt` to `.env` in a text editor; `.env` files are hidden by default. [06-17 L886–893]

## 8. Using the output

"I suck that information in once a week into my Obsidian from the companies that have created these tools, so now it's in my brain. Now I tell it, based on the best practices of using Codex, create XYZ, and now it's using the best practices of the tools from the people that created the tools." [08-06 L1299–1300] "You can level up your skill set without even being skillful." [08-06 L1302]

Follow-ons taught the same month: turn a repeated workflow into a slash command [06-24 L125]; "give your brain expert hats" [06-24 L126]; for a client with a hundred podcast episodes, "once the 100 episodes are processed, build a coaching playbook organized by client scenario." [07-09 L547] In the founder's vault a recurring channel watch feeds a set of distilled topic notes, each ending in a "gaps to fill on future runs" section, and every run is logged *(inference from the vault's import log)*.

## 9. Don'ts and failures seen

- Don't install a stranger's skill. "Don't do this with unknown people. If it is from somebody who's a bad person, it can mess you up bad." "If I like a skill, I'll bring the skill in and say, hey, rebuild this for me, instead of just installing the skill." [08-05 L601–606]
- Don't ingest everything. See section 4.
- Don't take the tool at face value: "You don't have to take this at face value. Customize these any way you want." [06-10 L715]
- When Claude stalls, push: "Anytime it asks you to do something and you don't feel like doing it, just say, you do it." [06-17 L711] "It was like, I don't have API access to that. I was like, yeah, you do. Gave it to you, like, twice." [06-17 L716–717]
- Failures on the calls: a repo shared while still private ("404 message in GitHub" [06-17 L174]); a missing `.env` key; `yt-dlp` not installed and no channel URL given [06-11 L1083]; a whole channel "is gonna take a while" [06-11 L1082]; Codex users had to say "adapt this to Codex" [07-30 L1471].
- Failures in the founder's own runs *(inference from the vault's import log)*: YouTube rate limits capping a run at a handful of pulls; a browser-cookie prompt hanging the run; an auto-translated caption track decoding into nonsense; duplicates when matched by title instead of video ID. Match on the video ID.

## 10. Open questions

- How the podcast watcher locates episodes is RSS per its README; Whisper is the paid fallback. Windows support for `yt-dlp` and `ffmpeg` was raised and not answered.
- Where podcast notes land: the generator's `/External` folder by design; the YouTube skills default to `sources/videos`. Pick one and say so in the setup guide.
- How recurring runs are scheduled is "tell it": cron, launchd, or a scheduled-task tool. Confirm the mechanism used and where it logs.
- The shipped `channel-to-vault` filters by title keywords; the taught rule is filters from goals. The generator does the latter.
